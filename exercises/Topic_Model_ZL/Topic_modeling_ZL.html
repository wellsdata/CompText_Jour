Topic Modeling all 700 articles

Process into corpus object

textdata <- sample700 %>% 
  select(doc_id, text, year, period, focal) %>% 
  as.data.frame()

Remove stopwords, clean corpus

# 1. Standard stopwords plus common news words
english_stopwords <- c(stopwords("en"), 
                       "said", "says", "also", "can", "will", "one", "new", "like", "just")

# 2. Build corpus
corpus <- Corpus(DataframeSource(textdata))

# 3. Clean in correct sequence
processedCorpus <- tm_map(corpus, content_transformer(tolower))
processedCorpus <- tm_map(processedCorpus, removePunctuation, preserve_intra_word_dashes = TRUE)
processedCorpus <- tm_map(processedCorpus, removeNumbers)


# Remove stopwords BEFORE stemming
processedCorpus <- tm_map(processedCorpus, removeWords, english_stopwords)

# Stem documents after removing stopwords
processedCorpus <- tm_map(processedCorpus, stemDocument, language = "en")
processedCorpus <- tm_map(processedCorpus, stripWhitespace)
#DTM: rows correspond to the documents in the corpus. Columns correspond to the terms in the documents. Cells correspond to the weights of the terms. (Girder)
# compute document term matrix with terms >= minimumFrequency
minimumFrequency <- 5
DTM <- DocumentTermMatrix(processedCorpus, control = list(bounds = list(global = c(minimumFrequency, Inf))))

# have a look at the number of documents and terms in the matrix
dim(DTM)
## [1]  700 5285
# due to vocabulary pruning, we have empty rows in our DTM
# LDA does not like this. So we remove those docs from the
# DTM and the metadata
sel_idx <- slam::row_sums(DTM) > 0
DTM <- DTM[sel_idx, ]
textdata <- textdata[sel_idx, ]
dim(DTM)
## [1]  699 5285

The function below lists the 20 articles that load most on a topic, with headline, outlet, and whether the article is relevant (focal = TRUE).

top_articles_for <- function(topic_n) {
  as.data.frame(theta) %>% 
    rownames_to_column(var = "doc_id") %>% 
    rename(topic = all_of(as.character(topic_n))) %>% 
    top_n(20, topic) %>% 
    arrange(desc(topic)) %>% 
    left_join(sample700 %>% select(doc_id, focal, headline, outlet), by = "doc_id") %>% 
    select(doc_id, focal, headline, outlet, topic)
}

Summary

I ran an LDA topic model (K = 6) on the 699 articles in my Pilot sample, drawn from a broad AI-and-health search in Nexis Uni. Only 40 of them are relevant to my project after screening: lay people’s use of GenAI chatbots for health is the central subject. I compared each topic’s average share in the relevant and irrelevant articles.

One topic is clearly relevant. Chatbot use and risks makes up 42% of the relevant articles but 17% of the others. Its top articles cover ChatGPT and teen mental health, a California law on companion chatbots, and lawsuits against OpenAI, the same harm stories I found when reading the full articles. Medical AI is partly relevant (22% vs. 15%), but it covers clinical tools for doctors, not lay users. Four topics are irrelevant: business and markets, tech products, politics, and lifestyle and entertainment (horoscopes, recipes, reviews), most of which never mention generative AI.

The benefit is a quick map of a large sample. The model shows where the noise in my search comes from, which will help me revise the search string before collecting the full corpus. The shortfalls are clear. The chatbot topic mixes health stories with campus stories about AI and cheating as an example, so it does not show the rise in relevant coverage from 2023 to 2026 that I found by reading. Some relevant articles fall into unrelated topics. Topics are word patterns, not frames. They cannot show the roles, attribution, or advice my research questions ask about, so I still need the codebook.
