Plans should be held 
Re: "Neighbors coming together -- Volunteers ensure everyone in community has voice in 765kV power line route," Monday news story. 
The need for any 765-kV powerlines and ERCOT's entire more than $13 billion Permian Basin Reliability Plan is based on questionable, and out-of-date, electricity demand projections. The plan for new 765-kV line construction should be immediately scrapped, and reliable electrical demand projections determined. 
The Dallas Morning News coverage on ERCOT's proposed 765-kV lines briefly touches on the alleged need for this unnecessary infrastructure. According to the story, the power lines are needed partly to "electrify the oil rich Permian Basin." 
However, ERCOT's own study indicates that fanciful green hydrogen and crypto currency electrical loads make up 40% of the estimated electrical demand growth that the Permian Basin Reliability Plan is based on. The sketchy electrical demand data attempting to justify these unproven power lines must be reviewed. 
Plans for any 765-kV lines based on ERCOT's Permian Basin Reliability Plan should be placed on hold immediately. 
Patrick Foley, Plano

Presidential speech 
Re: "Presidential words," by Kenneth Zagacki and Richard Cherwitz, Thursday Letters. 
Thank you for publishing Cherwitz and Zagacki's letter regarding President Donald Trump's bombastic threats to Iran, issued via social media on Easter 2026. Their excellent analysis distinguishes the speech of a political candidate or television personality from that of a president, which represents the position and the intentions of an entire nation 
Whether appreciated among his supporters or not, Trump's words are taken throughout the world in the context of the powers and traditions of his office. The two letter writers remind us that presidential speech is an action and does not vanish with the withdrawal of a social media post. 
Kathy Rechnitzer Kelly, West Fort Worth 
Consistency matters 
Re: "Fair assessment," by Don Skaggs, July 4, 2024, letter. 
Skaggs wrote on July 4, 2024, that President Joe Biden was "clearly declining physically, mentally and morally," that his profanity-laced speech disqualified him from leadership, and that our president lacked "the will or the ability to unite us." Fair enough -- those are legitimate standards to hold any president to. 
But Skaggs also celebrated November's election results in a letter on Dec. 24, 2025, praising voters who "voted their values." 
One has to ask: which values? Because the president now in office has delivered his own stream of profanity, public threats against political opponents and behavior that even some Republicans have publicly questioned -- on all three of Skaggs' own measures. 
Consistency isn't a partisan virtue -- it's a basic one. If physical, mental and moral fitness mattered enough to write about in July 2024, they should matter now. The standard shouldn't change based on whose name is on the door of the Oval Office. 
Alfonso Cevola, Dallas 
Focus on Iran 
Re: "What did he just say," Sunday letters. 
The Sunday letters section has just sunk to a new low. We have about 12 letters devoted to the fact that President Donald Trump used profane language in a social media post expressing his frustration with Iran. 
Iran has executed thousands of its own people in recent years if they dare to show any dissent to its tyrannical rule. Iranian leaders have threatened the world with their stockpile of enriched uranium that can be used to make a nuclear bomb. 
Apparently, none of this behavior compares with Trump using profanity on Easter Sunday. 
Kay Wrobel, Plano
