Highlight: While I got ya, here are nine things and one crazy prediction: 1. Credit where it's due Steve Sarkisian rightfully went on his soapbox last year and chastised the College Football Playoff selection committee for omitting his Texas team from the postseason tournament despite three wins over top-10 teams. And he did it again Monday. Except this time, it was Ohio State he was promoting as a contender, even after the Buckeyes' loss to the Longhorns. "You make the playoffs based off the opinions of others, essentially," he said. "And so that's the risk-reward. I hope Ohio State does not get punished for that game Saturday night. They lost with us scoring with 25 seconds to go in the game. I hope if they end up 9-3, the committee can appreciate the fact that they came to Austin in September, when it was 100 degrees, and played us in that environment. Quite frankly, Ohio State should be rewarded for playing in this game against us. And this isn't crying over spilled milk. But they may go 11-1." Amen. ... Actually, there is some spilled milk, but he's right. Every committee member should give more credit to the Buckeyes than to Indiana, Georgia or Texas Tech, which refused to play stiff nonconference competition. The Hoosiers played Howard or Bill or Tom. ... Speaking of national issues, Sen. Ted Cruz (R-Texas), went on "College GameDay" on Saturday to push for passage of the Protect College Sports Act, the bipartisan Senate bill he cosponsored with Washington Sen. Maria Cantwell, D-Wash. Cruz was loudly booed by the crowd, which was made up mostly of UT students. Incidentally, Bo French, the Republican candidate for the Railroad Commission, later shamefully lambasted a diverse section of student fans pictured celebrating because there weren't near enough whites. Or, as he put it, the picture showed that Texas "institutions have been overrun by illegal aliens and foreign nationals." I'm sure he was hoping ICE agents would show up and cart them off so that, in the future, he could see DKR's stands populated only by white, red-meat-eating Texans, resembling those crowds in the '50s. Whites make up less than 32% of the student body, in contrast with Blacks, Asians and Hispanics, who represent up to 59%, and international students, who make up just 4%, according to U.S. News. At least Regents Chairman Kevin Eltife addressed French's racist remarks, saying in a statement: "When I stood on the sidelines at DKR-Texas Memorial Stadium Saturday night and looked up at the seats filled with incredible fans, I saw Longhorns. It's the same remarkable view I have at graduation ceremonies and anytime I'm on campus. I'm proud to celebrate all our Longhorn students today and every day. Thank you for making us proud." ... I do think the Cruz-Cantwell bill would do a lot of good, such as getting rid of the maddening patchwork of state laws, banning college athletes from playing seven or more years and replacing that with a one-time transfer rule, limiting sports agents' commissions to 5% and safeguarding women's and Olympic sports. Those are admirable goals. But the good senator also said the current chaotic system is "terrible for the players." I have yet to hear a single player reject the big NIL checks he's cashing. I haven't heard a single one say he hates having the option to transfer to other schools to better himself and his family financially. Of course, none of these people in charge really care what the athletes think, or they would have invited more--or some--of them to the table to negotiate many of these issues. 2. Manning arrives OK, maybe he wished he hadn't arrived at Monday's press conference because of the insensitive comments he made about an AI-generated fake picture featuring a Sarkisian lookalike slapping Holly Rowe. Nothing about that was funny or decent, and Manning appropriately apologized for being, well, inappropriate. ... It wasn't a flawless performance on the field, either, thanks in large part to five crucial drops by his receivers, which would fluster any quarterback. He even said Monday in his press conference that he felt the effects and "hit a lull" over the next five drives. "I thought I could have responded better." Manning is nothing if not completely accountable. ... But Saturday night was his signature moment, the breakout game that stamped him as a tough-minded leader who never sulks and never stops believing. This was the game even his harshest critics should give him credit for. Was he perfect? Not at all. He still needs more touch on shorter balls. He may never be the most accurate passer because, as Mike Leach always told me, you can't coach accuracy. Ohio State's Julian Sayin is incredibly accurate, but he was 4-for-15 in the second half. ... Arch knew it was going to be a good night when he made it out of the tunnel during the runout for the game. No face-plant amid the eyes-clogging smoke this time, as there was before the Texas State game. He said, "I walked really slowly, and I had my hand on Trevor Goosby." Perfect protection by his left tackle. ... Arch was at his humorous best. He said his uncle Eli texted him, complimenting his patience and his not forcing any passes. When I asked him whether it was harder on him than on his famous uncles because they barely played during the social-media era, Arch objected: "That New York media, that's the real deal." ... Don't question Manning's heart. He's got grit, Sarkisian says. He actually plays better when he's behind. And he gets less help than most because his receivers still drop very catchable passes. Hello, Ryan Wingo. The junior wide receiver has to drastically improve his concentration if he wants a pro career. Texas still needs to throw to Cam Coleman more, and the Auburn transfer also has to improve his hands. ... On Buckeye receiver Jeremiah Smith, Manning said, "He's the real deal." I could see Smith and Miami's Malachi Toney in New York for the Heisman ceremony. ... As for all the blowback on Ryan Day, I wouldn't have let Texas score from the 1-yard line if it meant leaving between 60 and 90 seconds on the clock for Ohio State's offense to have a chance with more time. Have you not paid attention? Ohio State stuffed the Longhorns at the goal line in the Cotton Bowl and in the Horseshoe. Plus, if they had stopped Hollywood Smothers on his leap into the end zone, who knows what Sarkisian would have called after three inefficient runs up the middle? ... I really thought the Buckeyes were gassed in the fourth quarter in the 95-degree heat, especially with the Longhorns stuffing the run. At the suggestion of head football trainer Donald Nguyen, Texas smartly held its pregame warmups in its new indoor facility to save its legs. Every little bit helps.

3. Ranking the rally 
The top two for me, in order, will always be the 12-point rally to beat USC in the 2006 Rose Bowl and the comeback from a 14-0 hole at the end of three quarters against Arkansas to crush the Razorbacks' spirit forever in the Big Shootout in 1969. I really believe the improbable comeback to stun Ohio State ranks third because the Buckeyes looked dominant for three quarters, were ranked No. 1 and because the Longhorns earned -- weren't gifted by cheap turnovers, weren't lucky -- but earned the win by asserting their will with three consecutive scoring drives. Texas took that game. ... Sayin is an obvious first-round NFL quarterback, and Smith is better than Toney because he's bigger and more physical than the Miami receiver. 
4. All things Horns 
Besides the reliable Emmett Mosley V, who needs more targets for his sure hands and tight routes, three other Longhorns got tremendous love for their games. Sarkisian singled out defensive tackle Ian Geffrard, tight end Emaree Winston and left guard Dylan Sikorski. "I always knew Emaree was a gamer," Manning said. "He's a stud. He has some of the best hands on the team. I think he'll continue to do that going on this season." Winston will be called upon a lot, moreso with the knee injury to Nick Townsend. He's got big 10-inch hands. ... Sarkisian said he had planned to rotate Sikorski and Laurence Seymore III, but Sikorski was playing so well, "we just kind of let it go." This was the best performance by Texas' offensive line since the 2023 win in Tuscaloosa. ... Manning applauded the maturity of senior running back Smothers. "He's like a veteran. It feels like he's a 10-year All-Pro guy." ... Congratulate, Texas, on only two penalties for 12 yards. Of course, that officiating crew wasn't going to throw a flag for anything short of a homicide. Still, it was the cleanest game for the Longhorns since they were called for just two penalties for 10 yards in the CFP win at home over Clemson in 2024. ... The Longhorns rank tied for last with Kentucky in third down defense. Yikes. ... In baseball, Texas freshman pitcher Sam Cozart had right foot surgery this summer but is already playing catch and will be good to go in January. Second baseman Ethan Mendoza had shoulder surgery and is also sitting out fall drills. ... Saturday's game on ABC drew an audience of 14 million, making it the most-watched regular-season game in 10 years. 
5. McMonster up front 
I didn't know Bob McKay, who died last week at age 78, well. But I do know he was one of the best offensive linemen in UT history on a level with Jerry Sisemore, Dan Neil, Justin Blalock and Kelvin Banks Jr. And one of the biggest in his era. At 6-5, 236 pounds as a freshman, he was a behemoth and I mean that with the sincerest part of flattery. James Street dubbed him "Big Un" for a reason. ... Student manager Bill Hall said he probably texted McKay every day and remembers him as "one of the nicest human beings. If you were his friend, you were a friend for life." ... Running back teammate Ted Koy remembered McKay as the ultimate competitor on those wishbone days that included a 30-game win streak and two national titles. "If you just followed him," Koy said, "you were going in the right direction." ... McKay came from far, far, far West Texas at Crane, which played in Class AA and which the late Bill Little once wrote had a population of 37 people and 4,700 cattle. Darrell Royal showed up to recruit him, and McKay said, "It was like a parade. Everybody in town wanted to see him." ... Remember, he played on an offensive line that would be considered minuscule these days with teammates on the front like 200-pound Mike Dean. Texas' smallest offensive lineman now is right guard Brandon Baker at a very, uh, light 315 pounds. McKay was coming off shoulder surgery in May 1968. It was the next year that produced Texas' second national championship and was best remembered for the Game of the Century against Arkansas when Street connected with tight end Randy Peschel in one of the biggest pass receptions in school history. The fourth-down throw, which was called 53 Veer Pass, took the Longhorns to the Arkansas 13 and set up the game-winning touchdown and extra point. McKay was the first to reach Peschel downfield and lifted him off the ground, saying, "Nice catch, Pasquale." ... Of course, Texas sealed the national title with a 21-17 win over Notre Dame in the Cotton Bowl on Billy Dale's 1-yard run. 
6. Things I think I know 
I think the Cowboys redefined terrible until the Broncos re-redefined it Monday night. Their defenses can't stop anybody. Do NFL teams even practice tackling any more? ... I think I dig this NFL. Six games with a combined 60 points in Week 1. Sign me up. ... I think Josh Allen is my favorite quarterback. He deserves a Super Bowl. ... I think Bo Mascoe showed me a lot when the cornerback transfer from Rutgers shadowed Smith and held him without a catch in the fourth quarter on three targets. ... I think I know my fantasy football team is doomed now that I traded A.J. Brown after he was put on IR with a high ankle sprain. I know no one in the world cares about someone else's fantasy team but consider this venting and it's cheaper than a psychiatrist. ... I think I loved that the Rams lost Game 1 to the 49ers and destroys the nonsense LA might go undefeated. Kyle Shanahan is such a good coach. ... I think Mike Evans is such a stud. And a true Hall of Famer. He does not age. 
7. Save Muny, please 
Had a super time at Imagine Muny 5 at ACL last Thursday night. Good folks are doing everything in their power to preserve one of the most historic municipal golf courses in the country. Scotty Sayers, the longtime manager and friend of Ben Crenshaw, told the audience of about 2,200 that the Muny Conservancy committee is "optimistic" that the University of Texas will agree to keep open the 18-hold course in the heart of downtown that was one of the first golf courses in the South to desegregate and was a breeding ground for Crenshaw, Tom Kite, Mark Brooks and other greats instead of developing the land for commercial purposes. The fifth annual event raised $1 million, auctioning off things like Scottie Scheffler-signed Masters flags, Rory McIlroy-autographed wine bottles and rounds of golf at Pinehurst No. 2 before Ray Benson, Tanya Tucker and Jack Ingram sang. ... Sat with my wife Vicki at a table with Barbara Pruett, a former Texas State Champion, six-time Austin City Golf Champion and legendary instructor at the Barton Creek Golf Academy, and her daughter Paulette. Barbara told me she still plays nine holes every Sunday at Austin Country Club with her 93-year-old dad Roane, a Southwest Conference champion at Texas. Barbara is a Harvey Penick disciple who has taught four generations of Austin golfers. Oh, Paulette told me her five-time city champion father owns a career 22 holes-in-one. Crenshaw said he had his very first hole-in-one at age 10 at Muny and then had another two days later. Paulette's waiting on her first.
