Last month, we warned that Mark Cuban’s ominous prediction that artificial intelligence would be used to manipulate elections would come true. Well, it has.
In the latest example, tech billionaire Elon Musk shared a post on X that parodied a campaign ad for presumptive Democratic presidential nominee Kamala Harris. The AI-generated voice in the clip mimics Harris’ voice and earned 134 million views as of Friday, according to the site.
This video is so ridiculous that many who saw it probably assumed it was a joke — although one in bad taste. But it’s an alarming preview of election cycles today and to come. Some laws are already on the books, like Texas’ 2019 law criminalizing deepfake videos used to interfere in elections. Still, we need stronger guardrails that make social media platforms civilly liable for what they publish.
The fake ad showcases Harris saying things like, “I, Kamala Harris, am your Democrat candidate for president because Joe Biden finally exposed his senility at the debate.”
The AI-generated mimic of Harris’ voice says she is the “ultimate diversity hire” as a woman and a person of color, and that she is a totally incompetent “deep state puppet.” The voice talks about an important alliance with the Republic of North Korea.
The original post is labeled as a parody, though Musk did not make that immediately clear when he shared it. There’s no question, though, that the impression is believable enough and will only get more believable as the technology advances.
The account that made the original post has since released a second one — also labeled as a parody.
There have been more nefarious examples. Earlier this year, an AI-generated impression of President Joe Biden’s voice reached more than 20,000 New Hampshire residents through phone calls, encouraging them not to vote in the primary election.
Musk has long promised to make X, called Twitter when he purchased it for $44 billion, a haven for free speech, which is the “bedrock of a functioning democracy,” he said in a statement. He’s right about that part. But X hasn’t exactly strengthened that bedrock.
Shortly after a Monday night fundraiser for the political activism group White Dudes for Harris, the group’s X account was suspended, according to The Washington Post. We don’t see any good reason for having done it. Maybe it happened because of a software quirk or online trolls, or maybe to interfere with the group’s platform. The account is now active again.
Whatever happened, suspending political activists’ X accounts doesn’t exactly ring of free speech.
How will our country manage what will be a proliferation of fake speech on social media? That’s hard to say, given the possibility that laws aimed at limiting it will trample on free speech.
We can start with taking a hard look at how social media is regulated generally and get more serious about treating these platforms as the publishers they are.
