


Hackers Are Using AI to Steal Corporate Secrets and Plant Ransomware

September 1, 2025 Monday 6:00 AM EST

Length: 659 words
Byline: Chris Morris
Body


Turns out hackers are vibe coding, too. And small businesses are paying the price. 
Artificial intelligence may be able to handle the mundane day-to-day tasks that vex workers and help provide breakthroughs in several fields, but the threats it presents are still very real. While there has been plenty of talk about the risks to the labor pool, fewer people have spotlighted how hackers have been learning to adapt the technology to their own benefit. And, increasingly, AI is making it easier for them to steal information that might have seemed inaccessible before.
In one of the most recent examples, a hacker discovered an exploit in Anthropic's Claude chatbot that allowed them to "commit large-scale theft and extortion of personal data" at 17 (and perhaps more) organizations in the health care, emergency services, government, and religion industries.
The hacker then threatened to expose that data, demanding ransoms that, in some cases, topped $500,000. (Anthropic did not name any of the 17 organizations that were impacted by the hack.)
"This represents an evolution in AI-assisted cybercrime," Anthropic said in a statement. "AI tools are now being used to provide both technical advice and active operational support for attacks that would otherwise have required a team of operators. ... We expect attacks like this to become more common as AI-assisted coding reduces the technical expertise required for cybercrime."
While that hacker opted not to use traditional ransomware on those companies that were impacted, researchers have learned that other hackers have learned to use local AI models to create advanced programs that lock away a compromised system's data until a ransom is paid.
Multinational cybersecurity company ESET announced last week that it had discovered "the first AI-powered ransomware," which uses generative AI to create malicious scripts and execute its attacks in real time. The malware uses AI to determine which files to search, copy, or encrypt.
The emergence of such tools "highlights a significant shift in the cyber threat landscape," said Anton Cherepanov, senior malware researcher at ESET, in a statement. "With the help of AI, launching sophisticated attacks has become dramatically easier-eliminating the need for teams of skilled developers. ... [It] could severely complicate detection and make the work of cybersecurity defenders considerably more challenging."
AI eliminating the need for teams of hackers is a bad sign for companies. With AI, hackers will be both nimbler and broader, which could significantly increase the threat. The aids the technology provides lets them hit a greater number of targets with less effort.
That's going to have a financial impact on companies across the board, even if they're not targeted by bad actors. Research firm Gartner estimates corporate spending on cybersecurity will rise from $193 billion in 2024 to $240 billion by 2026, a 24 percent increase. A sizable amount of that spend, it adds, will come from small and midsize businesses.
The onslaught of AI-fueled attacks is already beginning. On the most recent earnings call for cybersecurity firm Palo Alto Networks (Nasdaq:PANW), CEO Nikesh Arora said "data security incidents related to GenAI more than doubled since last year." (That, in part, is why the AI-powered cybersecurity company's operating profits in the last fiscal year grew by 82 percent from the year prior.)
All of this, of course, is on top of the other ways hackers can use AI, such as fooling employers, creating deepfakes, or using it as a research tool to gather information and launch phishing schemes.
AI might save companies billions in labor and other costs in the years to come, but it could be nearly as expensive in other ways. Despite increased spending on cybersecurity, fraud losses from generative AI are predicted to reach $40 billion in the U.S. by 2027, according to Deloitte. That's up from $12.3 billion in 2023.
