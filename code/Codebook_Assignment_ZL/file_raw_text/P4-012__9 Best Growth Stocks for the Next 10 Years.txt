The stock market in 2026 has given investors plenty of reasons to be cautious. 
Concerns about valuations in artificial intelligence stocks, interest rate changes and economic uncertainty brought on by the war in Iran have all created plenty of short-term volatility. 
[Sign up for stock news with our Invested newsletter.]
Investors with a long time horizon should remember that temporary market pressures are rarely the most important factor in picking stocks, however. Even after a downturn of a few weeks or even a few months, great businesses can still compound wealth over many years -- and more importantly, investors will avoid locking in losses by sticking with their holdings until the bear market ends. 
The most successful growth stocks tend to have durable competitive advantages, strong customer relationships and deep pockets to continually reinvest in operations. Buying and holding those companies through periods of uncertainty has historically been a powerful strategy, and the following nine stocks all offer hopes for a path to sustained growth well beyond 2026:

Alphabet Inc. (GOOG, GOOGL)
Iconic digital communications leader Alphabet combines enormous scale driven by Google Search, an entrenched ad network and a dominant YouTube video platform. Google Cloud as well as Gemini AI efforts provide additional avenues for expansion in the years ahead, too. The key long-term advantage is Alphabet's enormous global user base, which gives the company an unmatched platform for introducing new products and succeeding in the years to come. Shares have performed exceptionally well lately, up nearly 40% in the past 12 months, but the long-term opportunity extends well beyond these gains. For investors looking beyond short-term market trends, Alphabet's current scale coupled with roughly $240 billion in cash and investments give it a great shot at being one of the strongest growth stories of the next decade. 
Apple Inc. (AAPL)
Another example of a stock with a massive and loyal customer base, Apple has grown far beyond its origins as an iPhone manufacturer. Its enormous ecosystem now encompasses computers, wearables, cloud services, digital payments, subscriptions and other services. Customers who own multiple Apple products have strong incentives to remain within the platform, and a user-friendly approach makes it very difficult for users to experience enough friction to leave. The company's extraordinary scale makes its growth rates less dramatic than those of smaller technology companies, but with consistent double-digit expansion to sales, there is no doubt Apple is a massive firm that's getting even bigger with each day. Apple also offers an interesting alternative for investors concerned about the concentration of the market's growth story in artificial intelligence and semiconductor stocks. 
Cloudflare Inc. (NET)
A different kind of tech stock, Cloudflare is an important part of the infrastructure supporting the modern internet. It provides cybersecurity, networking and performance services provided to clients around the world. Cloudflare continues to benefit from a long-term transition to the cloud, and its broad platform serves multiple enterprise needs in a way that makes it indispensable to businesses in all sectors. A recurring revenue model is another important advantage, allowing the company to build on "sticky" customer relationships to ensure continued growth. Long-term potential for NET stock is attributed to continued risks from an evolving cybersecurity environment where the stakes only get higher, as technology is embedded in every part of business workflows. With key partners including Mastercard Inc. (MA), Shopify Inc. (SHOP) and IKEA, Cloudflare is a high-growth technology stock with long-term potential. 
Eli Lilly and Co. (LLY)
Founded in 1876, Eli Lilly is one of the most compelling growth stories in the pharmaceutical industry. The company remains on the cutting edge of healthcare, with leadership in diabetes and obesity treatments that have driven revenue expansion lately. Its Mounjaro and Zepbound brands stand as market leaders. This gives Lilly a powerful position in two enormous markets and creates a strong financial foundation for growth through future R&D and product launches in the years ahead. A robust product pipeline and exposure to areas such as oncology give the company additional potential sources of growth over the next decade, and the stock's extraordinary gains of roughly 390% over the past five years illustrate the long-term investment case in this Big Pharma icon. 
First Solar Inc. (FSLR)
Alternative energy policy isn't what it once was in the U.S., but First Solar stock has held up well over the long term, with a 10-year annualized return of 18.5%. That's because FSLR offers investors a long-term growth opportunity tied to the continuing expansion of electricity generation and domestic energy infrastructure, along with the megatrend of decarbonization. The Arizona-based solar power firm is the leading vertically-integrated U.S.-based manufacturer, which has made it an attractive alternative to customers who want to reduce exposure to overseas supply chain disruptions. And First Solar's substantial order backlog continues to show that the business case for solar power -- particularly in an era of oil at $100 a barrel -- continues to ring true regardless of policymakers who ignore the threats of climate change. FSLR is an intriguing growth stock for the coming decade despite near-term policy uncertainty. Revenue is predicted to climb between 15% and 20% in fiscal year 2027, proving this is a stock with staying power regardless of who is setting U.S. energy policy.

Gilead Sciences Inc. (GILD)
Gilead Sciences offers a combination of defensive characteristics and long-term growth potential. Healthcare demand tends to be less sensitive to economic cycles because patients still need essential medicines regardless of the broader economy. This diversification is important for a long-term investor because it reduces reliance on a single therapeutic category and gives the company multiple ways to expand its business. Gilead also returns capital to shareholders through a steadily growing dividend, adding an income component to the potential for future stock-price appreciation. With shares up more than 100% in the past five years versus about 65% for the S&P 500 and with a current yield of 2.2%, GILD offers a rare combination of stability and income alongside innovation and growth potential in the years ahead. 
MercadoLibre Inc. (MELI)
MercadoLibre is perhaps the weakest short-term performer on this list, but it also presents one of the most compelling long-term growth stories in emerging markets. The firm combines e-commerce, digital payments, logistics and financial services on a single platform. Often described as the Amazon of Latin America, the company has established dominant positions in important markets such as Brazil and Argentina, where e-commerce and digital financial services still have substantial room to expand. MercadoLibre's growing customer base and expanding logistics network can reinforce its competitive position as more consumers shift their purchases and financial activity online. Shares are down about 20% in the past 12 months, but the long-term revenue growth trends are significant -- including a prediction of 40% expansion in 2026 and another 30% in 2027. With nearly 90 million unique shoppers using its platform, MercadoLibre has the scale necessary to succeed in the long-term. If you're willing to look past recent near-term headwinds, MELI could offer explosive potential growth in the decade to come. 
Robinhood Markets Inc. (HOOD)
Robinhood has made investing accessible to a new generation of traders through a simple, mobile-focused platform. Its appeal among younger and more self-directed investors gives the company an important long-term opportunity -- particularly in newer areas such as short-term options and crypto assets. The scale of the platform is already significant, with nearly 29 million funded accounts and almost $360 billion in customer assets. The long-term investment case, therefore, depends less on any individual trading trend and more on Robinhood's ability to become a home for those interested in the markets. There might be a bit more uncertainty than established financial companies, particularly given the strong ties to general market trends, but investors seeking higher long-term growth may want to buy and hold this stock. 
Visa Inc. (V)
Visa offers long-term growth because of a powerful structural shift away from cash and checks toward electronic payments across the global economy. Visa operates one of the world's largest and most trusted payment networks and benefits from enormous transaction volume without taking on the same lending risk as traditional banks. That asset-light model helps support high profitability while allowing the company to participate in the growth of digital commerce around the world. Visa's enormous network is also a major advantage and a wide moat that makes it difficult for other payment processors to compete with. Growth in emerging markets, mobile payments and online commerce provides additional room for expansion over the next decade. While only up about 7.5% in the past 12 months, Visa shares have gained more than 400% over the past decade, showing it continues to have big potential for the next 10 years.
