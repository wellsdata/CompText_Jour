


Stamford-based crypto conglomerate DCG sued by NY state for alleged fraud

Byline: Paul Schott, Staff writer
Body


STAMFORD — Embattled cryptocurrency conglomerate Digital Currency Group is facing further pressure after it was sued this week by New York state for allegedly defrauding more than 230,000 investors, including at least 29,000 New Yorkers, of more than $1 billion. 
New York Attorney General Letitia James filed the lawsuit Thursday against Stamford-based DCG; one of its subsidiaries, Manhattan-based Genesis; as well as another crypto firm, Manhattan-based Gemini, which was founded by Greenwich natives, Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss. 
Among the litigation’s allegations, it said that an investigation by the New York Office of the Attorney General found that Gemini lied to investors about the Gemini Earn investment program that it ran with Genesis, with Gemini repeatedly assuring investors that investing with Genesis through the program was a low-risk undertaking. But the investigation found that Gemini’s internal analyses of Genesis showed that the company’s financials were risky. The lawsuit also alleges that Gemini officials knew Genesis’ loans were undersecured — and at one point concentrated with Alameda, a trading firm founded by fallen crypto mogul Sam Bankman-Fried who is now on trial — but did not reveal that information to investors. 
The lawsuit also charges DCG; its founder and CEO, Barry Silbert; Genesis; and former Genesis CEO Soichiro Moro with defrauding investors and the public by trying to conceal more than $1.1 billion in losses. Consequently, thousands of investors lost millions of dollars and, in some cases their life savings, according to the New York attorney general's office. 
“These cryptocurrency companies lied to investors and tried to hide more than a billion dollars in losses, and it was middle-class investors who suffered as a result,” James said in a written statement. “Hardworking New Yorkers and investors around the country lost more than a billion dollars because they were fed blatant lies that their money would be safe and grow if they invested it in Gemini Earn. Instead, Gemini hid the risks of investing with Genesis and Genesis lied to the public about its losses. This fraud is yet another example of bad actors causing harm throughout the under-regulated cryptocurrency industry. My office will continue our efforts to stop deceptive cryptocurrency companies and push for stronger regulations to protect all investors.”
Through the lawsuit, James is seeking to ban Gemini, Genesis, and DCG from the financial-investment industry in New York and seeking restitution for investors and disgorgement of “ill-gotten gains.” 
A message left Thursday for a DCG spokesperson was not immediately returned. 
Gemini said in a statement that New York’s lawsuit validated the complaint that Gemini filed in the summer against DCG and Silbert for alleged fraud related to Gemini Earn. The program allowed investors to loan their crypto assets to Genesis, in exchange for Genesis’ promise to pay interest. When Genesis froze withdrawals following the collapse late last year of Bankman-Fried’s FTX crypto exchange, it acutely affected Earn users. Gemini alleged in its lawsuit that 232,000 users had a total of more than $1.2 billion “trapped” in Genesis. 
“The NY AG’s lawsuit confirms what we’ve been saying all along — that Gemini, Earn users, and other creditors were the victims of a massive fraud and systematically ‘lied to’ by these parties about ‘Genesis’s financial condition,’” Gemini said in its statement. “With that said, we wholly disagree with the NY AG’s decision to also sue Gemini. Blaming a victim for being defrauded and lied to makes no sense, and we look forward to defending ourselves against this inconsistent position.”
DCG has denied Gemini’s allegations. 
Gemini and Genesis’ dealings have also faced scrutiny from federal regulators. In January, the Securities and Exchange Commission charged Genesis and Gemini for what it said was the unregistered offer and sale of securities through Earn. 
Amid its legal issues, DCG has made a number of job cuts in the past year. Genesis, which filed for bankruptcy in January, laid off 30 percent of its staff at the beginning of the year, according to The Wall Street Journal. 
Around the time that Genesis filed for bankruptcy, DCG closed its wealth-management business, HQ. At the time of its shutdown, HQ employed about 30 people, most of whom were based in Stamford. In late May, DCG shut down its TradeBlock subsidiary. The company has not publicly commented on how many, if any, Stamford-based employees were affected by TradeBlock’s closure. 
Last November, following earlier layoffs, DCG said it had 58 Stamford-based employees, not including subsidiaries' staff. Since then, the company has not provided an updated Stamford head count. 
In November 2021, DCG announced its arrival in Connecticut with a press conference that was attended by a number of local and state officials. If it creates and retains more than 300 full-time jobs, DCG would earn from the state Department of Economic and Community Development a grant of up to approximately $5 million. If it creates a smaller number of jobs, it would receive a smaller amount of grant funding. 
No funding has been disbursed so far to DCG because it has not yet completed the signing of its contract with DECD, according to state officials.
