“I feel like that’s what Steve Lacy gives,” the “Gilded Age” actor says. She’s a fan of his new album, as well as Zora Neale Hurston and nude beaches.
Denée Benton is so here for her character’s arc in the new season of “The Gilded Age,” HBO’s lavish drama.
“She’s getting to have it all,” Benton, 34, said in a call from the Queens set after a day of shooting for the fourth season, set to premiere Nov. 1. “But she’s also feeling the stresses of trying to balance it all. It’s cool to see her dealing with modern struggles, like the pressure to have a kid as her career is taking off.”
Benton plays Peggy, an ambitious journalist from an upper-class Black family in 19th-century Brooklyn. At the end of Season 3, she had just accepted a marriage proposal from Dr. William Kirkland, played by Jordan Donica.
Benton is also back on Broadway this fall in “School Girls; or, The African Mean Girls Play.” She plays Paulina, the “queen bee” of a group of teenage girls preparing for the arrival of a Miss Ghana pageant recruiter.
“I see in Paulina what I also see in Peggy, what I also see in myself, which is this ferocious commitment to our desires and a true rage at things that don’t feel fair,” she said. The show begins performances Sept. 8. “I’m excited to really step into my villain arc,” she added.
In an interview in August, she shared 10 of her cultural essentials, including Steve Lacy’s latest album and the novels of Zora Neale Hurston. These are edited excerpts from the conversation.
Japanese Breakfast in Greenpoint
Starting the morning at Dashi Okume is my favorite day-off ritual. They have this incredible dashi broth, and I love their miso-glazed mackerel. And then I usually buy some Japanese kitchen foods, little fix-ins like sesame or nori flakes. They also have these amazing Japanese knives that are like a gajillion dollars. I never even cook, but I like to look at them and pretend, aspirationally.
Lauren Halsey
She makes these incredible Egyptian Sphinx-style sculptures but then mixes them with modern street art, and so it ends up feeling like all of Black art history in one sculpture.
My Houseplants
I have seven. One of them was probably two feet tall when I got it, and now she’s a six-foot tropical tree. Her name is Peach. She’s moved with me to all my different apartments — the poor moving companies that have to carry this tree that I can’t part with!
Steve Lacy’s ‘Oh Yeah?’
In my next life, I would love to be a detached, chaotic Gemini, and I feel like that’s what Steve Lacy gives. This album feels like my own internal toxic lover boy. I love to moodily walk around to these songs. He’s a great poet, and I find his lyrics to be really playful and also poignant, but they’re silly. There are also some good heartbreaking songs on it. “Nothing” is one of my favorites.
Diptyque Bath Oil
When I’m onstage, I try to treat my body like an athlete. Epsom salt baths just don’t sound as sexy as adding a decadent body oil. I have to take baths for my joints, and so I need to add things that make me feel rich and sensual instead of old and tired.
Alfonso Cuarón
As a child, I was obsessed with the film “A Little Princess.” I loved the magical realism elements of it. It would always make me cry. Then as I aged into a grown and sexy taste, I saw “Y Tu Mamá También” for the first time when I was studying abroad in Spain, and it was incredible. Cuarón’s work gives me the whole range of what I need in a film canon.
My Nordic Knots Rug
I really like the way this particular rug feels under my feet. It’s sort of Moroccan-inspired, and so it’s kind of soft, but it also feels supportive.
Zora Neale Hurston
I grew up going to church in Eatonville, Fla., which is where Zora was from. My church was right next to the historical landmark that’s her house. As I’ve gotten to know more about her, I’ve become obsessed with the fact that she’s a radical, queer, chaotic, vibrant artist who did not fit the times she was born in at all.
Nude Beaching
I did it for the first time when I was studying abroad in college in Spain. Our host mother was so scandalized that we had never taken our tops off at the beach. She was like, “You American girls are so oppressed! You need the sun!” I’ve been hooked ever since. Also, you need to eat a mango. It’s not for the public eye, though, by any means. You really need to eat mangos in privacy. I bite into them, and it’s very feral. There’s juice everywhere.
My Birkenstocks
I get made fun of on the “Gilded Age” set because I always try to get out of having to wear my heels. If my dress is long enough, I sneak my Birkenstocks into the takes. Hopefully they don’t make it on any Reddit boards!
