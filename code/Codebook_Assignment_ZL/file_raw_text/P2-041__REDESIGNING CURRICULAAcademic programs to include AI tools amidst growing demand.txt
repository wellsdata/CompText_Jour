There's no question that artificial intelligence is here to stay, and many educational institutions are making the most of it in classrooms and throughout education to prepare today's students for the future in their life paths. 
And using AI as an education tool in the classroom can greatly benefit students. 
The Harvard Graduate School of Education reported that teachers are implementing AI to enhance learning experiences in its article, Embracing Artificial Intelligence in the Classroom. These vary from using generative AI tools such as ChatGPT to answer questions, to learning what AI can't do, to using aspects of it to spark the imagination and alongside lessons. 
Dr. Hong Lin, director of University of Houston-Downtown's Master of Science in Artificial Intelligence program, and professor-computer science and engineering technology, said education programs are poised to give students an advantage in AI careers. Lin said that teachers have two options: whether to show students how to use AI in a responsible way, or expect them to abuse it. 
"To address these issues, coursework has to be redesigned to accommodate the use of AI tools in the learning process. 
"Education programs are poised to give students an advantage in AI careers by focusing on some of the skills that companies and firms are seeking including speech recognition, natural language processing, predictive machine learning, AI-assisted decision-making and more," Lin said. 
Various industries are fueling the demand for AI degrees, including health care, entertainment and retail, as reported by the U.S. News and World Report story, Best Undergraduate Artificial Intelligence Programs. 
Program choices abound 
Samir Saber, CISSP, M.Sc., Houston Community College, dean of Digital and Information Center of Excellence, and executive director, Workforce Technology, added there are two- and four-year degree options from HCC. 
These are the Artificial Intelligence Associate of Applied Science degree, and the Artificial Intelligence and Robotics Associate of Applied Technology degree. 
"Both are workforce programs, meaning the knowledge students learn are tied to learning outcomes that fit current jobs, current certifications, and reinforced by industry partners and professionals," Saber said. 
UHD also offers a Master of Science in Artificial Intelligence (MSAI), an application-oriented program that combines theoretical knowledge of AI as well as hands-on training in AI-specific applications through programming languages such as Python and open-source libraries such as TensorFlow. 
Students with strong programming and mathematics backgrounds are encouraged to apply. 
The UHD MSAI program was recognized by AI Degree Guide as a Top Pick of the Very Best Master's Programs in Artificial Intelligence. TechGuide recently ranked UHD's MSAI program No. 30 among the Best Master's in Artificial Intelligence Programs for 2024. 
According to Saber, the growing demand for a bachelor's degree, the lack of available degrees, and the massive impact of AI in this industry has led to huge interest from the community. 
"We have many students who have existing degrees that are looking to pivot into a new career. Many want to leverage their expertise in each domain," Saber said. 
These skills sets and education will continue to become more valuable, Lin said. 
"A large scope of industries including manufacturing, health care, financial, administrative, educational, and public service organizations, can benefit be deploying AI functionalities. The trend of using human-computer interaction tools in the future can only be more and more strong. 
"This will alter the business landscape, redefining the role of management, planning, production, and customer interactions," Lin said.
