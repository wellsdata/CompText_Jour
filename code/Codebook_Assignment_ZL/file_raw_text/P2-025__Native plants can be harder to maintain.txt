Q. Why do most nurseries only sell Tropical Milkweed when so many native milkweed selections exist? 
A. I am glad you did not say " when so many native milkweed selections are available"! Native milkweeds exist in many parts of the world but they are difficult to grow. Most of those in our area take four years to germinate and mature to the point where they produce generous blooms, nectar, and enough foliage for caterpillars. Tropical milkweed is apparently native to Central Mexico and germinates and produces nectar and substantial foliage in a short time. 
Q. Why do horticulturists including you, recommend Mexican sycamore rather than American sycamore? They both look equally good, and I have had good growing success with the American sycamore. 
A. Not everyone has good success with American sycamore in Central and South Texas. They grow fast only to become infected with fungus and require an expensive removal project. Mexican sycamore is attractive, grows fast, and is drought tolerant , and relative pest free. A good choice for our area. 
Q. We had trouble with fire ants on our okra last year. How should we deal with them to avoid problems this year? 
A. Sometimes it is enough just to spray them off with your water hose just before you harvest pods. There are also available fire ant insecticides labeled for the vegetable garden that have Spinosad as an active ingredient that are easy to use. Follow label instructions. 
Q. What are the best seeds with which to attract goldfinches to our landscape? 
A. I haven't had great luck attracting American goldfinches to my landscape in recent years, but if you are content with lesser goldfinches there are at least 3 options. They will come to a thistle feeder year round and also seem dedicated to harvesting seed from both sunflowers and coreopsis. Lesser goldfinches are more colorful in the wintertime here in Texas.
