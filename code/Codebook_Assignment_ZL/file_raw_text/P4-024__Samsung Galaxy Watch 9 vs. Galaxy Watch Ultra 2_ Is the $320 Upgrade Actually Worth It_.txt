Table of Contents  Samsung's 2026 Galaxy Watch lineup consists of two smartwatches: The Galaxy Watch 9 and the Galaxy Watch Ultra 2. The Galaxy Watch 9 offers minor hardware upgrades over the Galaxy Watch 8 and new preventative health features, including an AI-powered Heart Health Score and vitals monitoring. The Galaxy Watch Ultra 2 has a bigger battery, a brighter screen, and a thinner frame than its predecessor, while also offering better durability, particularly for water sports. I've tested both watches and am here to help you decide which one is right for you. It comes in 40mm and 44mm sizes. If you don't need a cellular plan, there's a Bluetooth-only Galaxy Watch 9 model. Like the original, the Galaxy Watch Ultra 2 comes in a single large 47mm size, with both Bluetooth and LTE baked in by default.  Left to right: Two Galaxy Watch Ultra 2 models, two sizes of the Galaxy Watch 9 (Credit: Eric Zeman)  The Galaxy Watch 9 starts at $379.99 for the Bluetooth-only 40mm model (a $30 price increase compared with the Galaxy Watch 8), while the 44mm model starts at $409.99. Add $50 to either size for the LTE-equipped variant.  The Galaxy Watch Ultra 2 costs $699.99, a $50 increase over the original Ultra. That works out to a $320 premium over the base Galaxy Watch 9, or an extra $240 over the most expensive variant (the 44mm Watch 9 with LTE, which costs $459.99).  The Galaxy Watch Ultra 2 comes in gray or silver. The 40mm Watch 9 is offered in graphite or cream, and the 44mm version is available in graphite or silver.  Winner: Galaxy Watch 9  Samsung Unveils Two New Galaxy Watches  Power: The Base Model Matches the Ultra  Both the Galaxy Watch 9 and the Galaxy Watch Ultra 2 have a new Qualcomm Snapdragon Wear Elite processor that offers better efficiency and increased CPU and GPU performance compared with the Exynos W1000 processor in the Galaxy Watch 8 and the original Ultra. The Ultra 2 has double the storage of the Watch 9, 64GB compared with 32GB, and both have 2GB of memory.  Both watches use Google's Wear OS 7 overlaid with Samsung's One UI 9, each one generation newer than what's currently available on older models, though the Galaxy Watch 8 and original Ultra could get the latest software via an update at some point.  While getting extra storage with the Ultra 2 is a nice bonus, this isn't the reason to spend the extra Benjamins. The Galaxy Watch 9 offers the same power for less.  Winner: Tie  Battery Life: The Main Reason to Pay More  The Ultra 2 has more battery capacity and lasts much longer than the Watch 9 between charges, and that's the main reason to spend more for the premium model if you're not into adventuring.  Specifically, the Ultra 2 has an 800mAh cell and an estimated battery life of 60 hours on a charge with the always-on display enabled. That's a sizable increase over the original Ultra, which has a 590mAh battery with an estimated 48 hours of power per charge. In testing, the Ultra 2 outlasted the company's estimate, running for an impressive 69.5 hours under normal use with the always-on display enabled, up from 46 hours for the original Ultra.  The Galaxy Watch 9 only lasts a day between charges (Credit: Joseph Maldonado)  Both Galaxy Watch 9 sizes have much smaller cells than the Ultra 2; the 40mm has 390mAh, while the 44mm has 445mAh. Both figures are larger than their respective Watch 8 counterparts (325mAh for the 40mm and 435mAh for the 44mm), but the Watch 9 has the same 30-hour battery estimate as its predecessor. Both the Watch 9 and the Watch 8 lasted for 26 hours on a charge in testing, slightly undercutting Samsung's estimate.  Battery has been a weak spot for Samsung's flagship smartwatches for years. Despite its more efficient chip and increased battery capacity compared with the last generation, the Galaxy Watch 9 doesn't offer improved battery life. On the other hand, the Ultra 2 offers a significant improvement over its predecessor, and lasts almost two full days more than the mainline Galaxy Watch 9.  Winner: Galaxy Watch Ultra 2  Screen: 5,000 Nits of Overkill?  The Galaxy Watch 9 screen matches the size and specs of its predecessor. The 44mm model has a 1.5-inch, 480-by-480-pixel Super AMOLED screen, while the 40mm version has a 1.3-inch, 432-by-432-pixel display. Regardless of size, the screen tops out at 3,000 nits of brightness, which is more than bright enough to see in direct sunlight.  The Galaxy Watch Ultra 2's screen can reach 5,000 nits of brightness (Credit: Eric Zeman)  The Ultra 2 has a 1.52-inch, 498-by-498-pixel Super AMOLED screen, which is larger and higher-res than the 44mm Watch 9, and the original Ultra (1.5 inches, 480 by 480 pixels). The screen reaches a blinding 5,000 nits, up from 3,000 nits in the original Ultra. While the figure is impressive, it doesn't significantly improve readability, since 3,000 nits is already more than sufficient for most lighting conditions. That said, I appreciate the bump when looking at the screen in direct sunlight or using the watch as a flashlight.  Winner: Galaxy Watch Ultra 2  Design and Durability: Only One Can Handle Scuba Descents  Both the Galaxy Watch 9 and the Galaxy Watch Ultra 2 have a squircle shape that the company first introduced with the original Galaxy Watch Ultra. The squared bezel and circular watch face sit above a dynamic lug system meant to keep the sensors in close contact with your skin as you move throughout the day. Samsung calls it a cushion design.  Both watches come with redesigned, lighter bands that feature air pockets for improved ventilation. Aside from the bands, the Ultra 2 is lighter than its predecessor, and the case is still made from durable titanium, whereas the Watch 9 is encased in aluminum.  The Galxy Watch Ultra 2 has more buttons than the Galaxy Watch 9 (Credit: Eric Zeman)  The Ultra 2 is also much more durable than its less expensive sibling, and this added ruggedness is the main reason to consider spending up for it. That said, the Galaxy Watch 9 is no slouch when it comes to durability. An IP68 rating ensures the Galaxy Watch 9 is impervious to dust and can be submerged in water, while its 5ATM rating means it can withstand underwater depths of up to 164 feet (50 meters). The Watch 9 even has a MIL-STD-810H rating, a military durability standard, meaning it can withstand extreme temperature changes, blowing sand, and other environmental stressors, such as vibration and shock.  The Ultra 2 offers a superior 10ATM rating, meaning it can be submerged to depths of 328 feet (100 meters), an IP69K resistance rating ensuring protection from high-pressure water jets, and the same military endurance certification as the Watch 9. On top of that, the Ultra 2 carries an EN13319 diving certification, whereas the original Ultra and the Galaxy Watch 9 are not safe for scuba descents. Overall, the Ultra 2 is well-suited for rough-and-tumble adventures on land or at sea. The Best Smartwatches for 2026  Fitness and Health: Niche Outdoor Tools vs. Everyday Metrics  The Galaxy Watch 9 and the Ultra 2 debut with a handful of new health features, including a Heart Health Score that monitors your overnight blood pressure and daily activity to assess your risk of heart disease, and provides tips to improve the score. Fitness Index quantifies your well-being, showing body composition, cardio, endurance, flexibility, and strength in a single spider chart. Daily Cardio Load gives runners an idea of how much to push in a day to maximize gains without burnout.  Galaxy Watch Ultra 2 (Credit: Eric Zeman)  The latest Galaxy Watches also monitor your vitals. After establishing baseline values for your blood oxygen saturation, heart rate, heart rate variability, respiratory rate, and skin temperature, they notify you of any concerning deviations they notice.  To support your adventures, the Ultra 2 comes with a couple of unique features to keep you informed as you hike or dive. Trail Run monitors your elevation and route, and tracks your terrain to warn you if it's rough enough to cause potential injury. A Nutrition Alert tracks your sweat loss and reminds you to hydrate. In the water, the Diving app can track depth and temperature as well as safe ascent and descent speeds.  These new features join the Galaxy Watch line's already impressive list of health and wellness capabilities. An AI coach provides guidance on running and sleeping. Overnight, the Galaxy Watch can listen for snoring (when paired with a nearby Samsung phone) and monitor for sleep apnea. You can also use the watch to check your body composition, similar to a smart scale. An antioxidant test provides insight into your diet, vascular load detection checks your arterial health, and advanced glycation end products (AGEs) index tracking provides a detailed picture of your metabolism.  In testing, both proved to be accurate exercise trackers but showed occasional gaps in the sleep data they collected, skewing the otherwise precise numbers.  Winner: Galaxy Watch Ultra 2  Lifestyle Features: Why You Don't Need the Ultra for AI  Both the Galaxy Watch 9 and the Galaxy Watch Ultra 2 have access to many useful third-party Wear OS apps, including Google's many tools and popular options like Spotify and Strava. Both have Google's Gemini built in to answer questions, send texts, and interact with Samsung apps such as Calendar, Clock, Health, and Reminder via voice commands.  Galaxy Watch 9 (Credit: Eric Zeman)  New to the latest models, you can raise your wrist to talk to Gemini instead of hitting a shortcut button or using wake words, making the generative AI assistant that much easier to access. Overall, the Galaxy Watch 9 keeps up with its more expensive sibling in terms of lifestyle features, for less money.  Winner: Tie  Verdict: Two Different Use Cases  The Galaxy Watch Ultra 2 fills a more niche role than its general-purpose sibling. If you have an Android phone and want a watch to keep up with you on outdoor adventures (or you simply want multiple days of battery life), the Ultra is the one to get. The Galaxy Watch 9 otherwise offers most of the same features but costs much less, making it a good choice for Samsung fans who want to save money.
