ZDNET's key takeaways
Qwen is now the fastest-growing AI tool in history.
It debuted earlier this month in public beta.
Alibaba says it plans to expand the app's functionality.
Chinese tech giant Alibaba's Qwen AI assistant reached 10 million downloads within the first week following its launch as a public beta app earlier this month, the company announced Monday. 
That means Qwen AI is growing faster than its well-established rivals. For reference, ChatGPT reportedly reached a million users within the first five days following its public debut just under three years ago, which was viewed as historic at the time. ChatGPT remains the most popular chatbot by several measures, but competitors like Microsoft Copilot and Google's Gemini have recently been gaining ground.

ChatGPT isn't available in mainland China, nor are other leading AI tools built by US-based companies, such as Anthropic's Claude. Models like Qwen, which are built by Chinese companies, have exclusive access to the country's population of around 1.4 billion people, roughly 17% of the global population.
What gives the model its edge?
The Nov. 17 beta launch of the Qwen app -- available in the Chinese version of Apple's App Store as Qwen Chat -- "marked Alibaba's most significant step yet into the consumer AI market, aiming to transform its cutting-edge foundational AI model capabilities into real-life applications and tools," the company wrote in its announcement. It added that the tool "is built to become the AI-powered gateway to daily life." The app is currently only available for users in mainland China.
The Qwen app specializes in "deep research" -- an industry phrase describing an AI tool's ability to search across a swathe of online resources and synthesize complex information -- as well as vibe coding, AI-powered camera features, slide deck generation, and other functions, according to the company.

It's powered by the Qwen3 family of models, which Alibaba introduced in April. The company said that the largest of those, Qwen3-235B-A22B, is a mixture-of-experts (MoE) model that exhibits skills in coding, math, and other domains exceeding or comparable to other leading models, including OpenAI's o1, DeepSeek's R1, and Gemini 2.5 Pro.
Alibaba wrote in its announcement that it plans to expand Qwen's repertoire, integrating "core lifestyle and productivity services" such as food delivery, "health guidance," travel booking, and e-commerce.
"This ecosystem approach will empower the assistant to handle a wide array of real-world tasks," the company wrote, "equipping it as a proactive, capable life and work partner."

Moonshot, another Chinese tech developer, made headlines earlier this month with the release of its Kimi K2 Thinking model, which, according to the company, outperforms OpenAI's GPT-5 and Anthropic's Claude Sonnet 4.5 on key testing benchmarks, including Humanity's Last Exam.
Government ties
Alibaba is a publicly traded company, but as Bloomberg reported early last year, entities owned by the Chinese government claim at least partial ownership of more than a dozen of its business units. 
A Reporters Without Borders investigation published in September found that three leading AI models developed by Chinese labs, including Qwen, generate outputs that "strictly align with Beijing's official narratives, especially when it comes to the integrity of China's political system, its ideology and territorial claims." Models from DeepSeek, another Chinese AI lab, showed similar signs of censorship when they were released in January.
