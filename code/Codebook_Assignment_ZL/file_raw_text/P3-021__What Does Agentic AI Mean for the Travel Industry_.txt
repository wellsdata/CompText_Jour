Conversational AI makes the trip planning process easier, while agentic AI elevates the process closer to the purchase path.
For the past few years, we've mostly talked to AI. We ask it for suggestions or explanations. We may have it write itineraries or draft emails. But it's ultimately just a conversation. It doesn't take action for us in the world. With the advent of agentic AI, that is changing.
Over the next few years, travel brands will integrate AI agents in sales, marketing, and all other business development. Conversational AI has already transformed how travelers choose destinations and plan trips as well as how destination marketing organizations (DMOs, or tourism boards) engage travelers and learn about what interests them. Agentic AI is the next evolution.
How do AI agents work?
Since the public launch of large language models (LLMs) in late 2022, AI has been making decisions. If you ask it for recommendations, it decides, based on the input you gave it, what to include and what to exclude. AI agents combine this decision-making capability with varying degrees of autonomy to achieve specific tasks.
For example, at Matador Network, there are a lot of travel creators we want to keep up with, either just to be aware of trends or with an eye toward potential collaborations. We set up an AI agent to monitor the social accounts of 150 creators and produce a digest email for us once a week summarizing what they've been up to and highlighting key takeaways.
This is essentially an automated workflow, which at the most basic level is what AI agents are. But the more trust we place in their decision making, the less automated and more proactive they become.
The classic example in the travel industry is instead of asking for flight recommendations, you would simply instruct an AI agent to book you a flight. You might have final approval, but you're not actually visiting an OTA or an airline website. The technology isn't there just yet, but it's close.
ChatGPT just launched an integration with Expedia and Booking that, in theory, allows you to book travel within the ChatGPT interface. We have spent a lot of time trying to use this feature and have yet to successfully book anything. But I trust the technology will evolve to deliver that functionality soon.
Marketing applications for agentic AI in the travel industry
AI avatars for marketing and sales are examples of agentic AI already being successfully deployed in many industries, including travel. From the user perspective, the experience is basically a more engaging conversation with a chatbot. You're on a video call with an AI agent that looks and sounds like a person and answers any questions you have in real time.
But behind the scenes, the brand has often empowered the avatar to make a wide variety of decisions. For example, an avatar from a tour operator may be able to offer different products and pricing depending on factors like current demand and availability, as well as information the user has shared such as group size, budget, and readiness to book.
The result is disruption of the linear, one-way marketing funnel we're used to in which content is bait that brings in leads which have to be nudged along each stage of a generic process. Instead, we're starting to see a flywheel in which content fuels the AI with information it uses to create a personalized process that compresses lag between awareness and action.
Why the travel industry is built for agentic AI
Prior to the launch of LLMs, the online travel planning and booking process was so tedious. I immediately pivoted my business when I saw how AI could remove friction. Travel is such an obvious AI use case that ChatGPT even runs TV commercials about it.
. Instead of searching, comparing, coordinating, booking, and then potentially rebooking or repricing, an agent will soon be able to simply handle all of this with as much or as little oversight as you want to give it. Travelers can go straight from idea to booked.
Another travel industry problem agentic AI solves is fragmented data. Information from airlines, hotels, OTAs, car rental companies, insurance providers, and loyalty programs is hard to gather and it's inconsistent. Conversational AI is great at rounding all of this up, but agentic AI prevents you from having to wade into it at all.
It's now been over two years since the start of the wave of reporters using AI to take trips. Journalists seemed to immediately see the potential for treating AI like a self-driving car of trip planning-just hop in and go. But the results were mixed because while the technology was clearly powerful, it couldn't handle flawlessly executing a trip from end to end. By the end of 2026, agentic AI will have arrived at this point.
