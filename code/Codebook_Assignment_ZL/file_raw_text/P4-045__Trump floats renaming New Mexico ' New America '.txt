


Trump floats renaming New Mexico 'New America'

Body


New Mexico landed in the crosshairs of President Donald Trump's penchant for renaming this weekend.
Trump took to Truth Social on Sunday to post a map of the state, crossing out the word "Mexico" and instead labeling it "New America." It was one in a bizarre string of posts from the president, including many images that appeared to be generated by artificial intelligence or otherwise doctored.
The Trump administration has yet to issue any formal action - such as an executive order - on New Mexico's name. However, the official White House account retweeted the map on the social media platform X, formerly Twitter.
The possibility of a name change comes less than two weeks after Trump announced plans to rename Lake Ontario "Lake America." He issued an executive order renaming the Gulf of Mexico on the first day of his second term with the stated aim of "restoring names that honor American greatness."
New Mexico Republicans were mostly mum on Trump's proposal to call the state "New America." Officials from the Republican Party of New Mexico and spokespeople for the state House and Senate GOP caucuses did not immediately respond to requests for comment on the matter.
Republican gubernatorial candidate Gregg Hull was an exception.
"New Mexico does not need a new name," Hull said in a statement. "This state carries the history of Native nations and Spanish explorers who named this land nearly five hundred years ago, and generations of families who built New Mexico into what it is today. That identity is not up for negotiation."
New Mexico Democrats, however, were far from amused by the proposed name change. Their response was best summed up in a tweet from Democratic gubernatorial nominee Deb Haaland: "You can't be serious," she wrote.
"New Mexico is not for renaming. Not up for sale," said Haaland, a former congresswoman and interior secretary.
"Our state's name carries generations of history, culture, and family - and it belongs to us," U.S. Rep. Melanie Stansbury, D-N.M., posted on X. "We're New Mexicans. Period."
Gov. Michelle Lujan Grisham slammed Trump's proposal as a way to "distract" Americans from affordability issues and the war in Iran.
"New Mexico's name isn't up for debate - it's been ours since before the United States existed," she said.
"Under Democratic leadership here in New Mexico, we're providing families with free childcare and college, and protecting their access to health care," Lujan Grisham added. "While the White House wastes time coloring on maps, Nuevo México is busy doing the work."
U.S. Rep. Gabe Vasquez, a Democrat who is facing Republican challenger Greg Cunningham in a race to represent New Mexico's most closely divided congressional district, called Trump's post an effort to "erase New Mexico."
"The President may want to call us 'New America,' but we know who we are," Vasquez posted on X. "Greg Cunningham has pledged himself to Trump's agenda but New Mexico has an opportunity to elect someone who represents us - real New Mexicans - not the sellouts like Cunningham."
"It's New Mexico, always has been and always will be," U.S. Sen. Ben Ray Luján, D-N.M., posted on X. "Just like how you'll always be a sinvergüenza and corrupt."
U.S. Sen. Martin Heinrich, also a New Mexico Democrat, tweeted a list of things he will continue to call by their "actual names": "The Gulf of Mexico, Lake Ontario, New Mexico."
