


AI Is Creating a New Kind of Workplace Burnout-and Most Bosses Are Completely Unprepared for It

Body


Managers need to be proactive about workers' mental wellbeing-perhaps more than before.
Gen-Z is often credited with leading awareness of mental health issues in the workplace. This generation is more comfortable with the subject than their elders, often choosing to prioritize mental wellbeing instead of bowing to traditional workplace norms-like "putting the company first." But despite rising awareness, many workplaces simply aren't keeping pace with supporting workers' mental health, and this issue is likely going to get worse. A new report highlights that the rushed, chaotic, controversial arrival of AI tech is intensifying needs for mental health help-and that company leaders just aren't equipped to help.
Recent surveys have highlighted how much pressure workers feel they're under, HRDive notes in its report. A study by online jobseeker network Monster found nearly six in 10 employees said their job is actively harming their mental wellbeing, and a survey from social job site Glassdoor showed a dramatic uptick in users reporting burnout compared to last year. 
All the traditional issues are still at play when it comes to workplace stressors, of course-everything from bad bosses to toxic workplaces and too many demands on workers' time. But the big culprit adding a whole new layer of stress, according to experts who spoke to the news site, is AI. 
It's not necessarily the tech itself that's to blame either, though reports say certain workers feel distinct unease about being forced to use AI systems. Subtle workplace cultural changes that accompany AI rollouts are increasing stress. For example, one consultant argued that AI's arrival is driving a near-constant sense of urgency in delivering work that can emphasize any feelings of psychological unsafety, leading to burnout. This parallels numerous reports of company leadership rolling out AI without adding in necessary training, then expecting workers to just deliver boosted productivity. Another expert noted that managers who feel they're on a leadership track can push their workers relentlessly, overworking themselves or pushing their staff into unhealthily competitive mindsets-and AI adds to this heady mix of emotional stressors.
Meanwhile, AI is known to worry many people who picture the tech replacing them in the long term, and who worry that by using AI every day at their desk they're merely training the machine that will soon displace them. A brand new report also highlighted that even Gen-Z, the smartphone-loving digitally native generation, is wary that overusing AI is eroding their skill sets...with long-term implications for their career and the job market as a whole. 
Add in the fact that company leaders, and even many HR professionals, aren't approaching mental wellbeing at work in the right way, and you've got a recipe for some serious health damage. In a new HRDive survey of HR workers, fully 55 percent said they feel mental health matters will grow in importance in the coming years, which is up from last year's 48 percent figure. 
Lisa Gross, chief people officer at health app-maker Headspace, told the publication that in her experience "people can sustain high performance, but not in a constant state of depletion." As pressures mount on workers thanks to AI and expectation of ever more efficient delivery, she feels that "the best organizations recognize that energy is a renewable resource only if it's able to replenish." The firms that have done well at this in the past have taken a holistic approach, and have "focused on both structural changes and benefits that really work for employees."
The issue, experts suggest, is that leaders need to be better at modeling good mental health behaviors themselves; policies need to be drafted that are proactive rather than reactive regarding protecting workers' mental wellbeing; and workplace culture shifts are needed that allow staff to depressurize and replenish their energy levels. 
The big take-away for your company
The important ideas here can be summed up in three points:
AI, and accompanying workplace changes, are increasing workplace stressors
Mental health issues at work are more in focus nowadays, and Gen-Z is leading this change
Managers and HR teams need to be more on top of pro-mental health policies, management styles and workplace culture than maybe they are right now. And they need to act fast as AI is accelerating the demand for change
Certain management styles involve constantly pressuring workers, with a "leave your troubles at the door" mindset. But as much data shows, and expert opinion backs up in this new report, workers are only human. And unlike the controversial, challenging AI tech that's upsetting the balance in many workplaces, humans' mental health is important. This is something that savvy company leaders should bear in mind when rolling out AI systems, when recruiting new, work-hungry Gen-Z staff and when trying to shape company culture in the AI era.
