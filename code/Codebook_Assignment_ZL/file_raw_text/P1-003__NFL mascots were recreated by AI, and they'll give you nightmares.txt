Body


At the end of July, AI recreated all 30 mascots in Major League Baseball. 
That was interesting.
Now AI — artificial intelligence for those who aren't hip to the times — has recreated all 32 mascots of the NFL, and they're terrifying. The graphics were created by the guys over at betonline.ag again using the image tool "Midjourney" for all 32 teams.
I completely understand that the football mentality is all rough and tough, failure is not an option, do or die, but does that have to trickle over to the team mascots who are supposed to be these fun people in costumes exciting fans — especially kids?
Off the bat, I'll tell you some of these AI recreations are fun and fit the traditional mascot appeal. The others, well, let's just be thankful that these are computer-generated characters made in the spirit of fun — or terror — you decide.
Let's get started...
Arizona Cardinals
There are a few NFL franchises named after birds, and as you move along through this list you'll see they almost all look alike. 
This is one of the more fierce Cardinal mascots I've seen between professional, minor league, semi-pro and college sports. It definitely fits a football persona, but isn't this suppose to be a cardinal? 
This mascot looks like he auditioned for the Philadelphia Eagles and didn't get the part. He didn't give up on his dream though, he dyed his feathers, tried out for the Arizona Cardinals and got the job. 
Atlanta Falcons
Again, fits the football persona, but just like its friend on the Cardinals, this mascot could've tried out for the Eagles or even the Baltimore Ravens.
It's not too scary or too over the top, and maybe the team should adopt this character — it could inspire the team and fans to get on board with same thing everyone else wants, winning. 
Baltimore Ravens
Just like its fellow AI Baltimore mascot, the Oriole, this mascot looks like one of the crows from "Dumbo" who broke away from Disney and chose a different career path. 
It's often been said, "The pen is mightier than the sword," so I'll take the descriptions and writings of a raven from Baltimore's Edgar Allen Poe compared to this. Nice try though.
Buffalo Bills
Finally, a mascot that isn't a bird.
This is something I can get behind. It's a very definitive character that looks like he's a fun guy to give a high-five to, but also be a dominant image and crowd-pleaser when the Bills are in the red zone. 
Carolina Panthers
Don't let this be the last thing your kids see before going to bed. Wow.
Yes, panthers are supposed to be fierce, and they're supposed to be a predator on the prowl, but isn't this a bit much? Kids at a Panthers game with this mascot would freak out at just the sight of it. It's supposed to be a mascot not some painting on the side of a WWII fighter plane. 
I will give a tip of the cap to the color scheme though. The Panthers have one of the best in all four of the major sports and they create a lot of promotional and marketing items very well without overdoing and shoving the colors in your face — take a hint Pittsburgh. AI did a great job working those colors into creating a character. 
Chicago Bears
Can AI please find a happy medium with bears? 
They made the Cubs look all cuddly and cute in a baseball uniform, now it's made the crosstown NFL Bears into "Cocaine Bear." 
The team's current mascot, Staley Da Bear, is much more well done. He's the big guy at the party that's everyone's friend and is always the life of the event. Now that's a bear mascot, ladies and gentlemen, not this thing that's in need of anger management and a little bit of Jesus. 
Cincinnati Bengals
There's a little bit of biasness with this one as I've adopted the Bengals as my NFL team after the Rams left my hometown of St. Louis. Plus when I was kid, and to this day, I find their helmets to just be awesome.
I'm not sure how the rivalry between Michigan and Ohio would feel if their mascots suited up for one team during baseball season and another during football season, but this could work well in Detroit and Cincinnati — given the similarity between the respective mascots. 
As far as this mascot goes overall, it would be a much better one to have other than the team's current mascot, Who Dey the Tiger, who looks more like Disney's Tigger in a football uniform. 
The Bengals have worked their way back to being one of the top teams in the NFL and what better time to get a new mascot brand out there than with this? You could even name it River, to honor the old Riverfront Stadium.
Cleveland Browns
The Dawg Pound was the east endzone bleacher section in old Cleveland Stadium and it has since been a staple at the team's new stadium built on the same site. That's pretty much where the dog mascot comes in. 
Brownie the Elf is also one of the team's longtime characters, but as to which is the official mascot or if there even is an official mascot or if it's a shared position — in all honesty, that's a mystery. Because Brownie the Elf is painted on the 50-yard line and the team continues to just have that burnt brown with a solid brown stripe for a helmet. And let's not forget the actual dog, SJ who makes his appearances at games and around town too.
But AI hit the nail on the head with this one. You've got an animated character that was born out of team enthusiasm in the 1980s, evolved into tradition, and its carried on ever since. 
Dallas Cowboys
I don't know what to make of this. 
It's more like a Texas Robocop.
Either that or if "Dallas" star Patrick Duffy and an alien had a baby, you'd get this. 
This looks like something out of "The Jetsons" or an image of what people in 1955 envisioned for 2023. Seriously, does this thing come with a jet pack? 
Creativity gets two thumbs up, but associating this with a storied NFL franchise, no. Just no.
Denver Broncos
Well, this is a whole lot better than what the Denver Broncos have now.
This cool dude has the features of the perfect mascot. Party guy, awesome with high-fives, great with kids, and hey, I'm diggin' the cool shades, bro. 
Definitely a far cry from the Bronco's current mascot, Miles, who looks like he could be the mighty steed for Lord Voldemort.
Detroit Lions
Well, well, the cowardly lion found his courage and made his way to Motown. 
I don't dislike this mascot, but I don't like it either. It's "meh" for me, just seems kind of boring. 
AI was able to give some fierceness to the Bears, but lacks even a notion of it in this mock-up. It's a lion for crying out loud, give it some dignity. 
This was a great opportunity for AI to do something great, and give Detroit fans much more than what they have now in their current mascot Roary.
Fail. 
Green Bay Packers
Wisconsin is known as The Cheese State and Green Bay Packers fans have become very well known for wearing those giant cheese heads at games.
So what's best for the team's mascot? Apparently, AI thinks a mouse is perfect for the job. Cute and clever. I mean what else can you play with if you're taking the Cheese State approach? A bottle of wine mascot? I'll take the mouse.
Houston Texans
This is what a Texas football mascot should look like. 
I started out in Texas and if there's one thing The Lone Star State has above the other 49 is a lot of pride. There's pride for everything in Texas, most especially football. And as someone who covered football in Odessa and suburban Fort Worth, everything down to the mascot has to represent the state in a state pride type of way. 
Long story short, AI scored on a Hail Mary pass with this one. Sorry, Dallas, better luck next time.
Indianapolis Colts
What on earth is this?
This mascot is basically what you saw if you breathed in too much air at Woodstock. An overabundance of incense and peppermints went into this one. 
I'll definitely take the Colts' fun, muppet-like mascot Blue over this "horse" that looks like one of those cheap stuffed animals your kids beg you far in the checkout line in Target. 
Honestly, the more I look at this it gives more of a haunted doll, crazy circus clown kind of unpleasantness. 
Jacksonville Jaguars
This guy looks like a lot of fun. 
He's one cool cat, but also wants to win as badly as everyone else at a Jags game. Who better to get the crowd going for a team trying to follow in the steps of the Bengals and show the sports world they're not perennial underdogs anymore? This guy. Just an awesome looking mascot. 
Kansas City Chiefs
I went to college in the Kansas City region and I was surrounded by a lot of Chiefs fans. But I honestly can't say what exactly they'd think about this. The city only has two of the major sports, and the Royals aren't exactly a team to look out for in the coming years for a world championship.
First of all, I get it. It's time to break away from Native American mascots, and AI takes a great approach by taking the king of the jungle, a lion, a synonym for a chief — so for the sake of this segment let's say a lion is also the chief of the jungle — and puts it all together in Chiefs colors. 
A great attempt, but one problem, there's already a lion representing a KC sports team and they're across the parking lot at Kauffman Stadium — at least for now. 
AI doesn't seem to be too intelligent if it was staying away from the Native American persona and finding an alternative. If it was really intelligent, perhaps it would've chosen a mule, the state animal of Missouri. But then again, the Chiefs may play in Missouri, however, they represent the region which also includes Kansas City, Kansas.
Maybe a pony to represent the historic Pony Express, which was made available to the great American west and began in nearby St. Joseph, Missouri. 
Los Angeles Rams
I think just this once I'm allowed to biased opinions. 
I could care less about anything to do with the Rams. Being a St. Louis native and witnessing first hand, like so many others, how the team's owner — he who shall not be named —treated the city and its countless efforts to keep them in the midwest. (For those of you keeping track, that's the second Harry Potter reference.)
By the way, I'll let the XFL continue to prove that owner wrong. 
This mascot is just plain bad. And that's not being biased, it looks cheap. Too cheap for a middle school mascot much less an NFL mascot. It's kind of creepy too, looks like Doug the sheep in "Zootopia" got clean and is doing the best he can to make it out in LA like everyone else with aspiring dreams. 
Las Vegas Raiders
The Raiders have a great fan base, and their section of passionate costumed fans in what's called "The Black Hole" is probably the most intense place to be at a sports game — ever since the team's move from Oakland to Las Vegas. 
This mascot is a great salute to those fans and team tradition. And it's just spot on. But truthfully, I'm sure some of the actual living and breathing fans in that section, of them can get even scarier than this mascot with all of their love and passion for Raider Nation. 
Los Angeles Chargers
Uh, okay. 
I see what AI is trying to do here. Chargers, a jet pilot charging into battle, but this doesn't exactly seem like much to me. Just doesn't have that football appeal in my opinion.
The team's current mascot, Boltman, also looks like it was designed by a sixth grader, but I'll take that over this. 
Miami Marlins
This isn't worth my time. It's just God-awful.
Minnesota Vikings
'Bout time AI created an actual human as a mascot for a team. 
This guy looks like social media influencer BeardOh, one of the most awesome guys out there on the web. I could see this guy suiting up just like this to be the Vikings mascot, despite it being a distance from his hometown in Canada. 
But if BeardOh isn't available, a costume of this magnitude would just be one of the best. 
New England Patriots
I'd like to think someone of patriotic, historical significance would at the very least be the inspiration for this mascot. Even in the world of AI. 
Alas, AI decided to literally dig up one of the founding fathers and dress them up in their revolutionary war gear. The team is the New England Patriots, but this mascot makes the team look like the New England Zombie Patriots. 
This looks like Alexander Hamilton crawled out of his grave and got here just in time to film the "Thriller" music video.
New Orleans Saints
This is perfect. 
Not only does this have great football and mascot appeal, but it's also very well done. Looks a lot like St. Patrick with a black and gray beard ready to watch a good game of football and get the crowd revved up at the same time. 
Given the way the Saints played last season, a mascot with the power of a blessing couldn't hurt. 
New York Giants
If this could be pulled off in real life, this would be one of the best mascots ever. 
You'd just have to find someone tall enough or someone who can move around well in such a big suit. The Giants don't seem to have a consistent character in costume, so this would be something great to start with. 
And here's Harry Potter reference No. 3: Looks a lot like Hagrid's half-brother Grawp, doesn't it? 
New York Jets
Yeah, let's just give AI an "A" for effort on this one. 
Guess you could go in any direction for a team like the Jets, but let's be reasonable and start to feel like the rest of the Jets fan base and hope that Aaron Rodgers is what they've needed. 
Philadelphia Eagles
A team name like the Eagles seems to have its limits when it comes to developing a mascot. 
I like it, but I'm not impressed. This is pretty much what anyone who roots for any Eagles team in any sport would probably envision. 
And as I mentioned before, this guy could probably fill in for the Cardinals, Falcons and Ravens with just a few wardrobe changes. 
Pittsburgh Steelers
A Lego? Really? 
Pittsburgh is a big sports town with three of the four major sports and giving the Steelers, the city's true pride and joy, this giant Lego man to resemble a steel worker is insulting. 
Say what you want, and have your fears, but AI isn't as intelligent as it seems to be and this Pittsburgh Lego man is proof. 
San Francisco 49ers
Well, AI tried, and it failed. 
A miner mascot with a pickaxe dressed in 49ers gear would've done just fine. 
Seattle Seahawks
Oh yea, I forgot about the Seahawks. 
This guy could too fill in for the other bird teams if one of them called in sick. 
It's okay, not great, but AI did work in the team colors as they did with the Panthers. So in that respect, it's not too bad. 
Tampa Bay Buccaneers
This mascot wanted to move on to bigger and better things, so he quit working at Disney World on the Pirates of the Caribbean ride and found his true calling the crowd-pleaser for the Bucs.
It's a nice attempt, creative for sure, but I'd prefer more of a play on the team logo with a skeleton pirate with a sword cap and earring.
Tennessee Titans
A team name like the Titans definitely gives any graphic designer, AI or human, a lot to work with and plenty of avenues to take. 
This monster, gorilla-looking thing is a fantastic creation, but it gives the appeal of the guy at work you can only take so much of. The guy everyone warns all the new people about. "Don't get caught up in a conversation with Kevin, you'll never get your work done."
Something more Nashville would be best too. Like a Johnny Cash-inspired mascot, a titan of country music if you will. 
Washington Commanders
Boy, good thing the team changed their name because I'm nervous just thinking of what AI would've come up with. 
Just like the Patriots, a historical model would've been nice. George Washington perhaps, is after all a team in the nation's capital.
AI went generic, and that's fine, but it just looks boring, as if Washington needed something in regard to a mascot and came up with this. Doesn't exactly scream football or fun in my opinion.
