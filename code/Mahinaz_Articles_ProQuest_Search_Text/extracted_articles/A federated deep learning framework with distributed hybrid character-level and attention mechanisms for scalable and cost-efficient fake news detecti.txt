Introduction Social media platforms such as Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, and YouTube have been developed into vital communication mediums that allow instant sharing of messages among users at any time. The big challenge for online social network 1 users is to find reliable information due to the existence of incorrect information. Because of the instance access of social media, false/rumor messages 2,3 that spread among a huge online crowd also causes severe harm to society and mislead the social network users. Unfortunately, social networking sites in recent years have transformed this issue into a significant one. Therefore, there is a great need for automated approaches for detecting rumors in a timely manner. The identification and detection of rumor information is one of the most crucial steps in preventing the spread of rumor. There are a wide variety of existing rumor detection techniques that deal with machine 4 and deep learning methods 5 .
To represent the natural language text in to vector, various supervised and unsupervised word embedding techniques known as Word2Vec, Glove, FastText and BERT are used. While representing the same word in different context, few embedding techniques provides same vector. It will not justify the word semantic properly. To solve this issue, some of the context based embedding techniques are employed which produces different word vector for same word present in different context 6 . Moreover, most of the word embedding techniques identifies only the word vector for already learned words. The word vector for new word that is not present in the dictionary is provided by introducing out of vocabulary handling word embedding techniques. Character based embedding technique is introduced in this work which reads the sequence of input characters as tokens for predicting the output. This sequence of input characters is converted into vector and a new unlearned words vector is produced with this character vector. Convolutional neural network helps to produce word vector from the character vectors. Multiple CNN’s are involved in this word vector formation process.
A Onan 7 proposed embedding scheme to obtain the vectors of the input texts and significant topics are extracted from the input text collections. The ensemble based clustering analysis is also incorporated to improve the performance of the prediction. Onan 8 examined sarcasm in the form of nonliteral language, wherein words conveyed opposite meanings to express sentiments. The proposed a deep learning-based approach and compared topic-enriched word embeddings to traditional ones used for effective sarcasm identification. The traditional detection model uses, Machine Learning methods known as Naive Bayes, logistic regression, support vector machine, etc., for rumor detection which required perfect training data while performing classification. Training the fake contents is a big challenge in this research. Onan 9 used text classification for finding the characteristics of data, primarily focusing on expressive, appellative, and informative categories. Supervised machine learning algorithms were commonly employed, necessitating the detection of a rich set of features for robust classification. The output is combined to obtain robust classification results.
Nowadays, Deep Learning (DL) models are replaced instead of ML and it have been widely used in the field of text feature analysis where their enormous potential has been shown. In existing, few deep learning-based feature selection methods are used to extract efficient features with single feature selection technique. Also, it is possible to extract most important and accurate features with the utilization of hybrid or sequential feature selection approaches to attain improved performance. Onan 10 aimed to optimize the analysing sentiment of instructor evaluation reviews using a recurrent neural network (RNN) model, demonstrating superior performance over traditional machine learning classifiers through comprehensive empirical analysis. Onan 11 adopted a powerful model for prediction with strong classification performance in MOOC reviews was showcased by integrating ensemble and deep learning paradigms. The contribution encompassed addressing diverse research questions related to sentiment analysis in educational data. The DL algorithms such as CNN, LSTM 12 , GRU 13 , Bi-LSTM 14 , Bi-GRU etc., are selectively used for effective rumor text classification task.
In recent years, the increasing volume of decentralized data across multiple news platforms have motivated the adoption of Federated Learning (FL) 15,16 in fake news detection 17 . Federated Learning 18,19 enables collaborative model training across multiple devices or data sources without transferring raw data to a central server. This approach not only protects sensitive information but also enhances model generalization by learning from diverse and distributed datasets. Integrating federated learning 20 into fake news detection 21 frameworks allows multiple media organizations to contribute to a shared model securely, thereby improving detection accuracy while significantly reducing centralized computational costs.
Major contributions In this work, the Federated COASBC model is proposed to identify the fake and real news. The model performance is assessed using four datasets, namely LIAR an augmented dataset, Fake & Real News dataset (ISOT), FakeNewsNet, and WELFake Dataset. The main contribution of this research work is as follows,

- CharBERT-based character-aware embedding model is introduced to combine character-level representations with multiple CNN layers to generate contextualized word vectors. The proposed embedding effectively handles misspelled and rare words by making the model robust for noisy and informal news content from social media platforms.

- A novel deep feature fusion model is developed by combining Optimized Convolutional Neural Network (OPCNN) with attention based Stacked Bi-LSTM (SBi-LSTM) for capturing relative and significant features between various classes to construct the efficient feature map.

- OPCNN is designed to capture local and discriminative semantic patterns from news text by enabling effective extraction of relevant features that contribute to accurate fake news classification.

- The attention-based SBi-LSTM captures long-range contextual dependencies and sequential relationships in the text by allowing the model to focus on informative words and phrases for classification. The incorporated attention mechanism dynamically assigns higher importance to significant features across different classes and improves the model’s ability to distinguish between fake and real news.

- In the proposed model, cost-sensitive learning is employed to address the unequal and misclassification costs associated with different class labels.

- The proposed model incorporates a Federated Learning framework using FedProx optimization technique to allow decentralized training across multiple news sources. It enhances data security, model generalization, and reduces centralized computational cost.

Literature survey In fake news detection system 22, 23–24 , spreading of false information on social media requires researchers to develop the best detection model for solving this kind of problem. There are a number of studies investigating the word embeddings and feature representation for the detection of rumors. Word embeddings captures the similarity between words and their linguistic connections. Traditional embedding models cannot deal with Out-of-vocabulary words and can lose the sentimental information. In recent years, transformer-based word embedding models have been developed for vector generation, but it will also not handle out-of-vocabulary words and misspelled words properly. Character based embedding models can mitigate this problem by embedding each character to produce the word vector. Wentao Ma et al. 25 proposed CharBERT which can enrich the representation of words by incorporating features at various levels of a word. The model is evaluated on eight benchmark datasets and the results prove that the CharBERT increases the performance than the BERT 26,27 and RoBERTa models. Onan 28 analysed reviews of products from twitter by utilizing the integration model of TF-IDF and CNN-LSTM for classification. Feature extraction is an essential step of rumor detection. A variety of automatic textual feature extraction approaches have been analyzed and the methods used for extracting the features significantly impact the results of the rumor detection model. A well-known feature extraction technique is convolutional neural network that uses several layers to find high-level and low-level features of fake news detection. In convolutional neural network, hyper parameter plays a vital role in each layer to detect the features for detecting the fake news. Hager Saleh et al. 29 employed optimized Convolutional Neural Network model 30 to select the optimal hyper parameter values of CNN in each layer by utilizing hyper-opt technique. Aytuğ Onan et al. 31 applied statistical keyword-based extraction methods and ensemble methods to classify the scientific text documents. Onan, A et al. 32 utilized ensemble method to choose the features from the texts. Individually, filter approach is used to find the features and finally all the individual feature lists are aggregated to generate the efficient feature subset which enhances the quality of classification model.
To improve performance of individual model, stacking approach is proposed in most of the research works. This approach combines the predictions from different ML (machine learning) models to build a new model. Tao Jiang et al. 33 proposed novel stacking model in which it utilizes 5 ML models and 3 DL models for fake news classification 34 . Vaibhav Rupapara et al. 35 designed stacked bi-directional LSTM that contains several layers of LSTM which are stacked and it traverses input data twice to enhance the accuracy of the classification. Onan et al. 36 proposed an NLP models and term-weighted word embeddings with trigrams. The stacked Bi-LSTM architecture with three layers achieved a high classification performance on sarcasm identification. Recently, hybrid models of several deep learning known as CNN, LSTM, Bi-LSTM and GRU have been proposed to improve the feature representation of the fake news prediction problem on social media. Arjun Roy et al. 37 developed the fake news prediction model which adopts CNN and Bi-LSTM network for feature representation. Onan et al. 38 developed an effective sentiment classification model using hybrid model in which ensemble pruning method is employed to integrate both clustering and randomized search methods. Shada Alharbi et al. 39 combined the model of CNN and LSTM for extracting the local and deep features to find rumors on Twitter dataset. Aytuğ Onan 40 combined the concepts of convolutional and recurrent neural network model which is integrated with bidirectional layers of LSTM and GRU to detect the high-level features for text classification.
In few research works, the attention-based 41, 42, 43–44 mechanisms have been introduced into various deep learning techniques for text classification. Aytuğ Onan 45 presented an improved text classification model that employs attention technique 46 to find the significant features of the nodes and BERT-based fusion method 47,48 is proposed to generate final vector representation which enhances classification accuracy. Abdulqader et al. 49 introduced hybrid attention neural network to find the rumors on social media platform by capturing related and significant features between various classes. Shiwen Ni et al. 50 developed Multi-View Attention Networks that applies two attention mechanisms to identify the words based on key clue in fake news words. Dun Li et al. 51 employed auto encoder for fake news detection that applies Bidirectional GRU and Self-Attention layer to obtain the hidden information and internal connection between each feature. Aytuğ Onan 52 employed text augmentation model which combines the genetic algorithms and graph-based attention network model to create several text data after augmentation. Aytuğ Onan 53 designed a framework for text augmentation that adapts semantic role labelling method to find the semantic of all the words present in each sentence and additional new sentences are generated for training by applying ant colony optimization method. Ghani et al. 54 . utilized multiscale feature fusion mechanism integrated with spatial attention mechanism. It significantly improves the recognition accuracy under noisy and challenging conditions.
Some research works have been developed to avoid the misclassification problem by utilizing the cost-sensitive learning methods. Zhi-Hua Zhou et al. 55 adopted cost-sensitive learning methods such as over sampling and under sampling to manage the class imbalance problem. Soleymanpour et al. 56 utilized Cost-Sensitive CNN in which cost matrix is adopted to allocate a cost for each class which enhances the accuracy of the classification model. Aytuğ Onan 57 proposed under sampling scheme of clustering-based model in which number of instances was under sampled to rectify the class imbalanced problem. Several recent studies have explored the use of Federated Learning in natural language processing (NLP) and misinformation detection to enable distributed and decentralized model development across multiple clients. Researchers have demonstrated that federated approaches can maintain high accuracy while keeping user and organizational data secure by exchanging only model parameters rather than raw content. Machová et al. 16 investigated the integration of Federated Learning with deep learning models 58 for fake news detection. Ghani et al. 59 proposed a GAN based augmentation framework with block chain to enhance the security in distributed environments. It provides secure and privacy-preserving data processing. Ghani et al. 60 utilized decentralized processing pipeline it addresses where the augmentation and blockchain technologies are combined to address data integrity concerns. Samriya et al. 61 proposed a fake news detection system combining blockchain, federated learning, and sentiment analysis. A generative convolutional Bernoulli Bayes neural network was used for feature extraction and classification of fake web recommendations.
Many existing fake news detection 62,63 studies mainly focused on word-level analysis, but character-level problems such as misspelling, rare words and informal writing are not handled properly. Several studies report that deep learning models perform better than traditional machine learning models. Hybrid models 64,65 enhance the performance by learning both low-level and high-level text features. However, the performance of the model strongly depends on the selection of suitable hyperparameters which remains a major challenge. Even though hybrid feature extraction methods improve classification accuracy, many approaches still struggle with noisy and unbalanced social media data. Although many fake news detection models report high accuracy, most existing studies have not properly addressed the misclassification problem caused by class imbalance. Most existing methods use softmax-based classification and standard loss functions, which do not handle class imbalance and lead to biased predictions toward minority classes. Many existing methods are based on centralized data storage and centralized model training. In these approaches, data from different media platforms must be collected and stored on a central server for model training. The centralized data storage creates a single point of failure and increases the risk of data leakage and unauthorized access. It also limits scalability and practical deployment. Managing large volume of data in one location is computationally expensive and inefficient. The proposed work aims to investigate these limitations by employing novel Federated COASBC model for fake news classification 66,67 .
Federated CharBERT-optimized CNN and attention based SBi-LSTM with cost sensitive (COASBC) The proposed Federated COASBC framework is a scalable solution for fake news detection. The integration of federated learning 68 with a hybrid deep learning architecture 69 is shown in Fig. 1. Unlike conventional centralized models, the federated framework enables multiple clients to collaboratively train a shared model without exchanging raw data and protect the sensitive information across media platforms.
Fig. 1 [Images not available. See PDF.]
Federated COASBC architecture.

Non-IID (non-independent and identically distributed) datasets such as LIAR, Fake and Real News (ISOT), FakeNewsNet, and WELFake Dataset are used for model evaluation. The ISOT dataset consists of real news articles collected from Reuters and fake news articles gathered from multiple unreliable sources identified by Politifact and Wikipedia. LIAR dataset contains short statements collected over a period of approximately ten years from Politifact, covering a wide range of speakers, topics, and contextual settings. FakeNewsNet is a comprehensive dataset that integrates news content with social context from platforms like Twitter, combining information from reliable fact-checking sources. The WELFake dataset is constructed by combining multiple existing datasets from Kaggle, the McIntire fake news dataset, Reuters, and the BuzzFeed Political dataset to create a larger and more diverse corpus. In the federated set up, Client 1 is assigned the Fake and Real News dataset, Client 2 uses the LIAR dataset, Client 3 uses the FakeNewsNet dataset, and Client 4 uses the WELFake dataset. Each client independently trains the local CharBert-Optimized CNN and Attention-based 70 stacked Bi-LSTM with Cost-Sensitive Learning (COASBC) model on its dataset. The model integrates character-level embeddings, convolutional neural networks, attention mechanisms, and stacked Bi-LSTM layers to find both fine-grained and contextual textual patterns. Cost-sensitive learning is incorporated to address class imbalance between fake and real news instances to improve performance of the model. After local training, clients transmit only the updated model parameters to a central server for aggregation. The server employs the FedProx algorithm to handle node heterogeneity and non-IID datasets. FedProx introduces a proximal term in each client’s local loss to restrict large deviations from the current global model for stabilizing convergence across clients with diverse data distributions and computational capabilities. The proximal coefficient 𝜆 controls the degree of regularization that limits the deviation of local model parameters from the global model. In this study, 𝜆 was selected empirically through hyperparameter tuning. Each client updates its local model while remaining close to the global model, improving stability and convergence of the federated training process. The global model is iteratively updated and redistributed to clients for subsequent training rounds, refining the shared model through direct data and gradient sharing. This federated approach allows the system to leverage diverse datasets distributed across multiple clients, improving model generalization to heterogeneous news sources. By integrating FedProx with the COASBC model, the framework achieves a balance between accuracy, scalability, and computational efficiency. The federated setup eliminates the need for centralized data storage, ensuring that raw news content never leaves local clients, while still enabling the global model to learn from multiple sources. Experimental evaluation on the Fake and Real News, LIAR datasets FakeNewsNet, and WELFake Dataset demonstrates that the proposed framework maintains high accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and robustness, even in a distributed, heterogeneous environment.
Design of the COASBC model for fake news classification The proposed work explains detection of fake news using character embedding with optimized CNN model along with attention based stacked Bi-LSTM model. This proposed work has significant stages namely character embedding with Optimized CNN, attention based efficient feature selection with stacked Bi-LSTM, and cost sensitivity-based rumour classification model. Figure 2 states the general overview of proposed work. The proposed COASBC architecture includes preprocessing stage, character embedding, an optimized convolutional neural network, an attention-based stacked Bi-LSTM and softmax-based classification layer. The text is preprocessed by applying stop-word removal, special character elimination, tokenization, and stemming to reduce noise and standardize the input data before feature extraction. After preprocessing, the text is transformed into character-level representations using a CharBERT embedding module which captures precise character information from each word. It effectively handles misspelling, and rare words in social media news content. The character-based embeddings represent text using individual characters rather than treating each word as a single unit. It allows the model to capture detailed patterns such as spelling variations, prefixes, suffixes, and morphological structures. It generates richer and more informative representations for effective feature extraction.
Fig. 2 [Images not available. See PDF.]
Proposed CharBERT-optimized CNN and attention based SBi-LSTM with cost sensitive (COASBC).

The generated embeddings are passed through an optimized convolutional neural network (OPCNN) which is designed to extract local and discriminative semantic features from the news text. The extracted feature maps are processed by an attention-based stacked Bi-LSTM that learns long-range contextual dependencies and sequential relationships in the text. The attention mechanism dynamically highlights important words and phrases that are most relevant for accurate classification. The final feature representations are passed to a dense layer. The SoftMax activation function is used to normalize the output vector and convert it into probability values for each class. To address class imbalance problem, cost-sensitive learning is applied by computing class weights for each class based on the distribution of training samples. These class weights assign different levels of importance to each class label during the classification process. It improves prediction reliability and reduces biased learning toward majority classes.
Pre-processing The input textual data is pre-processed by removing irrelevant information from the dataset and it is termed as Data Cleaning process. This irrelevant information has no gain in the process of detecting rumour information. Data Cleaning process helps to improve the quality of detection of rumour information.
- Removal of Stop Words: In the text analysis, stop words are not irrelevant thing. In order to removed or filtered such stop words like conjunctions, prepositions, articles.

- Removal of Special Symbols: Special symbols or characters do not impact the process. Therefore, it can be removed for the text analysis.

- Tokenization: It splits the longer text or sentences into smaller pieces of unit or tokens. Sentences are broken down into individual text or words.

- Stemming: Converting the words into their original text form and reduces the number of words from root to text. For example, the terms “asking,” “asked,” will be reduced to the word “ask”.


CharBERT model with multiple CNN Character embedding is an effective word selection method. Several language representation models have adopted the transformers architecture for efficient feature embedding. In the famous transformer BERT model, if any of the word which does not occur in its vocabulary then it splits the unknown word in to word pieces. Each word piece is embedded using embedding matrix while using this model it only focuses on subword instead of the entire word. These word pieces not always carry the prefix of the word, sometimes the preceding portion of the word may not be available. It is difficult to identify the exact word embedding of the given word token. CharBERT is a modified form of BERT that attempts to produce single embeddings for single word instead of word piece. It does not have any word piece vocabulary. It consists of character encoder and character decoder. Through character encoder, it can read the sequence of input characters as tokens, and it produces character vectors. The “base-uncased” type of BERT is used for CharBERT and it is a bidirectional transformer. This sequence of input characters is converted into vector. The overview of BERT and CharBERT is given in Fig. 3.

The sequence of character vectors is fed to multiple character CNN’s. It scans n character sequences at a time and it embeds the characters in the 16 dimensions by using small 1D-Multiple CNNs. All the character CNN outputs are aggregated into single vector. If the dataset contains misspelled words, then the traditional models ignore the incorrect words. In the proposed Optimized Convolutional Neural Network (OPCNN), Bayesian Optimization based hyperopt with Tree of Parzen Estimators (TPE) is employed for choosing the optimal hyper parameters of neural network. It uses sequential model-based optimization for minimizing the validation loss by selecting the best hyper parameters. It provides an interface for configuring the search space of the variables and customizing an evaluation function with the loss function to assess the parameters in search space. Hyperopt utilizes TPE, that search through an hyperparameter space of values for minimizing the loss function to yield better performance on both training and testing datasets.

Tuning the hyperparameters of CNN In proposed Optimized Convolutional Neural Network (OPCNN), hyper-parameters of hidden layers are tuned and further fed as input to the stacked Bi-LSTM attention layer. OPCNN possess different layers for handling the word embedding formed by CharBERT. The layers such as, Dropout, convolution, pooling and flatten. The OPCNN model takes word embedding as input and produces one dimensional feature vector as output from the flatten layer as shown in Fig. 5.
Dropout Layer During training process certain neurons are chosen and ignored randomly to prevent from complex computation.

Max-pooling layer The output of convolutional layer and to reduce the number of computational operation Max-pooling layer is effectively applied in the down-samples of the input. The hyperparameters such as the number of neurons, dropout rate, filter size, kernel size, pooling, batch size, and activation function directly influence the neural network’s structure and functionality. It has a set of values for various parameters. The number of neurons in every hidden layer is usually between the size of the input and output layers. The neurons are adjusted between 10 and 200. To prevent overfitting, the dropout regularization technique is implemented, which ignores the contribution of some neurons towards the next layer and it is set to the range of 0.1 to 0.6. By using filters, features are extracted parallelly by determining the size of the receptive field that has a varying number of filters from 32 to 512 for the given input. In addition, each filter hires the Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation function. Another hyperparameter is kernel size, it will be varied from 2 to 4. Usually, a kernel is a small vector that contains the weights. To reduce the number of features from the feature vector, the pooling layer performs the max operation and has a pool size of 3,6. Batch size decides the number of samples that will be passed through the network at one time. Hence, it has a range of values such as 32, 64, 73, 128, and 146. All these tuning is performed for a various number of epochs varying from 1 to 200.
The Bayesian optimization process using Hyperopt with the Tree of Parzen Estimators (TPE) algorithm is carried out for a few numbers of iterations. In each iteration, a single hyperparameter configuration is sampled from the defined search space and OPCNN model is trained using the configuration. The validation loss is computed and used as the objective function to guide the optimization. The TPE algorithm updates its probabilistic model after each iteration to focus on promising hyperparameter regions. The optimization process terminates after the maximum number of iterations is reached or if no further improvement in validation loss is observed. The hyperparameter set that achieves the minimum validation loss is selected as optimal configuration. The Optimal Parameter values for Optimized Convolutional Neural Network is described in Table 1.

Stacked Bi-LSTM (SBi-LSTM) with attention layer The features attained from the optimized CNN are fed to a SBi-LSTM model that produces a sequence representation. This section presents the Stacked Bidirectional-LSTM (SBi-LSTM) based fake news analysis model and it comprises stacking of two Bi-directional LSTM layers in a sequential manner. Traditional LSTM RNN have been extended as Bi-LSTM and the multiple Bi-LSTM layers are stacked which process the input multiple times and retain sequential information in a much efficient manner. Stacked Bi-LSTM can get rich contextual information from both past and future time sequences. During research, the stacked network achieved much better results than single layer of LSTM or Bi-LSTM.

Experimental setup with implementation details The proposed work has been implemented in python 3.10 using the LIAR 72 dataset and Fake & Real news (ISOT) 73 dataset. Each dataset is partitioned into 80% of training data and 20% of testing data to evaluate the performance of the model and to verify the testing outcome.
In the Optimized Convolution Neural Network (OPCNN), the best hyperparameters are selected for extracting the important features.
For LIAR dataset,

- The convolution layer with 48 neurons is selected in each hidden layer and it applies a dropout of 0.3 to prevent the overfitting problem. Each convolution layer contains the filter with size of 128 along with it chooses the kernel size of 3. Also, it takes the optimal batch size of 219 with 30 epochs for creating the feature map.



For Fake and Real News dataset,

- The convolution layer with 55 neurons is selected in each hidden layer and it applies a dropout of 0.4 to prevent the overfitting problem. Each convolution layer contains the filter with size of 22 along with it chooses the kernel size of 4. Also, it takes the optimal batch size of 500 with 30 epochs for creating the feature map.



For FakeNewsNet dataset,

- The convolution layer with 64 neurons is selected in each hidden layer and it applies a dropout of 0.4 to prevent the overfitting problem. Each convolution layer contains the filter with size of 128 along with it chooses the kernel size of 3. Also, it takes the optimal batch size of 256 with 30 epochs for creating the feature map.



For WELFake dataset,

- The convolution layer with 72 neurons is selected in each hidden layer and it applies a dropout of 0.3 to prevent the overfitting problem. Each convolution layer contains the filter with size of 128 along with it chooses the kernel size of 3. Also, it takes the optimal batch size of 64 with 30 epochs for creating the feature map.



In addition, the ReLU activation function is adopted to select multiple features. Further, max pooling operation is performed with the value of 3 to select the rich set of features. The features extracted from the OPCNN is fed to the first Bi-LSTM layer and further the output of the first Bi-LSTM layer will be input to the second Bi-LSTM to improve the rumor text classification accuracy. After each Bi-LSTM layer, one attention layer is included at the end. At last, dense layer is placed with softmax activation function. In the output dense layer, cost sensitivity is considered to reduce the misclassification rate. This entire model has been compiled for 30 epochs along with ‘Adam’ optimizer and the ‘categorical cross entropy’ loss function for loss prediction. An open source keras library is used for implementing the proposed model and it executes on TensorFlow platform. The experiments are conducted on a 2.40 GHz 11th Gen Intel(R) Core (TM) i5-1135G7 CPU of 8G RAM. The proposed federated learning framework consists of four heterogeneous clients, each trained on a distinct benchmark dataset. Specifically, Client 1 uses the Fake and Real News dataset, Client 2 uses the LIAR dataset, Client 3 uses the FakeNewsNet dataset, and Client 4 uses the WELFake dataset.
Evaluation metrics The performance of the proposed character embedding with attention mechanism is measured based on precision, recall, accuracy and F1-score.
Accuracy is calculated by finding the ratio between the correct classification and total number of classifications. 14

Precision is computed by taking the ratio between number of correct positive prediction and total number of positive predictions. 15

Recall is measured by taking ratio between number of correct positive prediction and total number of correct predictions. 16

F1-Score is mean of precision and recall. 17

Empirical analysis of CharBERT for robust text classification To evaluate the robustness of the proposed model, 20% of the true statements (11,138 samples) and 20% of the false statements (10,490 samples) from the Truth Seeker Dataset (2023) were reserved as an unseen test set which consists of user-generated tweets. For example, tokens such as “ANDDDDD”, “ALRIGHTTT”, “Aaaaannnnd”, “AMEEEEN”, and “ALLLLL” are frequently observed, reflecting common patterns of character repetition and non-standard spelling in social media text. These characteristics make the dataset particularly suitable for assessing the robustness of models designed to handle out-of-vocabulary (OOV) and morphologically complex inputs. To further validate the effectiveness of the proposed approach, we evaluated its performance in comparison with baseline transformer models such as BERT and RoBERTa under identical experimental settings. BERT and RoBERTa achieve strong baseline performance, with RoBERTa consistently outperforming BERT due to its improved pretraining strategy and better contextual representation. However, both models rely on subword tokenization, making them sensitive to misspellings words, which slightly limits their performance. CharBERT addresses this limitation by incorporating character-level representations, enabling it to better capture subword patterns and handle noisy text, resulting in improved precision, recall, and F1-score. Ablation analysis The performance of the proposed model is compared with various deep feature selection techniques such as OPCNN, OPCNN+LSTM, OPCNN + Bi-LSTM and OPCNN + SBi-LSTM.

- OPCNN: It is a basic CNN with hyperparameter tuning for fake news classification. Initially, char BERT model produces a word vector and the high-level features are extracted by using optimized CNN for classifying the rumor text.

- OPCNN+LSTM: It is a combination of OPCNN with LSTM. To find the dependency between the word sequences present in the sentences, optimized CNN algorithm is combined with LSTM. It provides better text classification results than simple optimized CNN along with high set of features.

- OPCNN + Bi-LSTM: It is a combination of OPCNN with Bi-LSTM. Instead of LSTM, the Bi-LSTM is used with OPCNN for predicting the word dependency in both the directions.

- OPCNN + SBi-LSTM: It is a combination of OPCNN with SBi-LSTM. Further, the Stacked Bi-LSTM with OPCNN is used to improve the prediction accuracy. Stacking more layers is highly advantageous in the feature detection process.

- OPCNN + SBi-LSTM (Attention + Cost Sensitivity): Previously discussed OPCNN with SBi-LSTM results are further improved by giving more attention and cost sensitivity. Moreover, attention based SBi-LSTM with OPCNN is applied to give more importance to the context words and class weights are added to reduce misclassification cost.

- Federated COASBC (CharBERT-Optimized CNN and Attention-based Stacked Bi-LSTM with Cost-Sensitive Learning): It integrates CharBERT-based character embeddings with an optimized CNN and an attention-enhanced stacked Bi-LSTM, incorporating cost-sensitive learning to address class imbalance. This federated architecture enables collaborative training across multiple clients and achieving high accuracy in fake news detection.

Conclusion Fake news detection demands advanced and intelligent models capable of addressing the complexity and diversity of online information. The proposed Federated COASBC framework introduces a hybrid architecture that integrates CharBERT embeddings for contextual character-level representation, an optimized CNN (OPCNN) for automated hyperparameter tuning through Hyperopt and an Attention-based Stacked Bi-LSTM for capturing long-range dependencies with focused attention on significant textual features. To overcome the issue of class imbalance, cost-sensitive learning is incorporated to provide balanced and reliable classification performance. In this approach, federated learning enables decentralized model training that enhances scalability and improves computational efficiency by distributing the training process across multiple clients. The model was evaluated on four benchmark datasets such as LIAR, Fake & Real News (ISOT), FakeNewsNet and WELFake datasets, which achieved superior results compared to traditional deep neural networks and other state-of-the-art methods. The proposed model achieved 79.18% accuracy, 77.12% precision, 78.34% recall, and 77.62% F1-score on the LIAR dataset. It attained 99.72% accuracy, 99.35% precision, 99.02% recall, and 99.18% F1-score on the Fake and Real News (ISOT) dataset. On the FakeNewsNet dataset, the model achieved 99.28% accuracy, 99.15% precision, 98.96% recall, and 99.05% F1-score. For the WELFake dataset, the model obtained 98.61% accuracy, 98.42% precision, 98.18% recall, and 98.30% F1-score. In addition, statistical analysis and model complexity analysis are conducted to assess the robustness of the model. The generalization of model is evaluated on Truth Seeker Dataset and achieved 98.3% accuracy. The results proved that the Federated-COASBC model effectively enhances fake news detection by improving accuracy, strong generalization, cost efficiency and computational optimization. The proposed framework was evaluated in a simulated federated learning setup with four clients, and the results confirm its effectiveness within this controlled experimental environment. Large-scale deployment, communication overhead per federated round, latency under realistic network conditions, and edge-device feasibility will be analyzed in future work.
