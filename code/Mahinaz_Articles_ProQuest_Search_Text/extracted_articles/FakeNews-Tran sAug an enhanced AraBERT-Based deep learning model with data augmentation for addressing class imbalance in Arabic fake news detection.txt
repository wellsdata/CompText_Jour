Introduction
The Internet and social media are growing so quickly that the amount of information being created and shared has reached levels never seen before, while the cost of sharing has dropped considerably (Muhammed & Mathew, 2022). Unfortunately, this has also facilitated the spread of fake information, fake news, and misleading content (Adeeb & Mirhoseini, 2023). Such deceptive elements have the potential to misinform the public (Shao et al., 2018), cause problems in society can potentially have serious effects, such as hurting people and causing problems on a national and worldwide scale (Al-Taie, 2025). As a result, addressing the issue of fake news and developing effective detection mechanisms has become imperative (Rahmanian, 2023).
There are a lot of big problems with finding fake news. One important problem is that real-world fake news databases typically have more real news than false news (Bhattacharjee, Maity & Chatterjee, 2022; Imani, Beikmohammadi & Arabnia, 2025). This imbalance can awry the performance of classifiers (Kuntur et al., 2024), diminishing their ability to accurately identify less frequent categories such as fake news. Moreover, the dominance of real-news samples can lead classifiers to erroneously predict fake-news instances as real news, exacerbating the social harm caused by fake news (Maharana, Mondal & Nemade, 2022).
To overcome these challenges, this study introduces a hybrid deep learning model tailored for Arabic fake-news detection. The suggested method combines advanced natural language processing methods with improvements that are specific to the field to make it easier to find fake news in Arabic texts. Specifically, the model employs Arabic Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (AraBERT), a pre-trained transformer-based model (Nassif et al., 2022), fine-tuned with data augmentation techniques to address class imbalances and enhance detection accuracy. The model’s goal is to use the unique linguistic and semantic aspects of Arabic to find fake news in Arabic media in a strong way.
Innovations and Contributions of This Work:
- (1) Improved Accuracy in Arabic Fake News Detection : FakeNews-TransAug’s targeted fake production improves training datasets by fixing class imbalances and increasing important performance measures like precision, recall, F1-score, and Area under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC-ROC).
- (2) Advancing Interpretability in Language Models : Investigating how FakeNews-TransAug was created, we may learn more about how big language models make and process fake information, which contributes to developing confidence and transparency.
- (3) Addressing Emerging Challenges in Fake news : The model may be changed to deal with more complex types of fake information, such deepfakes and organized disinformation operations, which makes it more useful for current issues.
- (4) Generalization to Other Domains : The methods in FakeNews-TransAug can be used for more than just finding fake news. They can also be used for sentiment analysis, social media mining, and natural language comprehension, which makes it easier to find fake information in various situations.

This study covers a major gap in the research by providing a full and effective way to stop the spread of fake information in Arabic-language digital media by dealing with the specific problems of finding fake news in Arabic.
Related work Traditional machine learning approaches
A lot of research in the past ten years has gone into employing machine learning to find fake news (Karim, Asad & Azam, 2024). Classical classifiers like these were employed as early benchmarks: K-Nearest Neighbours (KNN), Gradient Boosting Machines (GBM), Logistic Regression (LR), Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forest (RF), Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), Naive Bayes (NB), and Decision Trees (DT). These classifiers were usually fed with hand-engineered textual features (like punctuation marks, term counts, and Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TD-IDF)) taken from social media and news posts. Foundational credibility studies on Twitter illustrated this manual-feature paradigm and showed that supervised models over such features can be effective at scale (Castillo, Mendoza & Poblete, 2011; Alruily, 2021; Fouad, Sabbeh & Medhat, 2022).
Deep learning architectures
Supervised learning has become the standard, and it may be divided into two main groups: (i) classical machine learning (ML) pipelines that work with sparse vectors or static embeddings, and (ii) deep-learning methods that learn structures from text and context (Zhou, Wu & Zafarani, 2020; Alruily, 2021; Al-Jalabneh & Safori, 2022). With the advent of deep learning, various architectures have been introduced to capture sequential, semantic, and contextual patterns in news articles (Shen et al., 2023). Recurrent models such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and long short-term memory (LSTMs) have been widely employed to model temporal dependencies in text, while hybrid RNN-based models like CSI integrate article content with propagation and user-level features, outperforming content-only baselines. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and CNN-RNN hybrids have also gained prominence for textual feature extraction and sequence modeling in fake news and rumor classification (Nasir, Khan & Varlamis, 2021; Sorour & Abdelkader, 2022; Khalil, Jarrah & Aldwairi, 2024).
Classical classifiers like these were employed as early benchmarks: Bi-directional LSTMs (Bi-LSTM) and Bi-directional gated recurrent units (GRUs) (Bi-GRU) have been effectively applied to capture bidirectional context (Qandos et al., 2024). Multilayer Perceptrons (MLPs) have been used in comparative setups alongside RNNs, with LSTMs often outperforming them (Alsukhni, 2021); and hybrid CNN + Bi-LSTM or CNN + Bi-GRU models have delivered. Hybrid CNN + Bi-LSTM or CNN + Bi-GRU models have delivered superior accuracies across multiple Arabic datasets (Fouad, Sabbeh & Medhat, 2022; Wotaifi & Dhannoon, 2023). These studies highlight that combining convolutional layers for local feature extraction with recurrent or transformer-based components for sequential/contextual modeling yields robust performance in fake news detection across diverse datasets and domains.
Transformer-based models for arabic
The introduction of transformer-based models marked a significant advancement in Arabic NLP and has been widely adopted in fake news detection research. For example, AraBERT (Antoun, Baly & Hajj, 2020) has been fine-tuned for detecting fake news in Arabic social media content, achieving superior accuracy compared to traditional ML approaches (Alturayeif, Luqman & Ahme, 2022). Similarly, Arabic A Lite Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (ALBERT), a lightweight adaptation of ALBERT for Arabic, has been used in low-resource and computationally constrained settings to classify Arabic news articles with competitive accuracy (Alammary, 2025; Fat’hAlalim et al., 2025). XLM-RoBERTa, a multilingual transformer trained on 100 languages including Arabic, has been applied for cross-lingual fake news detection, enabling transfer of knowledge from high-resource languages to Arabic and showing strong performance in mixed-language datasets (Haraty & Chehade, 2025; Mohamed, 2025). Across multiple studies, transformer-based models such as AraBERT, MARBERT, and jointBERT have consistently outperformed earlier CNN- or RNN-based models in Arabic fake news detection tasks (Alqurashi et al., 2021; Nassif et al., 2022; Alturayeif, Luqman & Ahme, 2022).
Multimodal fake news detection
A parallel line of research incorporates multimodality by jointly using text and images, motivated by the prevalence and persuasive impact of visual content on social media. Early multimodal systems such as TI-CNN processed text and image streams in tandem, projecting both modalities into a unified feature space within a convolutional model (Yang et al., 2018; Galli et al., 2022). More advanced algorithms look at how similar or different text and images are and how consistent they are across different types of media. An example of this is Similarity-Aware Multi-modal Fake News Detection (SAFE/MCNN), which compare features from both modalities to detect incoherence indicative of deception (Zhou, Wu & Zafarani, 2020, May). Recent methods use transformer encoders and multilevel attention models to find deeper semantic relationships between different types of data. The ETMA approach, for instance, uses a transformer-based multilevel attention architecture to combine text and images in a way that works well (Yadav et al., 2023; Ni’mah et al., 2025). Survey studies consistently affirm the superiority of well-designed multimodal fusion over unimodal systems (Zidan et al., 2025).
Class imbalance handling in fake news detection
A pervasive issue in fake news detection is class imbalance, where real news samples typically vastly outnumber fake news examples, leading to classifiers biased toward the majority class and reduced recall for the minority class. Systematic reviews highlight that inadequate handling of class imbalance often results in misleading performance when accuracy is relied upon in skewed datasets (Chen et al., 2024; Aljohani, 2024). Traditional resampling techniques such as Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE), while common, can introduce noise, cause overfitting, or distort class distributions (Burnaev, Erofeev & Papanov, 2015). More advanced methods include latent-space resampling ( e.g ., variational autoencoders), contrastive learning models, and raw-text augmentation techniques like Easy Data Augmentation (EDA) and An Easier Data Augmentation (AEDA) (Bhattacharjee, Maity & Chatterjee, 2022). These strategies show promise but may degrade semantic integrity or cause distributional shifts when used with pre-trained transformers (Zaiton & Alansary, 2025).
Research gap and motivation
Despite significant advancements in fake news detection, few studies have examined class imbalance in Arabic datasets using representation-aware augmentation and transformer-based designs. Most current methods use generic oversampling or basic textual augmentation, which don’t capture the language richness, morphological complexity, and contextual intricacies of Arabic. Moreover, the integration of augmentation strategies specifically designed for rumor-like and fake news-specific language patterns remains underexplored. Motivated by these gaps, this work proposes FakeNews-TransAug, a transformer-based model leveraging AraBERT with domain-tailored prompt engineering for advanced data augmentation, combined with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques such as Quantized Low-Rank Adaptation (QLoRA), BitFit, and Prefix-Tuning, to effectively mitigate class imbalance while enhancing contextual and semantic modeling in Arabic fake news detection.
Methodology Model overview (FakeNews-TransAug architecture)
To address the challenges discussed in the previous sections, this article introduces the FakeNews-TransAug model an Arabic fake news augmentation and detection approach tailored to the JoFakeNews dataset. The model leverages the capabilities of pre-trained Arabic transformer-based models, specifically AraBERT, fine-tuned using parameter-efficient adaptation techniques (QLoRA, BitFit, and Prefix-Tuning) to capture Arabic fake news writing styles, thematic patterns, and semantic nuances found in real-world digital media. This integration enables both accurate classification and the generation of realistic synthetic samples for data augmentation.
AraBERT, pre-trained on large-scale Arabic datasets, is specifically effective for Arabic NLP due to its ability to capture deep contextual relationships between tokens, even in morphologically complex forms. Its architecture built upon stacked transformer encoder layers and multi-head self-attention combined with subword tokenization enables robust handling of root-based derivations, affixes, and orthographic variations. By integrating AraBERT into the FakeNews-TransAug model, we exploit its strong semantic representation capabilities to improve classification accuracy and generate high-quality synthetic fake news samples, thereby addressing both detection and data imbalance in multi-level classification scenarios.
Fine-tuning is guided by prompt engineering, ensuring that generated samples align with authentic Arabic fake news characteristics while maintaining thematic and stylistic diversity. The core architecture is shown in Fig. 1.

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning
The FakeNews-TransAug model harnesses the semantic comprehension, analysis, and generation capabilities of Arabic-specific transformer models (AraBERT) in combination with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques, namely:
- QLoRA to reduce memory usage while adapting low-rank matrices within the transformer layers.
- BitFit to update only bias terms, further minimizing trainable parameters while retaining strong adaptation capability.
- Prefix-Tuning to prepend learnable prefix vectors in attention layers, steering the model towards Arabic fake news patterns without altering the main weights.

This generative–augmentative approach enables the creation of synthetic fake news samples that mimic the tone, style, and thematic structure of genuine Arabic fake news. Incorporating these samples into training mitigates the pervasive class imbalance problem where real news significantly outnumbers fake news by enriching the representation of minority (fake) categories and less frequent subcategories. By integrating this generated content into the training process, FakeNews-TransAug improves both recall on minority classes and the model’s ability to detect subtle, AI-crafted fake news in multi-level classification tasks.
In addition, exposure to augmented, high-variance samples strengthen the model’s ability to recognize subtle and sophisticated fake news, including content generated by advanced generative tools. This dual functionality generation for augmentation and enhanced detection positions FakeNews-TransAug as a comprehensive model for tackling Arabic fake news in both binary and multi-level classification scenarios.

Our preprocessing pipeline is built to deal with the special problems that Arabic language presents, like diacritic variation, morphological richness, and diglossia, while yet keeping the meaning of the text intact. The steps include:
- (1) Normalization: Unifying different forms of Arabic letters ( e.g ., converting “أ“, ”إ“, ”آ” to “ا”), removing Tatweel (ــ), and standardizing Hamza forms.
- (2) Morphological light stemming: Using Arabic-specific light stemming rules (like Larkey’s Light10 stemmer) to get rid of frequent affixes without changing the meaning of the root, making sure that the samples that are made still have a natural flow.
- (3) Stop-word and noise removal: Eliminating high-frequency functional words ( e.g ., “من“, ”على“, ”في”) and removing URLs, emojis, and repeated characters.

Following normalization and light stemming, we apply AraBERT-based contextual embeddings to extract semantically salient tokens. The token salience is determined by combining contextual embedding similarity with document-level term importance. The most informative tokens are ranked and integrated into controlled prompt templates for generative fine-tuning.

Data augmentation with FakeNews-TransAug
To address dataset imbalance, particularly in underrepresented fake news subcategories, the FakeNews-TransAug model integrates a controlled data augmentation pipeline designed to generate realistic and linguistically coherent Arabic fake news samples. This process enriches the diversity of the dataset while preserving semantic fidelity and the stylistic characteristics of real fake news.
Augmentation Steps:
- 1. Keyword Extraction from Embeddings
- Using AraBERT contextual embeddings, we identify the most semantically salient tokens from existing fake news samples.
- Token importance is calculated based on embedding similarity scores and positional relevance within the document.


- 2. Prompt Reconstruction
- Extracted keywords are integrated into structured prompt templates to ensure that the generated samples are topically aligned.
- Prompts are tailored to mimic real-world fake news styles while maintaining linguistic variety.


- 3. Controlled Fake News Generation
- The fine-tuned FakeNews-TransAug model, adapted using QLoRA, BitFit, and Prefix-Tuning, generates new samples guided by the reconstructed prompts.
- Generated text inherits the linguistic richness of Arabic, including narrative flow, idiomatic expressions, and domain-specific terminology.


- 4. Integration into Training Data
- Newly generated samples are reviewed for quality, diversity, and topical relevance before being merged with the original dataset.
- This augmented dataset enhances model training, improving recall on minority classes and robustness against AI-crafted fake news.

Data validation and leakage prevention
A two-stage validation technique was used to make sure that the false news samples were real and varied.
- (1) Automatic filtering: A cosine-similarity threshold of over 0.90 was utilised to automatically identify and remove sentences that exhibited high similarity, hence preventing data leakage.
- (2) Manual verification: two Arabic-language annotators manually evaluated a random subset of 500 augmented samples to assess semantic plausibility and linguistic diversity. Only augmented texts that passed both validation stages were retained for model training.

This process made sure that all of the data that was created was real, varied, and not repeated, which made the augmented dataset more reliable.
Cross-validation setup
In order to further guarantee the stability and resilience of the suggested FakeNews-TransAug system, the experimental design included a cross-validation process. To test the model’s generalization over various data partitions, a 5-fold cross-validation approach was employed in addition to the conventional 80:20 train–test split.
The JoFakeNews dataset was split into five equal sections at random during this process. Four subsets were used for training and the remaining subset was used as the validation set for each fold. To ensure that each data segment was used once for validation, this process was carried out five times. The preprocessing, augmentation, and PEFT-based fine-tuning parameters outlined in the preceding subsections were all used in the cross-validation runs.
The FakeNews-TransAug model is stable under various sampling settings, as seen by the results’ consistent performance with minimal variance over the five folds. This extra assessment phase strengthens the evidence of the model’s resilience in imbalanced fake-news situations in the actual world and supplements the main train-test findings.
Advancements and impact
The FakeNews-TransAug model, enhanced by AraBERT and advanced data augmentation techniques, provides a comprehensive solution for Arabic fake news detection and generation. Its key contributions are:
- - The model employs controlled data augmentation to enrich underrepresented fake news subcategories, resulting in a more balanced dataset and reduced classification bias.
- - Using AraBERT’s rich awareness of context lets the model accurately capture Arabic-specific language patterns, morphological differences, and stylistic differences.
- - Fine-tuning AraBERT with fewer parameters makes the model more accurate and reliable for both binary and multi-level classification problems.

This method sets a new standard for finding fake news in Arabic by combining generative augmentation with strong detection abilities. The system may grow, change, and work well to deal with the changing world of fake information in Arabic digital media.
Experiments Dataset
For the experiments and evaluations in this study, we utilized the JoFakeNews dataset, a large-scale, manually annotated Arabic fake news dataset specifically collected from the verified Facebook pages of 12 Jordanian news agencies. The dataset was designed by the researchers themselves to support multi-level, multi-label classification, containing annotations for Main Category (22 classes), Subcategory (75 classes), and Fake/Real labels. The experiments were conducted using the JoFakeNews dataset; evaluation on additional corpora is planned for future work.
The dataset contained 134,160 news posts that were produced between 2023 and 2024. After going through tight filtering (removing short, non-Arabic, or duplicate postings) and advanced text pre-processing (normalization, stemming, and stop-word removal), the final dataset had 50,000 high-quality news items). Annotation was performed in two stages:
- (1) Manual labeling by four trained annotators.
- (2) Expert validation by two domain specialists to ensure labeling accuracy and consistency.

The dataset includes not only clean textual content but also Part-of-Speech (POS) tags and emotional indicators, which were later integrated with AraBERT semantic embeddings to create a hybrid 894-dimensional feature representation. Table 2 present the Statistics of the JoFakeNews dataset.

Setup
The JoFakeNews dataset was split into training and testing sets using an 80:20 ratio, ensuring adequate data for both model training and evaluation while maintaining representativeness across all main categories, subcategories, and Fake/Real labels. All experiments were conducted in Google Colab Pro using an NVIDIA T4 GPU (16 GB VRAM) and an Intel Xeon CPU environment. The FakeNews-TransAug model was implemented with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques QLoRA, BitFit, and Prefix-Tuning applied to the AraBERT model.
The experimental setup was as follows:
- Batch size: 8
- Optimizer: Adam
- Initial learning rate: 1 × 10 −5
- Weight decay: 1 × 10 −4
- Dropout rate: 0.5 after each fully connected layer
- Prefix prompt length (Prefix-Tuning): 128
- Training epochs: up to 50 with early stopping based on validation loss

To ensure fairness in evaluation, no data augmentation was applied to the test set.

Baselines
To ensure a fair and comprehensive evaluation of the proposed FakeNews-TransAug model, we compared its performance with several strong Arabic NLP baselines widely used in text classification and fake news detection:
- AraBERT : A model built on transformers that was trained on enormous amounts of Arabic text. It understands the complicated morphology and syntax of Arabic and has done well on a number of Arabic NLP tasks, including as sentiment analysis and text classification.


- Arabic ALBERT: A lightweight variant of BERT optimized for efficiency through parameter sharing and factorized embeddings. It reduces memory footprint and computational cost while maintaining competitive accuracy in Arabic text classification tasks.
- XLM-RoBERTa: A multilingual transformer model that was trained on a lot of Common Crawl data in 100 languages, including Arabic. It uses cross-lingual knowledge transmission and works well in places with few resources and many languages.
- FakeNews-TransAug (Proposed Model): An enhanced Arabic fake news detection model that integrates parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques such as QLoRA, BitFit, and Prefix-Tuning with the AraBERT backbone. It incorporates advanced data augmentation strategies to improve generalization and robustness, particularly in multi-level, multi-label classification of Arabic fake news.

For fairness in evaluation, all baselines and the proposed model were trained and tested on the same JoFakeNews dataset without any augmentation applied to the test set.

Computational efficiency
Along with the performance indicators, it’s also important to see how well the suggested framework works for computing. We did all of the studies on an NVIDIA T4 GPU with 16 GB of VRAM, using Python 3.10 and PyTorch 2.1. It took around 2.8 h to train FakeNews-TransAug, but it took about 4.5 h to fine-tune AraBERT completely. It took roughly 58 milliseconds for each sample to make an inference, and the most memory used was 5.3 GB, which is far less than the 7.8 GB needed for comprehensive fine-tuning. To make sure that the results could be repeated, all iterations used the same random seed (42). These findings show that the parameter-efficient fine-tuning setup (QLoRA, BitFit, Prefix-Tuning) saves time and money during training while still getting good accuracy and F1-scores. So, the FakeNews-TransAug method is a cheap, simple, and scalable solution to find bogus news in Arabic in the real world.
Conclusion and future work Conclusion
This research presented FakeNews-TransAug, an advanced Arabic fake news detection model developed to address two primary challenges: dataset imbalance and the linguistic complexity of the Arabic language. The model combines AraBERT, a transformer-based language model pre-trained on large-scale Arabic dataset, with sophisticated data augmentation techniques to generate realistic and diverse synthetic samples. These enhancements improve the model’s effectiveness in detecting fake news within underrepresented categories and enhance its generalization to unseen data. Extensive experiments on the JoFakeNews dataset demonstrated that FakeNews-TransAug outperforms strong baseline models. It has the highest AUC-ROC score of 90% of all the methods tested, as well as an accuracy of 86.5%, a precision of 74%, a recall of 62%, and an F1-score of %68. These findings suggest that the model is strong across a number of different categories and strikes a fair mix between recall and precision.
This article addresses a critical deficiency in Arabic false news detection by offering a scalable, domain-specific, and linguistically informed approach. The suggested methodology sets a new standard for performance and gives researchers a strong base to build on when they try to fight fake news in Arabic digital media. The suggested FakeNews-TransAug framework is a scalable and resource-efficient model for finding Arabic fake news. It directly addresses class imbalance through prompt-engineered data augmentation and parameter-efficient fine-tuning. This sets a standard for future Arabic NLP applications.
Research potentials
The FakeNews-TransAug model makes significant contributions to combating Arabic fake news and expands the scope of research into fake news. Using precise event tuning and advanced data techniques, it can generate examples of fake news that appear authentic, mimicking the style, vocabulary, and storytelling often used in Arabic misleading articles. This makes it far more effective than simple techniques such as word mixing or adding, which rarely produce truly diverse or convincing examples. The model’s capacity to find phoney information in real-life situations gets better when it learns and copies the real writing style of fake material. It can do more than only find fake news; it can also be used for fact-checking, sentiment analysis, and keeping track of certain topics. This makes it a useful tool for anyone who wants to learn about and fight fake news online.
Ethical considerations
The FakeNews-TransAug framework was created only for research and academic objectives to make it easier to find bogus news in Arabic. The model creates fake news samples during augmentation, but these samples are solely used to balance the dataset and are not meant to be shared with the public or utilised in the real world.
The experimental pipeline keeps all created data internal and automatically filters it to keep out dangerous, biassed, or misleading content.
We realise that utilising language models that can make false text is wrong, and we firmly support responsible AI use. So, any usage or sharing of the model must follow rules that stop people from using it to make or propagate false information.
Future work
The FakeNews-TransAug model did a good job of finding bogus news in Arabic, but there is still a lot more to learn. For example, it hasn’t been tested on big datasets or datasets with a lot of different types of data. Testing it on collections with more Arabic dialects and in more areas, such politics, health, and sports, would give us a better idea of how it works in the real world. We want to do a detailed linguistic and interpretability study using attention-map visualizations to learn more about how the model picks up on syntactic and semantic cues in Arabic literature. These kinds of analyses could make model predictions more open and trustworthy.
Another possible path is to change this framework such that it works with additional low-resource languages that are fundamentally similar to Arabic. Future studies might also include multimodal elements like photos, brief video clips, and sharing-pattern metadata, since bogus news is showing up more and more in media other than text. To stay up with new ways of spreading false information, especially with the rise of AI-generated material, the model will need to be constantly fine-tuned.
As a limitation, this study evaluated the proposed FakeNews-TransAug framework exclusively on the JoFakeNews dataset. This dataset offers a robust and equitable basis for Arabic fake-news research; however, subsequent investigations will broaden the evaluation to include other publicly accessible corpora, such as AFND, ArCOV-19, and multilingual fake-news collections, to determine cross-domain and cross-dialect generalizability.
