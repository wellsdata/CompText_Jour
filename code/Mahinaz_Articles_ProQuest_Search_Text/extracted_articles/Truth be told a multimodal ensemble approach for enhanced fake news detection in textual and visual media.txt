Introduction
Amidst the alarming spread of false information, accelerated by the advent of various social media platforms and the increasing number of individuals relying on them to obtain news information, the challenge of the spread of fake news has become particularly salient. In response, a plethora of scholarly endeavors is currently focused on developing different strategies to detect and mitigate the dissemination of misinformation within news articles. fake news detection (FND), which encompasses the methodologies to identify such misinformation, predominantly leverages statistical and word-based characteristics[1, 8, 9, 10, 11–12]. For instance, Rashkin et al. [15] utilize adverbs and their respective classifications to discern fraudulent information inside textual content. In contrast, Nakashole et al. [13] train conventional models by employing both the textual content and its corresponding source as input features. More recently, Gupta et al. [6] have acknowledged the significance of incorporating other modalities while placing greater emphasis on the use of context-aware approaches. Today, the efficacy of exclusively textual-based techniques has diminished due to three distinct factors.
The predominant determinant persists as the limited duration of contemporary news platforms. In a recent study conducted by Shim et al. [21], it was found that Twitter continues to be a prominent platform for the dissemination of false information despite the limitation of 150 characters. The optimization of artificial intelligence algorithms to evaluate the precision of tweets can pose some obstacles. Furthermore, the proliferation of deliberate and purposeful dissemination of false information has resulted in a convergence of writing styles between fraudulent news items and authentic news sources. The diminished distance between the two entities also amplifies the intricacy of the undertaking. Ultimately, the proliferation of interactive information, such as Graphics Interchange Formats (GIFs), videos, and photographs, has led to significant dissemination of fake news through alternative channels. According to [21], the reliance on text alone as a means of communication has made it increasingly challenging to discern information accurately without the use of supplementary modalities.
In recent times, there has been a growing interest in multimodal approaches to fake news detection for many reasons. In their study, Shim et al. [21] utilized the inclusion of hyperlinks within tweets and articles as an independent modality to enhance the contextual understanding of their classifier. Additionally, they demonstrated enhanced efficacy in comparison to traditional text-only approaches. Moreover, Singhal et al. [24] have documented a significant performance improvement when including photographs as a modality for fake news detection activities. Nevertheless, none of the aforementioned studies have endeavored to construct a comparison analysis across pre-trained image models, particularly in terms of the relationship between the number of training parameters and their performance. This analysis is crucial for determining the most suitable model for fake news detection tasks.
Moreover, the majority of studies have mostly focused on investigating the use of the widely used BERT text model for the purpose of analyzing lexical aspects. In addition, there is a limited body of literature that has delved into the use of ensemble models specifically for the text content [16]. Previous studies have demonstrated that the combination of text models through ensembling can result in enhanced performance [3, 29]. While a few recent studies have begun exploring approaches to decrease the processing speed of fake news detection (False Negative Detection) systems, the majority of the existing literature has not prioritized this aspect. Metrics like the total amount of time spent training haven’t been reported on very often in these studies [3].
Taking inspiration from the aforementioned context, the current study introduces an innovative architecture for the detection of multimodal fake news. This design utilizes a stacked ensemble approach and incorporates feature-level fusion. Moreover, the study shows a combination of two deep learning models, namely SBERT and DeBERT, to extract textual features. The decision-level fusion of the ensemble is achieved through the application of equal weightage averaging. Ultimately, the fusion of image and text modalities occurs at the decision level through the process of equal weightage averaging. This study presents a new approach to extracting Fine-grained fake news detection (fake news detection) that incorporates both picture and text modalities, with the objective of enhancing the efficiency of the task.
The paper’s noteworthy contributions can be succinctly summarised as follows: A successful presentation and testing of a deep learning-based ensemble multimodal architecture for fake news detection has been conducted. A comparative analysis is conducted on two datasets to determine the optimal pre-trained picture model that balances accuracy and efficiency. In this study, the combination of two foundation text models, namely DeBERT and SBERT, is used. In general, the work presented demonstrates a higher level of speed compared to recent contemporaries while also obtaining superior performance.
Related work
In an effort to address the aforementioned concern, several novel multimodal frameworks have been presented with the objective of effectively integrating multimodal information through interactive means. Singhal et al. [24] developed a novel multimodal framework called SpotFake with the primary objective of identifying and recognizing instances of fake news. The proposed approach utilizes BERT to acquire textual characteristics while employing a pre-trained VGG-19 model built on the ImageNet dataset to extract image features. Within this framework, the authors exclusively examine textual and visual aspects to ascertain the authenticity of news. The framework’s ability to detect bogus news is enhanced by the exclusion of other subtasks, thereby ensuring optimal performance. However, this approach exclusively employs a fusion of BERT and VGG models without addressing the incorporation of inter-modality features. In addition, Zhou et al. [34] introduced the Similarity-Aware fake news detection approach, which aims to directly leverage picture features and incorporate multimodal information from an alternative standpoint. The SAFE framework utilizes an image description model to convert the image into a textual representation. This textual representation is then combined with the news content to train a text-based classifier. Throughout this procedure, the authors held the belief that the SAFE method effectively integrates the interdependencies among the derived features across different modalities, hence enhancing overall performance. Nevertheless, this approach solely concentrates on the correlation between textual content and accompanying visuals inside a given news article, disregarding any potential connections between input news articles. Zhang et al. [32] developed a multimodal model called BTIC, which is based on BERT and incorporates contrasting learning. The proposed framework utilizes a contrastive learning strategy to capture the representations of textual and visual information derived from inaccurate news items pertaining to a certain subject. The BTIC framework incorporates the use of picture patches, as facilitated by the visual transformer, to produce image characteristics. This approach aims to comprehensively capture the interplay between text and visuals. Nevertheless, the efficacy of employing image patches in the detection of fake news remains untested, and the superiority of this approach has yet to be substantiated. In addition, it should be noted that none of the aforementioned solutions effectively address the issue of limited data size. In recent times, there has been a surge in the examination of leveraging visual elements and textual context, as evidenced by the focus of this study. In their study, Yang et al. [30] employed a methodology to uncover concealed patterns within the textual and visual components of fabricated news. This involved the extraction of latent characteristics through the utilization of several convolutional layers. The study’s design put both explicit and hidden data into a single feature space, and it was trained using both text and images at the same time. A precision rate of 92.20% was attained when evaluating a dataset specifically centered on pre-trained news. In contrast, Wang et al. [27] introduced a comprehensive framework of adversarial neural networks. This framework aims to extract event-invariant features, which may be utilized to enhance the identification of fabricated news pertaining to recently emerged events. The research comprises a multimodal feature extractor that combines language and visual data. Furthermore, the utilization of a counterfeit news detection system is implemented in order to obtain distinguishable representations. Moreover, the utilization of an event discriminator is employed to exclude elements that are peculiar to a certain event while retaining the common features that are shared across multiple events. Prior research has provided evidence of the capabilities of frameworks that employ attention-based cross-modal frameworks. The aforementioned frameworks have demonstrated favorable results through two approaches: improving the feature representations of two distinct modalities [25], or resolving the misclassification problem caused by the disparity between these features [2]. Driven by the aforementioned observations, this study presents an innovative multimodal architecture based on stacked ensembles that enhance the efficiency of fake news identification. To improve performance in the realm of visual modality, a study was conducted on 18 models using unimodal analysis techniques, with a focus on minimizing processing time. The ensemble for text analysis is constructed by carefully selecting pre-trained word embedding models, with the objective of minimizing the number of parameters without compromising accuracy. Feature-level fusion is a technique employed to integrate many modalities and layered text models.

Text embedding models: DeBERT and SBERT
In order to utilize textual data effectively, word vector representations were generated by the application of DeBERT [18] and SBERT [26]. DeBERTa introduces advancements to the BERT and RoBERTa models through the utilization of two innovative techniques. Firstly, it employs a disentangled attention mechanism, wherein each word is represented by two vectors that encode its content and position. The attention weights between words are then computed using disentangled matrices based on their content and relative positions. Secondly, DeBERTa replaces the output softmax layer with an enhanced mask decoder, which enhances the model’s ability to predict masked tokens during pre-training.

Ensemble approach
In this approach, the final classification outcome, namely ”real” or ”fake,” is derived by utilizing the model prediction vectors produced from inference on the news titles for several models. The primary rationale for employing an ensemble of diverse fine-tuned pre-trained language models is to leverage the knowledge acquired by each model from the specific dataset on which it was trained. In the context of the FakeNewsNet dataset, an additional prediction vector is derived by applying SBERT and DeBERT to the news body. This vector is then attached to the current feature set. All the features utilized in this study were derived solely from the unprocessed textual data. In order to mitigate the limits of an individual model, the utilization of an ensemble method might prove advantageous, particularly when applied to a group of models that exhibit similar levels of performance. Two approaches, namely soft voting and hard voting, have been explored in our experiments, as depicted in figure 1.

Experiments and analysis
Dataset descriptions
The Twitter MediaEval Dataset was compiled as a component of the ’2015 Verifying Multimedia Use Challenge’ organized by MediaEval [17]. The objective of the exercise was to determine if the audio-visual elements accompanying a tweet accurately depict the reality as claimed by the tweet. The dataset consists of a total of 17,000 distinct tweets and their corresponding photos, which were gathered in relation to various globally recognized events or news articles. The dataset is partitioned into two distinct subsets: the development set, consisting of 9000 tweets containing false news and 6000 tweets containing true news; and the test set, comprising 9000 tweets with fake news and 6000 tweets with genuine news, totaling 2000 tweets. The tweets have been through a process of verification and classification, wherein they have been systematically examined and categorized as either ’genuine’ or ’false’ based on a thorough cross-referencing of web sources, including articles and blogs. An authentic Twitter is characterized by the presence of an accompanying image that faithfully represents the event mentioned in the tweet. Conversely, a counterfeit tweet is characterized by the inclusion of photos that are not relevant to the event described in the tweet. In the present investigation, data points containing just unimodal information were omitted from the dataset. Furthermore, the analysis removed data points that contained movies and GIFs.
The second dataset used in this study is the Weibo dataset [7]. The Weibo NER dataset encompasses messages containing false rumors collected from the social network, Sina Weibo, over the period from May 2012 to January 2016. The dataset in question comprises a variety of multimedia content, including photos and Graphics Interchange Format (GIF) files. It is worth noting that all posts within this dataset have undergone verification via Weibo’s official rumor debunking mechanism. While Weibo adheres to the microblogging framework pioneered by Twitter, it is important to note that there exist notable divergences between these two platforms [7]. Initially, it should be noted that many linguistic features commonly examined in English tweets, like repetition of letters, case sensitivity and elongation of words, are not applicable to the Chinese language employed on the Weibo platform. Furthermore, there is a distinction in the categories of microblogs that are retweeted on different platforms, such as Twitter. The datasets utilized in this study exhibit a diverse range, hence enhancing the evaluation of the resilience of the suggested framework.

Conclusion and future work
The study effectively introduced a very effective multimodal system for the detection of fake news. This framework leverages DeBERT and SBERT models to extract contextual information from textual data, while RasNet50 is employed to analyse the accompanying images. Moreover, it revealed the viability of integrating textual embeddings and feature-level fusion across modalities. The proposed framework effectively reduces the processing requirements for the task and lessens the overall time required for decision-making, while yet maintaining a high level of accuracy. The assessment of the proposed approach was carried out by evaluating its performance using measures such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score. The study was conducted using two commonly public datasets. The pipeline that was suggested achieved an accuracy of 87.40% on the Twitter dataset and 88.34% on the Weibo dataset. The findings of this study underscore the efficacy of employing a multimodal strategy and employing basic deep neural networks (DNNs) in conjunction with tailored processing models for each modality, as opposed to relying on traditional classifiers, adversarial networks, or computationally intensive DNN models like long short-term memory (LSTM) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs).
While the framework presented in this study demonstrates superior performance compared to previous research, a logical next step would be to integrate user metadata into the proposed pipeline. This integration would enhance the ability to differentiate between genuine and false information, as well as identify accounts that are more likely to engage in the spread of misinformation. The task at hand has proven to be challenging as a result of the scarcity of datasets that encompass the necessary resources for extracting all pertinent features. One potentially fruitful avenue of research involves the development of an extensive and expansive benchmark dataset for false news. This dataset would encompass a wide range of linguistic and visual characteristics, as well as include accompanying user profiles and metadata. Moreover, it would be worthwhile to explore the utilisation of part-of-speech tagging and topic modelling principles. Textual semantics are taken into consideration and can be utilised for the purpose of acquiring the underlying stance from various themes. This, in turn, assists in the extraction of features and the refinement of classification models. Finally, it is worth noting that the integration of NLP techniques with information retrieval and reinforcement learning has been explored in existing research, which predominantly emphasises supervised learning methodologies. The extent to which semi-supervised, unsupervised, and hybrid techniques have been sufficiently evaluated in terms of their accuracy remains insufficient.
