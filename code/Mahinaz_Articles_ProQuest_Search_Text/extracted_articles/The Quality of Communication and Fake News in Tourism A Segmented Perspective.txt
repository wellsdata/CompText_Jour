Headnote Abstract
This manuscript aims to contextualize the phenomenon of fake news, its concepts and implications, in the context of tourism activity. The relationship between the concepts is still little explored in the literature, which culminated in the realization of an illustrative study of practical cases with the theory presented by Fedeli (2019). The study of consumer behavior has gained particular interest in recent years in multiple contexts, notably with the development of the digital age. Niche marketing has been with us for some time, what is new is the increasing diversity of markets, and the advanced technologies that allow new approaches to marketing and the deterioration of large companies and their traditional approaches to marketingTourism is a good example, assuming itself as a multifaceted and geographically complex activity, and tourism increasingly originates new (and different) market segments with different individual interests. For this exploratory research a selection of news considered emblematic that connect the fake news with tourism, a methodology also suitable in the study by Delmazo & Valente (2018). Thus, we obtained insights, reflections and indications for future studies on the analysis of news portrayed within three spheres: ethics, marketing and impact on tourist perception and behavior.
Keywords: fake news; tourism; news mapping; niche marketing.
1.Introduction
With increasing competitiveness, advancing technologies and increasing consumer choice, influence and intervention, it is necessary to focus on new marketing strategies aimed at creating profitable, long-term relationships that target consumer loyalty. The concepts of traditional marketing, based on product and service characteristics, are no longer sufficient in the highly competitive market in which we live. Tourism as an economic and social phenomenon is able to contribute significantly to a society. Over the years, the strength of tourism activity and the interest in investing in the sector for its potentiality becomes evident. However, like every activity, tourism also suffers from the constant social change. Clearly the paradigms at the time when tourism began during the European grand tour or petit tour (Barbosa, 2002) are different from those of the 21st century. Therefore, the evolution of the concept, in view of market needs and desires, for other purposes or even the influence of macro environment factors, requires constant studies to maintain the activity. Today fake news is spread more often and the repercussion can have serious consequences. It is as if we are in a period of ascension, due to the level of tools we have for news production, and the realization that such news sometimes seems to have. Of course, the spread of this kind of news is part of a game of interest, whatever the area to which it relates: sales, marketing, politics and so on. Tourism is yet another sector hit by the spread of fake news for different purposes. Whether to create false characteristics of the destination or to increase the rating of some tourism equipment, fake news is a reality in this sector, which encourages studies like this to alert the appropriate stakeholders. To find a suitable solution to the fake news problem, you need to discuss who is behind this content (either creation or distribution), as well as the main associated motivation. This subject reveals a huge field of research and configures a current and relevant aspect of concern regarding tourism. Therefore, this article aims to contextualize the phenomenon of fake news, its concepts and implications, and it is key to present news of emblematic facts that connect fake news with tourism, a methodology also suitable in the study by Delmazo & Valente (2018). The selection of news in this area was conducted within three spheres of the subject proposed by Fedeli (2019), they are: ethics, marketing and the impact of perception and behavior. Importantly, there is still little collection that correlates tourism and fake news. This fact allows that while there is difficulty in research, it is a factor that encourages this study and justifies it as preliminary and exploratory, as it is a field still little explored (Fedeli, 2019). From an interdisciplinary perspective, the present study aims to gather inputs from the area of communication and tourism, specifically, some research guidelines will be addressed and the next steps for research will be discussed.
2.Fake News Perspectives and Quality of Communication
The fake news phenomenon is in evidence today due to the proportions of its consequences in large sectors such as tourism and politics (Allcott & Gentzkow, 2017; Fedeli, 2019). This phenomenon can be defined as "news articles that are intentionally and verifiably false, and could mislead readers" (Allcott & Gentzkow, 2017, p. 213), which is a broad view of the concept linked to all areas that fake news reach. In Portugal, a survey conducted by EUROBAROMETER in late 2018 found that only 46% of the population feels able to identify false news (Alvarez, 2019). Which characterizes that the majority of the sample is not prepared to detect false news. Thus, this corroborates the thinking of Kanoh (2018) who argues that there is a social problem linked to fake news, and therefore there is a significant importance in educating the population on identifying such fraudulent news. Society is increasingly agile and aware of the news, which gives rise to a cyber-cultural urgency (Barbosa, 2017). The speed of information and globalization, together with internet access, the expansion of social networks and websites are factors that affect (increasingly) tourism. Brites et al. (2018) show that a major problem in the context of fake news is the lack of verification of news before it is released, which consequently influences the delivery of false news. However, the cause mentioned matches the moment of immediacy / speed either to post or share that the current society lives. This age of connectivity, equally prevalent in marketing, results from the greater interaction of people on social networks, that is, consumers increasingly post their trips in real time or share their experience with some tourist equipment, with a set of opinions can influence family, friends and others (Kotler, et al., 2017; Ferreira et al., 2018). This may be one of the reasons that justify the relevance of review sites, as reading a common denominator among the testimonials can be a decision-making factor for an individual. Consumers' opinion about the product / experience they are going to consume is increasingly based on previous experiences or opinions they get from various sources. It is important for companies to be able to generate the best possible feedback on their services so that the customer is tempted to come back and recommend, which usually results in repeated purchases and new customers (Silva & Sousa, 2018). But there is controversy regarding the variability of tourism, which implies a unique experience for individuals, especially because tourism is a complex activity (Sousa & Simöes, 2010). Despite the large sphere of fake news media such as newspapers, TV channels and radio, according to Zhang & Ghorbani (2019) there is a greater ease of fake news being spread over the internet. This is understandable by the fact that internet tools provide relatively greater ease for manipulating news, and the authorship of the individual who produces such misinformation is easier to conceal. The spread of fraudulent news on social networks seems to be a key point in this new paradigm, which characterizes a dilemma between this phenomenon and freedom of expression (Delmazo & Valente, 2018; Tandoc Jr et al., 2018). In 2015, for example, a fake Facebook page was created that ran a paid-for Disney travel promotion. The publication that had over 60,000 likes and over 80,000 shares, was unmasked by the real Disney. Thus, in this study, the internet is considered a key database for the search for fake news in tourism.
3.Niche Marketing and Tourism Perspective
The study of consumer behavior has gained particular interest in recent years in multiple contexts, notably with the development of the digital age (Pinto da Silva et al., 2019). Tourism is a good example, assuming itself as a multifaceted and geographically complex activity, and tourism increasingly originates new (and different) market segments with different individual interests (Sousa & Simöes, 2010). These segments are characterized by the motivations that make them travel and, in turn, by the differentiation of the existing offer (Sousa, Malheiro & Veloso, 2019). According to Robinson and Novelli (2005) niche tourism has emerged in recent decades and is strongly associated with niche marketing theory. On the other hand Dalgic and Leeuw (1994) state that niche marketing has been with us for some time, what is new is the increasing diversity of markets, and the advanced technologies that allow new approaches to marketing and the deterioration of large companies and their traditional approaches to marketing. Focused marketing, focused marketing, and micromarketing are other terms used interchangeably with niche marketing. Although they have similar characteristics, they differ to some extent (Dalgic & Leeuw, 1994). The term niche, in a marketing perspective, refers to two key interrelated ideas: that there is a place in the market for the product, and that there is an audience for that same product. This refers to a specific product capable of keeping up with the needs of a specific market segment. Therefore, one should not look at the market in a simplistic and homogeneous way, since it represents a group of individuals with specific characteristics and needs (figure 1).
A "niche market" can be viewed as a narrower group, where individuals are identified based on the same type of needs or wants (Robinson & Novelli, 2005). Niche tourism is a way of providing a connection between the tourist's dreams, desires, imaginations and experience (Robinson & Novelli, 2005). Due to stiff competition, only the strongest survive, and niche marketing can help companies stay among the healthiest survivors. It is possible, in the context of macro niches, to identify a wide variety of micro niches that arise from the most attractive features, places of destination and specific interests (Sousa, 2014). In this way, niche markets emerge as a response to the growing demand for sophisticated and specialized tourism. According to Yang and Wall (2009), for instance, ethnic tourism is employed by many countries to facilitate economic and cultural development and to assist in heritage preservation. Ethnicity has been increasingly promoted to attract tourists and to generate income and foreign exchange for ethnic communities and the countries in which they are located (Jamison, 1999; Sousa & Rodrigues, 2019). Many countries take advantage of their cultural diversity and employ ethnic tourism to stimulate local economic development (Henderson, 2003).
4.Methodology
This article is characterized by an exploratory study which the research process will be based on an inductive model because it is a qualitative research (Marujo, 2013; Creswell, 2007), in order to understand punctual implications of false news on tourist activity. For this, the collected indications were adapted to carry out the study as a review and retraction of news according to an agenda proposed in Fedeli's research (2019). In the scope of a practically pioneering article in the study of fake news applied to tourism, the author suggests some themes related to four distinct spheres: 1. ethics; 2. Marketing; 3. Impact on tourist perception and behavior and 4. Security and regulation: In this respect the author includes the question of what would be the possible form of protection against fake news for tourism companies, the author also questions if cybersecurity can be the center of this sphere (Fedeli 2019, p. 2-3). Thus, the reference article, which was considered relevant for the beginning of the study on the relationship between phenomena, was also taken as the basis for this methodology. As the research is similar to the objective of encouraging more research involving both themes, it is believed that exposing events that prove the seriousness of the theme for tourism is a considerable way to explore this field, with a view to future research. However, we conducted a news search of facts related to the first three spheres mentioned, and each sphere was described according to the base research mentioned (Fedeli, 2019), and added the perception of the authors of this work. Therefore, the news considered most representative of each sphere were described and analyzed in order to illustrate this theoretical part (Delmazo & Valente, 2018).
5.Analysis and discussion of results
5.1.Ethics sphere
Ethics in tourism is considerably sensitive to the current moment of each generation, culture, region and other social factors. From multiple perspectives, it is presupposed to discuss the pathways of tourism ethics from the perspective of organizations, professionals and especially the tourist consumer, who in turn calls for credibility and demands ethical practices (Wyse, 2006). In this sphere of research for fake news, Fedeli (2019, p. 2) addresses the extent to which freedom of expression may be acceptable if we live a new paradigm in which truth is no longer so easy to identify? This is a tireless and complex questioning that carries the essence of a democracy and the conditioning of fake news as a possible catalyst for censorship. For the author, this topic also encompasses satire and entertainment, a reality in tourist activity, which is common in humorist agendas. It is precisely this problem niche that will be pictured below. A New Zealand comedian made a sarcastic video about North Queensland in Australia and, in the comedian's opinion, little inhibitory guidance about the dangers of that region. Representation acts as a fake commercial to promote tourism in Queensland (Foster, 2018). In the video, the comedian makes use of phrases like "The best part is that you only have to pay a one-way ticket because you probably won't come back" and "One person suggested that they give away t-shirts to tourists who said," I lived in northern Queensland and survived "(Foster, 2018, p. 1). The comedian speaks in a joke, but it is nonetheless a criticism of the place or signaling the dangers of the place which in his opinion are not enough. So the question arises as to whether this kind of content is taken seriously? Can it influence the decision making of the tourist incisively? Or is this kind of video still a warning despite the hype? This example also makes us reflect on the position of the competent tourism agency of the region. It is up to those responsible for the development of tourism activity to fulfill the role of demystifying or facing issues for the construction or deconstruction of the image of the destination, which has not happened so far in the case mentioned. It can also be seen that this type of video can cause, besides a possible confusion or fear in the tourist's decision making, a certain curiosity of the people, with this, can boost the brand awareness of that destination. At this point it can undoubtedly be for both the positive and negative sides. In the region highlighted by the video, there are to some extent the dangers mentioned by the author of the video. However, only further study of the consequences of this video bringing the author's view, the impact on the viewer's view, and even the importance given and procedures performed by the competent tourism body to such an eventuality, can be a case study research line to prove the size of the impact of this type of production. The fact is that the comedian has achieved considerable engagement on his post, which has over 100,000 shares and over 60,000 likes on Facebook.
5.2.Marketing Sphere
In this category of reflection, Fedeli (2019) addresses issues such as the credibility of organizations, whether it is possible for fake news to be an acceptable practice in tourism marketing and the extent to which organizations are responsible for using fake news in their marketing activities. The author further suggests questioning the difference between scam and fake news in marketing (Fedeli, 2019, p. 2). According to Beni (2007) the purpose of tourism marketing for a territory is to "formulate and adapt its products to achieve optimal demand satisfaction" (p. 231). In this age of fake news, marketing is yet another tool, and more, it can be used as an "excuse" for using fraudulent news. In 2017, for example, the Lithuanian tourism department used photos from Finland and Slovakia to promote the country. The real is beautiful campaign was ridiculed on social networks and culminated in the departure of the head of tourism from Lithuania (BBC News, 2017). Thus, it is worth reflecting on the motivations for an organization to use a false registration as a photo to promote the tourist destination. The justification for using photos that do not match the reality is to boost the tourist activity? This is currently incomprehensible, since this kind of manipulation is very fragile to sustain, as we live in an era when increased internet access and search ease deconstructs this kind of news within minutes. This case may also enter into the discussion of misleading advertising, all these facts also being related to the sphere of ethics. The age of connectivity, expressed in marketing 4.0 (Kotler et al., 2017) indicates the greater participation of consumers in the digital perspective, to share their experience and evaluate something. Thus, in addition to this greater participation, there is greater importance given to these evaluations by both consumers and the evaluated company. These positive reviews can even generate awards for the company, such as the seal of excellence or star rating of the ratings that can be displayed on the physical or digital company. Therefore, these evaluations end up being part of marketing strategies, being something that has become a key point for maintaining a good image. In this context, review sites are gaining more attention and space in the market, such as tripadvisor (Zhao et al., 2019).
However, in this fake news paradigm, these ratings do not always have to be true today to be rated for company rankings, which is worrying. In London for example, over a certain period of time, the top-rated restaurant through Tripadvisor reviews was a fictional restaurant. The restaurant did not exist and was created by a journalist to test this controversial side of review sites (Filieri & McLeay, 2014). Just as you live in an age of connectivity where review sites are booming, isn't it time to create alternatives within these sites in light of the reality of fake news? How can websites control this? Thinking of creating an industry in these companies to ascertain the establishment, for example, as a requirement for site participation, might be a solution. But what if the company exists and you know the comment is fake, and worse, paid for it? This is what happened in Italy, when a man unheard-of a fraud decision was arrested in 2018, accused of selling false reviews through tripadvisor, to hotel companies (The Guardian, 2018). While driving the company's marketing, it hurts the ethical sphere and encompasses a digital "new business" sector, not bots, but real people profiting to make positive comments without criteria and without even have had the experience of enjoying the product. However, one of the remarks in these cases is how reliable in this age of fake news are review sites are generally reliable? Just as there was a transition to marketing 4.0, the adaptation of this tool to the context of fake news is essential for the credibility of tourism products and organizations in this sector. Thus, in this perspective an alternative may be the study of the creation of quality indicator tools, with reliability parameters for attribution to companies, such as a seal.
5.3.Sphere of impact of perception and behavior
Fedeli (2019) argues that this aspect refers to the impact of the news on changing the notion of a place, and this potential factor influences the decision making of tourists. This sphere may be part of the concepts of image building of a place, which should be part of the marketing objectives of the region, in the words of Guerreiro (2014, p. 3) "[...] build a solid and positive image of the city, through the integrated management of the place marketing mix variables. "
Efforts to build a brand image are also ways to stimulate the decision to buy tourists, as well as the positioning of the tourist product perceived by tourists (Faias et al., 2009; Oliveira et al., 2008). From this point of view, it can be reflected that all reported efforts may fail for the real probabilities, as well as for associating fake news with destiny. In Olimpia - Sao Paulo (Brazil), a rumor of a meningitis outbreak (severe disease causing meningeal inflammation and may leave severe sequelae in the patient) emerged in December 2018. Although the city's official pages have published that the news was false, it was not enough to change the credibility of the rumor that circulated mainly via whatsapp. As a result, many tourists associated the city with the disease and gave up the trip, which resulted in a 35% loss in local tourism (Olimpia SP, 2018; iFolha, 2019). In the case presented, there was a retraction of the competent body, but even so the result of the rumor was a significant loss, as mentioned. It is also noteworthy that the season is high season in Brazil, which prices are high as well as the importance of maximizing the tourist flow in the period. Giving up travel for false reasons is a problem to be questioned from this viral fake news perspective. A study of why tourists do not change their behavior, even in the face of true information is essential. This type of study is common from a fake news perspective, and clearly is something that needs to be further explored within the reality of tourism activity.
6.Conclusions
Tourism is increasingly one of the most important socioeconomic activities in many countries, and in some cases the most powerful activity, particularly in countries that live in this tourism sector. In general terms, there has been a growth in tourist activity. Tourism is thus presented as an activity that, when well planned and developed, has advantages and positive aspects, such as job creation and environmental conservation, culture enhancement, conservation and / or recovery of historical heritage and large increases in infrastructure, among others. Therefore, it is true that the evolution of technology has brought new business opportunities, but on the other hand also brought about changes in the behavior of society, specifically its consumers, and therefore it becomes imperative to study and understand their behavior so that it can combine strategies that are consistent with these new behaviors (Vilas-Boas & Sousa, 2019) and the environment at the same time as it is influenced by the organizations influences the conduct and performance of the same, especially, with respect to the practice of innovation (Walter et al., 2019).
Based on what has been investigated, this field turns out to be broad and lacking in exploration. In addition, fake news and its implications for tourism is a reality that raises a number of questions that may be a perspective of change in the operationalization of companies related to the activity, as is the case of evaluation sites. Therefore, the reflection of the relationship of themes through studies is a way to contribute with credible alternatives to deal with this paradigm. In this article, at each sphere studied, insights were presented with the stimulus for future investigation, being a scope form for deepening. It was noticed that the spheres have a strong connection, so in these cases it is possible to understand the compilation of marketing, ethics and behavior themes in just one piece of news. However, the spheres presented by Fedeli (2019) are sometimes hybrid, and it is up to the researcher to specify which perspective to work on, or even to conduct a case study and to address the perspective of all spheres, including that of non-regulatory regulation contemplated in this article.

In general terms, there has been a growth in tourist activity. Tourism is thus presented as an activity that, when well planned and developed, has advantages and positive aspects, such as job creation and environmental conservation, culture enhancement, conservation and / or recovery of historical heritage and large increases in infrastructure, among others. However, in order to cope with this rapid growth in the tourist flow that has occurred in recent decades, it is important to adopt tools for preparing and controlling tourism activity in tourist destinations, thus avoiding negative impacts. This study presents a preliminary contribution around the theme of tourism (i.e. fake news). From an interdisciplinary perspective, this study presents contributions to marketing, tourism and local development. Future studies should measure causal relationships presented here and test the influence of affectivity on specific tourism contexts and communications (i.e. fake news).
