STRENGTHS AND LIMITATIONS OF THIS STUDY
- This is the first study to examine the aetiology of mis- and disinformation within social media environments about healthcare policies among Latinos.

- This study will use data across social media platforms, enhancing generalisability.

- A Spanish-language sentiment lexicon will be created via crowd-sourcing that will help address current gaps in knowledge regarding Spanish-language social media sentiment.

- Development of the sentiment lexicon and analyses is limited to publicly accessible social media data.


Introduction
The flow and spread of mis- and disinformation have been major concerns with social media, defined as sharing false information without the intention to mislead and sharing false information that is intentionally misleading, respectively. 1 Consequently, there have been attempts to predict virality and potentially characterise the impact of social media on users’ beliefs and behaviours. An important factor in the potential virality of social media posts is the intensity of emotional reaction to a post. 2 3 A widely used approach to categorising emotional reactions to social media posts is the use of the natural language processing (NLP)-based Valence Aware Dictionary and sEntiment Reasoner (VADER). VADER was developed and made publicly available in 2014 to determine sentiment associated with social media posts. 4 Using workers from Amazon Mechanical Turk, 7500 lexical features were rated on a scale of −4 to +4, denoting the direction and intensity of sentiment. Using VADER, prior research has explored the emotional valence of Twitter tweets that were providing misinformation about COVID-19, examined sentiments about mental health before and during the pandemic using Twitter data and characterised tweets posted among emergency physicians as they discussed the pandemic. 5–7 Other research has used VADER to measure negativity bias due to voter fraud conspiracy-related tweets during the 2020 presidential election in the UUSA and most recently to conduct a comparative study of loneliness in India and the USA. 8 9
One major limitation of VADER is that it is available only in English. In 2019, the US Census Bureau reported that over 40 million US residents spoke Spanish at home, which is an increase of more than 50% since 2000. 10 Furthermore, compared with non-Latino White (hereafter White) adults, Latino adults are more likely to report using X/Twitter (25% vs 20% for Whites), Reddit (23% vs 21%) and Instagram (58% vs 43%). Two-thirds (66%) of Latino adults reported using Facebook. 11 While Latinos and especially Spanish-speaking social media users are highly connected to social media, there is very little knowledge regarding the extent/flow/spread of mis- or disinformation to this population. For instance, the Pew Research Centre reported that one in five Latino adults use social media for political and election news. 12 The translation of English language news into Spanish may introduce additional potential for inadvertent misinformation. 13
Prior research has demonstrated that misinformation is impactful on decision-making, and it is difficult to modify behaviour in response to misinformation, even if the misinformation is later corrected. 14 There is evidence that Latinos have been subject to mis- and disinformation campaigns in prior election cycles. 15 16 Further, a survey in 2022 reported that more than one in three of all social media users perceived substantial health-related misinformation was occurring on social media, and two-thirds reported difficulty in discerning accurate vs inaccurate health information. 17 The same study also reported that Hispanic social media users were less likely than non-Hispanic white users to have the same difficulty in discernment. It is not clear whether this finding was due to Hispanic users receiving less misinformation or if they were less aware of it.
It is also unclear how mis- and disinformation regarding how policy changes impact Latino perceptions of their vulnerabilities if they attempt to enrol themselves or their family members for healthcare programmes or use healthcare. Little is known about the context in which communication about health policy is accessed, assessed, interpreted, spread and acted upon.
To address this gap in knowledge, we will study the aetiology of mis- and disinformation within social media environments about healthcare policies such as Medicaid enrolment and healthcare access among Latinos. First, we will develop a Spanish-language version of VADER (S-VADER) using native Spanish-speaking crowd-sourced individuals to rate the negative and positive sentiment of a Spanish lexicon of words and expressions. Using S-VADER and social media tweets/posts relating to health policies and their intersection with policies identified as mis- or disinformation following prior research, we will characterise the intensity of reactions to these posts and track their virality over time using data from X/Twitter, Facebook/Instagram and Reddit.
Methods and analysis
Social media data
Our study will use social media posts from Facebook/Instagram and Reddit and threads from X/Twitter. Posts and threads (P/T) (and their sources) will be classified as (1) fake-news-related, (2) fact-related and (3) other (ie, posts that do not make clear fact-based claims or contain clear misinformation). The ‘other’ category will include advertisements, opinions, questions, entertainment or stories, or other irrelevant content. This classification approach will consist of two components. First, if the posts or threads contain links, these links will be classified based on whether they are included on a selected list of fake-news-related websites identified by the Columbia Journalism Review and by using fake-news site lists provided by fact-checking organisations like PolitiFact, FactCheck, OpenSources and Snopes. Domain names that are classified as fake, conspiracy, bias and unreliable by these organisations will be included in our study. Similarly, we will use a curated list of fact-related websites that has been used in prior research. 18 Second, posts or threads without links will be evaluated using artificial intelligence-based fact-checking tools such as Chequebot. Chequebot is a NLP-based tool that was developed specifically for fact-checking of Spanish language social media content.
We will identify posts and threads focused on sharing information related to healthcare and health benefits programmes, such as Medicaid and provisions from the Affordable Care Act. The study will identify keywords and phrases associated with these policies and programmes, such as ‘public benefits’, ‘Medicaid’, ‘Affordable Care Act’ or ‘Obamacare’ and ‘healthcare’. In addition to English search terms embedded in Spanish-language posts, we will use the corresponding Spanish language search terms: ‘beneficios públicos’, ‘asistencia pública’, ‘ayuda pública’, ‘servicios de salud’, ‘cuidado de la salud’, ‘sistema de salud’ and ‘acceso a la salud’. At the state level, we will include specific programme names, for example, ‘Covered California’, ‘Pennie’ (Pennsylvania) and ‘Kynect Care’ (Kentucky). The final search terms will be developed in collaboration with the research team, and these search terms will be used to conduct a systematic search of posts and threads using the Application Programming Interfaces (API) for X/Twitter, Facebook/Instagram, and Reddit. The API will return a collection of posts and threads that match a specified query. X/Twitter API for academic research access provides real-time and historical data of up to 10 million tweets per month for the study period. Facebook/Instagram data are available to researchers via Meta’s Academic and Researchers Programme. Meta tracks over 7 million Facebook pages, groups, and verified profiles including all public pages with more than 50 000 likes, all public groups with 95 000 or more members, all US-based public groups with 2000 or more members, and all verified profiles. Finally, Reddit provides access to data for over 100 000 active communities and 57 million users. For Reddit, we will use the Python Reddit API Wrapper (PRAW) in order to identify relevant subreddits and compile data from subreddit posts and post comments.
To identify Latino users, we will follow prior research using social media data. 19 This includes using self-reported race and ethnicity (when available from social media data), location in majority Latino communities and use of Spanish language or mixed Spanish/English language for P/T. We will compile posts and threads based on the parent posts and threads with the fake-news- or fact-related information and all subsequent posts and threads following the parent tweet/post. Afterwards, we will explore the dissemination of fake news and facts across the social media platforms in two ways. First, we will examine the number of re-tweets and comments within a 1 week and 30-day period after the initial post. The two periods of time will capture virality and longer-term sustainment of the information diffusion. Second, we will conduct a sentiment analysis using the Python-based NLP algorithm of VADER, which has been compared and validated with other established sentiment analysis lexicons. 20 VADER was specifically designed to classify the positive or negative sentiment of social media posts using an established lexicon of words connoting sentiment and then ranks these words according to a positive or negative numerical scale. 4 For example, words denoting violence, hate and curses were the most negatively rated; expressions such as ‘aml’, ‘ilu’ and words such as ‘magnificent’ or ‘heart’ were the most positively rated. Thus, for each P/T from a fake-news- or fact-based source (described above), the overall positive or negative sentiment can be ascertained based on its lexical features and corresponding ratings.
Developing a sentiment-based algorithm
Because VADER is based on English, the research team will create a sentiment-based machine learning algorithm specifically for Spanish. To do this, we will follow the methodology of VADER, which used a crowd-sourced approach to create a dictionary of words and attached sentiment labels. We will use a crowd-based source of participants/workers: Prolific. In this way, many and diverse raters can review and classify text, which results in an efficient and less potentially biased approach to determine sentiment compared with more traditional approaches such as having a small number of raters in the labelled lexicon. Established in 2014, Prolific is one of the world’s largest platforms for crowd-based research consisting of over 120 000 active participants. For VADER, this involved 20 crowd-based worker raters of 7500 lexical features to help create its English language-labelled lexicon.
To create the Spanish-language sentiment-labelled lexicon, we will first use the API from X/Twitter, Facebook/Instagram and Reddit to create random pulls of posts and threads. To generate a large and varied lexicon, we will pull 5000 Spanish-language tweets, Facebook/Instagram posts and subreddit posts. We will vary the times and days of threads and posts. A lexicon consisting of Spanish nouns, verbs and adjectives, as well as emojis, acronyms, emoticons and slang, from the social media tweets and posts will be compiled (‘lexical features’).
Recruitment and characteristics of raters
We will contract raters from Prolific to label the lexicon. Qualifications for the raters will include fluency in Spanish and Hispanic/Latino ethnicity. This provides a panel of 6529 eligible raters in Prolific; over 3500 raters also report their first language as Spanish. For participants meeting these qualifications, we will provide a short quiz created in Qualtrics to confirm their fluency in Spanish and to provide sample ratings to gauge their understanding of the task. The Spanish fluency quiz will be based on a subset of questions from the University of Wisconsin–Madison’s Spanish Language Usage and Reading Test and will take approximately 10 min to complete.
Raters will be asked to rate each lexical feature using a rating scale from −4 ‘Extremely Negative’ to 4 ‘Extremely Positive’. Adapting the methodology used in the English VADER, we will validate responses by the raters using a subset of lexical features that are pre-validated by other raters. We will remove all sentiment ratings for any rater who is more than one SD away in their sentiment ratings from the average sentiment ratings on the majority of features within this subset of pre-validated features.
Prolific reports the geographic location of its users. In addition, our Qualtrics survey of raters will ask which country is most representative of cultural background. Based on these data, we will capture differences in the use of language expressions between, for example, Mexican-origin speakers and speakers from Central America. We will review the ratings, and any idiomatic differences for a lexical feature will be flagged and noted within the S-VADER dataset.
In addition, Prolific participants are queried on their social media usage (‘Which of the following social media sites do you use on a regular basis (at least once a month)?’ and tweeting frequency (‘How many times did you tweet on Twitter in the last 12 months (excluding retweets)?’). This information will be used to compare differences in ratings by social media engagement, for example, Facebook vs non-Facebook users.
Planned analysis
The constructed Spanish-language-labelled lexicon (‘Spanish-VADER’) will be used in conjunction with Python to determine the positive or negative sentiment of tweets/posts in response to an initial posting of fake-news- or fact-related information. Thus, we will examine the rapidity and scale of the spread of Spanish language information in addition to the sentiment scores of posts and tweets. Rapidity and spread will be calculated based on time in hours until peak engagement (retweets or shares) and cumulative engagement within 1 week and 30 days in response to the tweet/post. Using both VADER and S-VADER, it is feasible to examine cross-language sentiments in response to English- or Spanish-language posts. Bivariate differences in mean sentiment scores will be calculated. Similarly, we will also calculate descriptive statistics of sentiment scores in order to examine differences in sentiment to healthcare-related dis- and misinformation among Spanish speakers in Latin America compared with Spanish speakers located in the USA. Mann–Whitney U tests will be performed to determine statistical significance.
Ethics
The study will commence on 15 May 2025 and end on 31 January 2028. Our study protocol was approved by the University of California, Los Angeles IRB (IRB#23–0 01 123).
Patient and public involvement
Patients or the public will not be involved in the design or conduct of the study; however, S-VADER will be disseminated to public repositories such as GitHub. Similar to the previously established VADER, by making S-VADER publicly accessible, we anticipate that S-VADER will be used for a broad range of potential research areas and interventions relating to Spanish-language social media, from analysing sentiment to political disinformation to characterising attitudes and discourse towards health, immigration, crisis response or other high-profile topics. 21–23
