Introduction
The increasing use of the internet is gradually becoming mainstream in human life. The Internet is turning into the prime source of knowledge for many users. Millions of multi-level users use the internet every day for recreation, knowledge enhancement, and connecting with peers and coworkers. With the advancement of usage of recent smart and intelligent mobile phones, tablets, and laptops, social networks have become more influential and popular. Anyone with these devices can conveniently access the Internet, World Wide Web, WhatsApp, Twitter, MySpace, Friendster, Facebook, LinkedIn, etc. anywhere and at any time. People actively participate in social networks and disseminate information in the form of images, text, audio, video, etc. The growing use of smart devices also plays a significant role in increasing the number of internet users nowadays. Smart devices are tiny, cheap, user-friendly, and used in various complex settings. With the recent boom in online social networking, many social media platforms have grown massively due to their ability to smooth data retrieval and fast distribution of information. Social networks [52, 55] are the platforms to connect with people in no time, access real-time information, grow the business, online classes, and entertainment. This is the only reason that the number of publications referencing “Social networks” is expanding at an exponential rate after 1970 s. The potential impacts and applications of social networks are explored in papers [56, 57, 66]. The important dimensions of social networks are explored in our previous papers [51, 54, 58, 60]. Social networks have positive and negative sides both. As we know every coin has two sides. It has negative impacts too like rumor and fake news spreading. We have already addressed rumor detection and its prevention in our recent work [62]. Fake news detection is another potential task. As we use images, text, audio, video, etc. in social networks to address a multimodal fake news detection is an essential task. Spreading fake news on social networks is one of the concerns of online social networking. As lies diffuse faster than truth on social networks, fake news detection becomes a significant concern worldwide. Fake news can affect economic, political, and social welfare immensely. In the current scenario, social networks are prone to lose their credibility and charm as content creators tempt users by posting fake information to make money and gain popularity. Therefore, detecting fake information on social platforms has earned special recognition in research.
Fake news is an event that links to many different ideas, which users continuously read. The purpose of fake news written or published on social media platforms is to deceive people and ruin the appearance of somebody or something, potentially for economic or socio-political gains [20, 89]. For the past few years, researchers have seen fake data on social media platforms which have become a popular places for sharing fake news [21, 80]. Detecting fake information has become a severe problem due to its adverse impacts [89]. There are a multitude of reasons why people believe false news. With multiple approaches in the past to detecting fake news [41], it is still an active area of research.
The term ‘fake news’ is interchangeably known as ‘hoax’, ‘misinformation’, ‘rumor’, and ‘disinformation’ in the literature. A rumor is an unproven claim concerning any matter transmitted from one person to another [7, 15, 21]. The rumor may refer to an event, blog, or any other issue of any genuine civic concern that may negatively affect people. A hoax is a lie or story purposely built to disguise the truth [79]. Today, it is growing at a fast pace as people are using it for financial or political gains.
Fake news that spreads to mass readers or viewers can cause financial damage, high societal infliction, and shape political events. The harmful effects of spreading misinformation are well known and can have devastating consequences [11, 33]. Various studies have examined the impact of unverified news on legitimacy and public confidence in authorized political and media foundations. The presidential elections held in 2016 in the USA are an example of fake news. Intentional misinformation and fake news swayed the election and caused public misperception [4, 8]. There have been other instances of the spread of unverified news, which has often caused devastating consequences. Events like Brexit [32], growing disgust, atrocity, and nationalism in Indonesia [34], and the jeopardy of European and Latin American countries [16] are associated with the spread of rumors and unverified news. Admittedly, fake news supported by bots propagates considerably quicker and more profoundly than any mainstream news [71]. In addition, the following analysis also hints that it is challenging to differentiate fake information from reliable information. The more frequently a piece of fake news is leaked, the more credible that news seems to the readers [5]. The earlier practices also explain the significance of identifying and opposing misinformation in its initial stages of spread [9]. Hence, up-to-date, accessible, and effective fake news detection automatons are essential to countering fake news. Motivation
Despite the goodness and facilities of social media in the news sector, the diffusion of fake news due to a lack of control and verification mechanisms is one of the most serious menace to democracy, freedom of expression, health, etc. Therefore, designing an automatic fake news detection method on social networks is a challenge. With the emergence of new technologies, ML and DL techniques are growing toward more decentralized perspectives [19]. This study suggests an ensemble approach based on CNN and Bi-LSTM for fake news detection. It also leverages blockchain to ensure that genuine news remains immutable. Experiments are conducted to demonstrate the feasibility and assess the performance of the FNNet model. We employ accuracy, recall, and precision as the measures to assess the performance of the model.
Contributions of the paper
The contributions of this work are as follows: - This paper proposes a secure social network by detecting fake news in the early stages to prevent it from propagating.

- We develop a model that leverages blockchain to assure news security and DL to learn data representations.

- The framework is divided into four layers: node layer, DL layer, blockchain layer, and network layer. The node layer performs low-level functions such as hardware management, user interface, and message generation. The DL layer classifies the news contents into genuine and fake. The proposed model is an ensemble model that uses Bi-LSTM model and CNN models. The blockchain layer evaluates the integrity of the message to be transmitted or received. In this layer, blockchains store the metadata of the news. It allows the creation of a set of interconnected blocks that are immutable. This layer ensures that the correct information circulating in the network remains unaltered. Finally, the fourth layer, the network layer, aims to determine the routing and updating node credibility.

- A hybrid PoW and PoS based consensus algorithms are utilized at the blockchain layer to ensure the accumulation of credibility within the network.

- We evaluate the performance of FNNet using three datasets for fake news detection.

- We compare our model with five previous studies and obtain the best results.

Related works
With the recent boom in internet users, detecting fake information on social platforms has earned special recognition in research. There exist various fake news detection approaches such as expert-fast-checkers [89], crowdsourcing [17, 45], NLP methods [31], graph-based [25, 68, 77, 78], ML [2] and DL [28, 40, 76, 82]. Expert-fact-checkers are a group of professionals in various fields, who can verify the accuracy of the news, and decides whether the news is genuine or fake. However, this approach is time-consuming. The crowdsourcing approach is an alternative to expert-fact-checker, as this approach relies on the collective approval of groups or individuals. However, the users who affect the decision should be verified. NLP methods use powerful mechanisms like lexical and semantic analysis. For early fake news detection, graph networks perform well but they use many hyper-parameters for which they require high computational costs. A significant record of present studies uses ML-based classifiers that automatically decide if the news circulating is fake based on various information characteristics.
The authors in [10] recognized three kinds of fake news in practice- heavy lies, large-scale fabrications, and witty fake news. They were the first ones to apply network analysis in fake news detection. Recently, a survey of multiple types of false information with real-world examples is presented by [67]. Authors in [76] presented a thorough summary of the fake news detection approaches and matching problems.
Many recent papers in the literature use BERT model in this area [13, 29, 87]. The authors [82] used the social and behavioral information of the speaker and proposed a hybrid deep model. The model uses BERT and CNN to detect fake news. Their algorithm achieved 48% accuracy on the Liar [81] dataset. Similarly, authors of [40] proposed a hybrid model for fake news detection using CNN and RNN. They experimented with the model on ISOT and FA-KES datasets. Further, authors in [6] experimented with various ML models viz. kNN, Adaboost, RF, and DL models like LSTM, CNN, RNN, and GRU. They achieved the highest accuracy of 97% on a COVID dataset. However, RNNs cannot capture long sequences of text-based data. Further, authors of [2] applied different algorithms viz. logistic regression, linear SVM, kNN, multilayer perceptron, ensemble learners, and benchmark algorithms on four different datasets of which one is the ISOT fake news dataset. They achieved the maximum accuracy of 99% by RF algorithm (ensemble learner) and Perez-LSVM (benchmark algorithm). A blending ensemble model was proposed in [22] to predict the integrity of news reports. They used the following ML models: logistic regression, LDA, stochastic gradient descent, ridge regression, and SVM to build the ensemble model. They implemented their blending model on the Liar and ISOT fake news dataset and concluded that it perform better than previous studies. Zhang et al. [86] proposed a FakeDetector framework to detect fake news. It utilizes a deep diffusive network model with credibility label inference and representation feature learning. They obtained an accuracy score of 0.63 using the proposed model to spot fake news on the PolitiFact dataset.
Other methods to recognize fake news are based on topic content [46, 83]. However, these methods have several shortcomings like the incapability of detecting fake news in images and videos and lack of accurate information on prominent social media like Twitter. Hence, the linguistic features are usually lacking for ML algorithms to deliver correct predictions. The added step about existing studies recognizes fake news as a user’s characteristics (people who initially tweet the concerning news on the social media platform) [83]. Although, these methods overlook the features of news circulators (people retweeting the concerning news), which could also give us evidence about the news credibility.
Authors of [38] identified fake news by extracting temporal-linguistic features from user comments and temporal-structural features from propagation networks. Although these methods are more efficient in detecting fake news than the preceding methods, which only utilize textual data or the circulator’s characteristics, temporal linguistic and structural data are usually not available or incompetent in the initial news advancement stages. Some existing proposals identify fake news utilizing various contrasting features [35]. On the other hand [84], employ CNN to develop a fake news detection model. The model analyzes the text and image data.
Authors in [48] worked on detecting fake images that suffer multiple compressions. They exploited RNN to identify and analyze fake and real images semantically by presenting an approach named MVNN. This approach incorporates CNN to extract frequency-domain patterns and integrates CNN with RNN to extract pixel-domain patterns. The purpose of these extractions is to highlight the periodicity in frequency due to the re-compression of images. It uses an attention mechanism to fuse these approaches to improve the performance of its model. Authors of [14] evaluated the performance of individual models and other machine-learning models by using all 23 textual features extracted from the ISOT news dataset. The proposed RF-based ML classifier categorizes the news into fake and real news. They achieved the highest score of 97.25% compared to other classifiers on all features.
A strategy to detect rumors on social media platforms was devised by [90]. The strategy preserves the context preceding a message using a sequential classifier. They tested over five detailed datasets gathered from Twitter and other sites for gossip and non-rumors. They reveal that the initial context manipulated as a series can increase the rumor detector’s execution. Li et al. [36] built a generalized classifier to filter tweets of the CrisisLexT6 dataset. They followed a feature-based approach that represents tweets in the form of numeric vectors. They used sentence encodings and word embeddings to reduce dimensionality.
Authors in [91] proposed a credibility detection system that alerts users of unproven posts. They collected 5802 tweets using Twitter streaming API. They used social features and content-based features for the evaluation of their work. They used CRF as a sequential classifier. Another research in [24] developed a classification model to classify Twitter messages into credible and non-credible. TF and TF-IDF are used as the weighting methods. They used word N-gram analysis to introduce a supervised ML model for tweet classification. They performed experiments on different classifiers, namely, kNN, linear SVM, logistic regression, RF, and Naïve Bayes, on the Pheme dataset. They attained the best performance by using the N-gram and LSVM models.
Authors of [1] attempted to achieve benchmark results in fake news detection by blending CNN with RNN. They also incorporated the concept of word embedding in their model to improve performance. In a similar work [69], designed an ensemble model based on CNN and Bi-LSTM and achieved an accuracy of 44.87%. In this ensemble model, the Bi-LSTM model is used to capture the sequential data in both directions and CNN is used to capture the hidden features. The representations obtained from CNN and Bi-LSTM are then fed into a multilayer perceptron model for multi-classification. Recently [26], designed an architecture that combines contextual embedding and the attention mechanism to classify news into one of the six classes.
Paul et al. [44] and Shae and Tsai [70] leveraged blockchain in the area of fake news detection. Recently, the authors in [63] used blockchain technology to propose a secure healthcare system. They designed a four-layer framework that describes the collection of patient information, routing the patient information over the network, and sending the critical data through the blockchain layer for further computation. Rani et al. [62] also used blockchain to verify the credibility of information flowing in the network. They proposed a model that exploited the DL technique and used LSTM model to prevent rumors in the network. Inspired by this work, we present a fake news detection model in this paper that uses CNN with the LSTM model to improve the proposed model’s performance.
Recently, to evaluate the credibility of publishers on social networks, authors in [27] attempted to propose an algorithm, called CreditRank. They also suggested a multi-modal framework for fake news detection, namely FR-Detect. The framework employs user-related and content-related features. They also provide a sentence-level CNN to combine latent textual content features with publishers’ features. Many models of fake news detection use news content as input. They leveraged user features and the extracted news as text and neglected the interaction between news content. Since news on social media does not exist in isolation, but is interconnected and interactive. To address this problem, a dual-channel graph convolutional attention network is proposed in [88]. They constructed a heterogeneous graph that models the semantic information between words and source tweets, and the spread structure between users and source tweets. A multi-source dataset MCFEND is given by [37]. They constructed this dataset to detect Chinese fake news. This dataset comprises news from different sources like messaging apps, social sites, and online news outlets. Another work [23] uses stacked supervised and unsupervised ML techniques for fake news detection. They used FastText word embeddings on SVM, DT, RF, and bagging classifiers and implemented the proposed model on three datasets. Moreover, they used FastText embedding on DL-based methods like LSTM, GRU, and CNN.
Based on an extensive literature survey, many existing models for fake news detection employ conventional ML models such as SVMs, LDA, and pre-trained neural networks. However, such models are not robust enough when applied to textual data. Further, these models face several issues, including a lack of generalizability and an inability to use user-specific information such as credibility rating and previous message transmissions. Various ML models have been experimented with to detect fake news automatically in the literature, but they failed to spot fake news when the data is multi-modal. Therefore, this paper proposes an ensemble model that assists in the automated identification of fake news. This paper addresses the identified gaps in detecting fake news in multi-modal data. Preliminaries
This section discusses the concept of blockchain used in the proposed work.
Blockchain
Blockchain technology is an approvingly reliable distributed storehouse system with encryption to deal with the mistrusted behavior of consumer nodes. Blockchain is a secure, integrated, and disseminated ledger approach where many blocks are chained jointly [85]. Blockchain has an unchangeable database for transmitting messages safely and confidentiality among nodes. It is the emerging technology used excessively in improving security and reducing fraud [39, 53, 59, 61, 64].
Blockchain allows the creation of a set of interconnected blocks that are immutable. It implies that once a block is formed and added to the blockchain, its contents cannot change. Every node has its copy of the blockchain, consisting of interconnected blocks. Every node’s blockchain evaluates a node’s transmission history and is designed to be of a fixed length to prevent resource exhaustion. The primary use of blockchain is to ensure that the correct information disseminated in the network is not altered by malicious parties. Blockchain ensures that data is decentralized and does not suffer from drawbacks such as a single point of failure. Blockchain also uses various consensus algorithms to check for the legitimacy of data [50]. PoW and PoS are the widely used consensus algorithms. These algorithms have their advantages and disadvantages in terms of computation and randomness. The consensus strategy used in the proposed work uses a hybrid of PoW and PoS algorithms.
Block structure
The block structure of the proposed model consists of various attributes used for data storage and block linkage in the blockchain. Figure 13 depicts the blockchain structure which is used in the model. Block 0, or genesis block, is the first block in the blockchain, intentionally initialized with random values as it does not have a previous hash value. Subsequent blocks consist of actual data and messages that nodes communicate with each other. Every block in the blockchain is interlinked using the SHA-256 hashing algorithm. The components of a block in the blockchain are metadata, nonce, previous hash, hash, and timestamp. The metadata in a block identifies and stores various information and properties of the block, such as version, sender ID, receiver ID, message type, and priority. The receiver and intermediate nodes access this information to evaluate whether the block is intended for a particular receiver. A nonce controls the difficulty or restriction of the block creation and hashing process. A nonce is a vast number to increase or decrease the problem of hashing a particular message. For the simulation, a constant nonce value is used for all nodes and all types of messages. The previous hash is the hash value of the previous block of the blockchain. The previous block’s hash links a block to the blockchain. For any block i in the blockchain, the previous hash value of the i th block and the hash value of ( i - 1 ) th block are used to evaluate if the block contents have been modified or not. The hash value is a fixed-length string generated after passing the block contents via an SHA-256 hashing algorithm. Figure 3 depicts a general blockchain structure. [See PDF for image]
Fig. 3 Blockchain structure


Hashing ensures that the hash value changes if any modifications to the message contents are made. Further, changes to the message will also lead to consistency between the same block’s hash and previous hash values. The timestamp is a string of format MM:DD:YYYY:HH:MM:SS:MS, which is a unique value generated whenever a new block is created. It is also added to the hashing algorithm and ensures that the hash value of two or more blocks is never the same.
Consensus strategy
The proposed work uses IoT-driven mobile nodes that are low energy in nature, which means that heavy computation and energy-demanding tasks such as PoW consensus are not feasible. Low energy-demanding algorithms like PoS lack randomness and lead to the accumulation of credibility toward a specific set of nodes. The proposed consensus algorithm is a hybrid PoW–PoS model that uses node credibility ratings for incentivizing nodes. However, pseudo-random node selection criteria ensure that randomness is maintained and wealth accumulation is minimized within the network. Such a strategy allows validation opportunities for new nodes and is a more healthy consensus algorithm. Both consensus algorithms are briefly explained as follows: - Proof of Work: PoW is the conventional and most widely used consensus algorithm for blockchain validation. It allows miners to create and validate new blocks by performing heavy computations. The miners are incentivized to use their resources by a fixed reward: the currency (credibility rating in the proposed model). While PoW is an excellent algorithm in its randomness and validation, it requires heavy computation and high energy consumption. IoT nodes are often low on resources like energy which makes it an infeasible solution for opportunistic IoT nodes. Hence, a less resource-intensive scheme is required for the proposed use case.

- Proof of Stake: PoS allows nodes with high currency or credibility ratings to participate in the block creation and validation process. Nodes can stake a specific credibility rating for validation in the proposed framework. If the node performs a valid transaction, it is incentivized by an increase in credibility rating, which is an indicator of the legitimacy of a node. On the other hand, if a node performs an illegitimate transaction, its credibility rating reduces by a certain predetermined amount. It ensures that the network is balanced and does not run out of validator nodes. While this approach may decrease computational and energy requirements, it comes with the drawback of accumulating wealth. PoS leads to an endless selection of high credibility nodes, which creates a cycle and collects credibility ratings within the network. It decreases the randomness of node validation and threatens the security of the blockchain. A new routing strategy requires performing the same tasks with more randomness and less energy consumption.


Deep learning
DL has become an advancing technology used by researchers in various fields like recommendation systems [72, 73–74], fake news detection [28], healthcare [47], etc. DL has proven to be more efficient in detecting fake news than traditional ML methods. DL models mimic a human brain. In a general DL model, a multi-layered deep neural network is built-in in which data is passed to nodes in highly connected paths. We process multiple features in DL algorithms (CNN and Bi-LSTM) for better fake news analysis. The model integrates two powerful DL techniques: CNN and Bi-LSTM. The main motive is to learn both character-level and word-level features that enable the model to accurately classify the news into genuine and fake. The Bi-LSTM model captures long-term dependencies from previous and future sentences, while the convolutional layer in the CNN model detects relevant local features.
Bi-LSTM
This paper uses a Bi-LSTM model for extracting the temporal relationships between the text and its corresponding classes. The proposed model aims to address the various issues faced by conventional RNNs. RNNs cannot capture long sequences of text-based data as it is challenging to persist the long sequence of data from the initial stage to the final one. Deep RNNs also face vanishing gradient problems while updating weights during backpropagation. LSTM is the version of RNN that is used to predict the time series data [65]. As in natural language processing, we need to know the coming word in addition to the previous word. Bi-LSTM uses RNN to remember the long-term dependencies in the sequences at every time step. Bi-LSTM is a sequence processing model that uses two LSTMs. It uses propagation two times, one in the forward direction (past to future) for the forward cells and another in the backward (future to past) for the backward cells. The forward LSTM layer processes the input sequence as is, from t=1 to T (where T is the length of the sequence). This results in a sequence of forward hidden as follows: h 1 → , h 2 → , … , h T → here h t = o t ∗ tan h C t is the hyperbolic tangent function. C t updates the old cell state to the new cell state, and o t decides what part of the cell state is output. Similarly, the backward LSTM layer processes the input sequence in reverse, from t=T to 1 . It uses the same LSTM equations but with its own set of parameters (weights and biases). This generates a sequence of backward hidden states as follows: h T → , h T - 1 → , … , h 1 → Both forward and backward activations determine the output. The proposed Bi-LSTM and the inherent nature of LSTM models overcome this issue and ensure that the model is robust and achieves high accuracy on various datasets.
CNN model
The CNN model contains several embedding and convolutional layers to facilitate optimized feature set generation. These layers are cascaded with dropout layers to prevent overfitting. The initial embedding layer converts each element of the tokenized text to a fixed-length vector. An embedding-based strategy maintains the information in a vectorized form with reduced dimensionality compared to conventional encodings like one-hot encoding. 1-dimensional convolutional layers are used to build the rest of the CNN. The 1-dimensional layers allow the model to produce outputs over a single spatial dimension (suitable for textual data). Figure 4 depicts the structure of a 1-dimensional convolutional layer. Proposed work
This section introduces the framework and implementation of our proposed model. We propose a novel DL framework FNNet capable of identifying and detecting fake news. The proposed model is a secure network that uses DL to detect fake news effectively and applies blockchain to keep genuine news immutable. DL algorithms progressively learn about the data as it goes through each neural network layer [72]. Detecting fake news falls under the classification problem. The goal is to use labeled instances to train a classifier. It then deploys the trained model to predict labels of unseen test instances. There exist many approaches based on traditional ML approaches for fake news detection. Unlike classical feature-based models, DL models do not need handwritten features. DL models identify the best feature set on their own.
We organize the framework of FNNet into four layers, namely, the node layer, the DL layer, the blockchain layer, and the network layer. Figure 5 depicts the proposed secure framework for fake news detection. [See PDF for image]
Fig. 5 Proposed framework of secure network


The layer-wise description of the framework is as follows: - The first layer is the node layer which collects data from different smart devices. This layer includes the user devices that communicate with the system, start rumors, and verify facts. The devices may be smartphones used for communicating on social networking, computers for forum and news website viewing, or any information-generating or information-consuming device with an internet connection.

- The second layer, the DL layer, evaluates the legitimacy of the messages transmitted or received. The purpose of this layer is to filter the content, influence the credibility rating of nodes and notify the user about fake content. This layer stores software modules with DL models. This layer used the ensemble of CNN and Bi-LSTM models. The layer uses large-scale factual and labeled fake news datasets for training. It examines data from the device layer to determine the traits of fake news. These traits include emotional language use (such as overuse of exclamation marks and words that incite fear) and disparities between the facts. This framework uses CNN to analyze textual information and recognize linguistic patterns and uses Bi-LSTM to comprehend the informational context and flow of a fake news.

- The third layer is the blockchain layer which creates a set of interconnected blocks to make data immutable and ensure security in the network. Blockchain technology is known to guarantee the security, immutability, and transparency of data. Blocks (as represented by X, Y, and Z blocks in Fig. 5) in the blockchain store the verified data and fake news flags. It is possible to employ many blockchains for different fake news or themes. Before adding data to a block, a consensus mechanism guarantees that all participating nodes in the network reach consensus on the validity of the data. It stops information from being manipulated and tampered with. The blockchain layer uses the hybrid of PoW and PoS as the consensus scheme.

- The fourth layer is the network layer, which aims to determine routing and updating node credibility. This layer represents the channels of communication that let devices communicate with the blockchain network. The layer guarantees data integrity during transmission between devices and the network via secure communication protocols.

Each layer breaks down different features and combines subsequent layers to provide higher accuracy.
FNNet is an ensemble model based on CNN and Bi-LSTM models. CNNs are neural networks that take two-dimensional or three-dimensional matrices as inputs, learn features, and identify essential input parts. CNNs apply a weight-based filter across every element of the dataset. The network learns to optimize the filters through automated learning. CNNs are a specialized type of neural network that use convolutions in place of general matrix multiplication in at least one layer of the network. Convolutional layers convolve the input and pass the result to the next layer.
Preprocessing
The textual data must be preprocessed before being fed into the DL models. Preprocessing is the first stage to clean the data and extract the features. It selects the potential parameters that contribute to detecting fake news. Tokenization, stop word removal, and stemming/lemmatization (reducing words to their base forms) are some of the stages that fall under this category. The preprocessing of datasets begins with tokenizing the text. We vectorize the text of tweets or news by converting it to a sequence of integers based on word count. Tokenization is the process of breaking the sequence of characters in the text by locating the word boundaries. In other words, tokenization is a method of splitting any kind of data like sentence, paragraph, documents, etc. into smaller units such as individual words or terms. The next step of our preprocessing is stopword removal. Stopwords are common words appearing in the natural language and do not correspond to actual events. In this, we remove frequently occurring words like “and”, “the”, etc. We create a list of stopwords consisting of natural stopwords and redundant words occurring in our specific dataset. Finally, we pad the resulting sequences to create a uniform feature set to a classifier.
Proposed model
FNNet is an ensemble of CNN and Bi-LSTM models. Ensembling the models leverages the best of CNN and Bi-LSTM models. The CNN model assists in extracting news content features, while the Bi-LSTM model extracts temporal sequence features of news. Feature sets of these models create their own mutually exclusive predictions about a given input. Figure 6 depict the architecture of the deep learning layer in the FNNet model. [See PDF for image]

We collect fake and authentic data of three different datasets and pre-process it to train the FNNet model. The proposed ensemble model uses an initial embedding layer to convert text sequences into meaningful features. The news content contains visual and textual features. The embedding layer creates feature vectors of size 128 for the next layer. Next, a Bi-LSTM layer uses the feature vectors created by the embedding layer. The Bi-LSTM layer extracts meaningful text-based relationships from the data and passes an advanced feature set to the consequent layers, followed by the dropout layer. The dropout layer prevents overfitting and keeps the model robust. Finally, there are two dense layers to obtain the final output in the order of the number of classes. Blockchain and security analysis
This subsection explains the underlying blockchain implementation followed by the security analysis of transactions recorded. Processed results with the assigned class from the DL layer are fed to the blockchain layer to ensure information security. The designed framework consists of a decentralized network structure with no control authorization and no control over data dissemination. Therefore, no surprise broadcasted data is vulnerable to modification in transmission. To ensure data integrity, the proposed framework utilizes blockchain-based data communication to store the generated results permanently as an immutable transaction.
As soon as the DL models assign the class to the data, the created transaction is stored in the transaction pool and waits for approval. Each transaction stores the following information: generated news, assigned class, the identity of the respective node, and timestamp. In the transaction pool, transactions wait for the validator node and consensus procedure to commit the immutable block. A consensus procedure is used to verify transactions and store the set of transactions as the permanent block in the blockchain to prevent further modification. In the proposed framework, a hybrid PoW and PoS-based consensus are utilized for block commitment. We consider that only an immutable committed block can be broadcasted into the network, consequently, any kind of changes and malicious activity becomes impossible. The proposed blockchain framework ensures that the received information is always similar to the original content generated by the sender node which results in data integrity and system integrity.
Blockchain technology and neural network models have both individually brought significant innovations to their respective fields, but their combination holds even more promising, particularly in affecting training data in various ways. By combining the strengths of blockchain technology and neural network models, we propose a solution for fake news detection. With the integration of both blockchain and neural network models (Bi-LSTM and CNN), we address challenges related to training data management, privacy, and collaboration. Blockchain’s immutable ledger is leveraged to securely record the origin, history, and transformations of training data. Each step of data collection, preprocessing, and labeling can be timestamped and stored on the blockchain, ensuring transparency and integrity. This enhances trust in the training data and mitigates the risk of data manipulation or bias. The transparency and auditability of blockchain is leveraged to track and verify the entire training process of the model.
Security analysis
This section explains the security analysis of the proposed framework under various circumstances. We consider that any malicious node can act inappropriately by either modifying the transmitted information or by eavesdropping on the communication channel to disclose private data. Considering the decentralized nature of the proposed peer-to-peer network, the malicious actor creates multiple fake identities, or “sybils”, to gain control of a significant portion of the network known as the Sybil attack. In a blockchain network, a Sybil attack could be used to gain control of a significant number of nodes in the network, which could then be used to influence the consensus mechanism or launch a 51% attack. In a PoW blockchain, for example, a malicious actor could create multiple sybil identities, each with its mining power, to control the majority of the mining power in the network. This would allow the attacker to control the blockchain and potentially double-spend coins. In a PoS blockchain, a Sybil attack could be used to gain control of a large number of stakes and thereby control a significant portion of the network, potentially allowing the attacker to manipulate the consensus mechanism. To prevent Sybil attacks, we propose a credibility-based incentive mechanism using a hybrid of PoW and PoS models. We use pseudo-random node selection criteria to ensure that it maintains randomness and minimizes wealth accumulation within the network.

Performance assessment
In this paper, fake news detection is a bi-classification problem that classifies a piece of news into either fake or real. The model uses 40% dropout to prevent model overfitting. Adam optimizer is used along with binary cross-entropy as the loss function. We perform five-fold cross-validation in our experiments for evaluation. We split the datasets into the training set and testing set in 70% and 30%, respectively. To evaluate the performance of our model, we use the extensively used metrics: accuracy, precision, and recall. We use these metrics to prove the effectiveness of our model in fake news detection.
Accuracy is the metric that determines how correctly a model classifies the news pieces into fake and real. However, the balance in the dataset can influence the accuracy. Therefore, we use precision and recall for more efficient evaluation. Precision is the proportion of true-positive predictions to the total number of positive predictions made by the model. On the other hand, recall is defined as the true-positive predictions divided by the total number of actual positive samples.
- True Positive (TP) : when predicted fake news are actually mentioned as fake news;

- False Positive (FP) : when predicted fake news data are actually mentioned as true news;

- False Negative (FN) : when predicted true news data are actually mentioned as fake news;

- True Negative (TN) : when predicted true news data are actually mentioned as true news.

Conclusion and future works
This paper proposed a secure social media network that detects fake news effectively. FNNet uses DL to learn data representations effectively. The proposed multi-layered framework uses blockchain and DL models to build a secure social media network. This blockchain-based decentralized network ensures data immutability, network security, and content regulation. It ensures that only legitimate data is transmitted such that it reaches the receiver efficiently in an unmodified state. The FNNet model combines the outputs of CNN and Bi-LSTM models and processes them in a dense network layer to create an ensemble that makes the final prediction. The above results provide substantial evidence of the validity and applicability of the model in real-world use cases. The results indicate that the proposed model achieves state-of-the-art accuracy in detecting fake news. The blockchain layer uses the hybrid of PoW and PoS consensus algorithms to ensure that news pieces do not get modified by malicious parties. Results show that FNNet achieves the accuracy of 86.93%, 92.22%, and 98.53% on Pheme, CrisisLexT6, and ISOT datasets, respectively. The results imply that this blockchain-based DL model has significant improvement over state-of-the-art models. The results also prove the applicability of FNNet in social media networks for fake news detection. The limitation of this model is that it has been trained only with the Pheme, CrisisLexT6, and ISOT datasets. We can expand FNNet and collect more data to train our model and ensure its training on different types of data from multiple sources. This data can be collected using social media platforms like Facebook, Twitter, etc. It will not only increase the range of data sources but can also improve its accuracy for unforeseen data from new data sources.
We can integrate the proposed model with real-time APIs for a practical application for counterfeit news detection. Recently, federated learning has been extensively used as an approach for training AI without the need for any centralized server [18]. In future work, we intend to employ federated learning as a decentralized communication approach where individual models use their data for training and share the weights of only trained models with the global server. We can also employ transfer learning in this area. Furthermore, since fake news is a dynamic phenomenon, it is critical to retain the models with fresh information regularly. It is important to provide the most recent model weights and evaluation metrics to the blockchain as new articles are released and new data becomes accessible. This will help to keep the false news detection system up-to-date and flexible enough to adjust to evolving fake news trends.
