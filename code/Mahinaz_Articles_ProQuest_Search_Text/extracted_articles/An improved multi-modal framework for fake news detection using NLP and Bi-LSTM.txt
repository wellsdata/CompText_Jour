Introduction
Digital platforms together with social media have redefined relationships and information sharing, thus making news accessible worldwide. This transformation has, indeed, launched the wide promulgation of deceptive or misleading information also known as 'fake news' [1]. In addition to typical misinformation, the leading goal of fake news is to chaotically confuse, primarily to sway public opinion, focusing on either individuals or groups and thus causing social fragmentation [2]. The broad rollout of this medium represents a threat to public safety, the stability of politics, and trust within society, which destroys the achievements of democratic processes and causes dissent [3].
Investigation and capital into artificial intelligence (AI), natural language processing (NLP), and cybersecurity are concentrating more on the recognition of fake news [4]. In the last several years, escalating unease about misinformation has prompted approaches to stem its spread, notably after key events including election interference, the COVID-19 pandemic, and important geopolitical questions [5]. The recognition of an urgent requirement for better fake news detection tools by governments, organizations, and technology platforms has led to several important developments in the sector [6].
This study aims at identifying the emerging difficulties in the identification of fake news in real-time across digital platforms, given that the conventional methods are not sufficient in identifying intricate forms of misinformation such as deep fakes and click bait [7]. The evolution of techniques employed by the authors of fake news is complicating the task of maintaining the trust of the public in genuine news sources. Also, high false positive rates that are characteristic of detection systems that label truthful content as misleading erode the credibility of the systems and threaten freedom of speech [8]. A critical issue is scalability because most systems are often unable to handle content in multiple languages and cultural contexts [9]. It is therefore important to track these matters in order to regain the confidence of the public, prevent fake news and promote democratic values [10, 11–12].
The goal of this work is to design and compare a more effective approach to identifying fake news by proposing a deep learning architecture for multimodal feature learning. The goal of this framework is to increase the detection rate, confidence, and efficiency by combining several types of data from different datasets such as ISOT Fake News Dataset, LIAR Dataset and COVID-19 Fake News Dataset. This study also seeks to establish the advantage of this multimodal approach as opposed to the conventional unimodal methods that only consider a single feature of the data in order to improve both detection efficiency and understanding of the misinformation trends. The final goal is to develop reliable real-time detection systems that would help to gain the society’s trust in the information presented across various channels. The major contribution of this study are as follows:
- The study presents a new approach to the identification of fake news through the MM-FND. The proposed framework uses Word2Vec, TF-IDF, Bi-LSTM, NER, and GloVe embeddings for global, temporal, and spatial features, addressing all aspects of fake news detection.

- The study also compares the performance of the proposed method on three different datasets including ISOT Fake News Dataset, LIAR Dataset, and COVID-19 Fake News Dataset, thus making the proposed framework applicable for different scenarios.

- The proposed framework with Word2Vec, Bi-LSTM, and Random Forest feature extraction methods is more accurate, precise, and recall than the conventional models. is to boost detection accuracy, reliability, and scalability by merging a variety of data representations from different datasets.

- This study also aims to illustrate the superiority of this multimodal method compared to traditional single-feature techniques, increasing both detection accuracy and comprehension of misinformation patterns. The ultimate objective is to construct trustworthy real-time detection tools that encourage public confidence in information across a variety of platforms.

Literature review
The literature review highlights the quick (pace of) advancement in false news identification by looking at a variety of multimodal techniques, machine learning algorithms, and deep learning architecture. Every approach has improved the accuracy and detection performance, even though there are continued challenges with multimodal feature fusion, generalization, and dependency on datasets.
Shah and Kobti [13] applied a Cultural Algorithm with feature extractors for both text and images to spot fake news, gaining 9% better accuracy than existing methods. Their framework that integrates text and images worked well, although challenges existed in dealing with unstructured social networking data and representing multimodal features. Masciari et al. [14] used deep learning approaches, taking advantage of Google BERT features. The framework they developed exceeded current models even with minimal content but experienced challenges in news propagation information, which contributed to the population's tendency to trust fake news because of its characteristics.
Xue et al. [15] introduced a Multimodal Consistency Neural Network (MCNN) that has four subnetworks, one each for text, visual semantics, image tampering detection, and multimodal fusion. The network showed increased detection accuracy, but it struggled with inconsistencies between images and text and finding evidence of image tampering. Qi et al. [16] suggested an entity-enhanced framework that extracted visual entities to understand both high-level semantics and multimodal clues. Although it enhanced multimodal fake news detection by linking text and images, the existing models only addressed image semantics, which limited their performance.
Wang et al. [17] used a Meta-learning framework with neural process networks to find emergent fake news, exceeding the performance of baseline approaches. Still, this methodology had trouble with domain shifts and the hardship of obtaining significant labeled datasets. Ramya and Eswari [18] centered their research on deep neural networks (Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), and BiLSTM) that include an attention mechanism for fake news detection. Their system showed good results employing common evaluation metrics, but confirming the precision of deep learning models in challenging scenarios was still a challenge.
Shao et al. [19] created a classifier ensemble that combines results from distinct classifiers for text and image inputs, which boosts detection accuracy. Still, single-modal models were not able to fully tap into multimodal information, and multimodal models failed to exploit single-modality information. Sciucca et al. [20] introduced FakeNED, a method that joins BERT for recognizing text with VGG-16 for characterizing images, which has resulted in better performance than previous models. While it may be true, the dependence on the Fakeddit dataset and the challenges associated with merging text and image features created important challenges.
Liang et al. [21], using Text-CNN and Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) modules resulted in a 6.5% improvement in accuracy for the Weibo dataset during multimodal feature fusion. Nonetheless, this technique introduced noise and information loss during feature fusion, which led to an enhancement in the need for extra feature processing for the backbone network. According to Jayakody et al. [22], a decentralized deep learning design utilizing Federated Learning (FL) obtains 99.6% accuracy on the ISOT dataset. Even though this model was successful, the centralized training methods restricted its capacity for generalization.
Xiang [23] achieved superior results in multimodal fake news detection compared to methods such as Ensemble Adversarial Neural Network (EANN) and SpotFake by using Multimodal Compact Bilinear Pooling (MCBP) modules for textual and visual feature representation. Still, the challenge was in being dependent on pre-trained models and the involved complexity of multimodal systems. Kozal et al.'s [24] multilingual architecture for classifying false news combined deep learning and traditional models using an ensemble approach. Though it boosted performance for numerous languages, the task of adjusting to changing factual contexts and generalizing previously acquired knowledge was still quite significant.
Jawad and Obaid's [25] study on data categorization found that the accuracy rate of CNN and Deep Neural Networks (DNN) deep learning algorithms was 84.6%. Still, increasing detection precision in the disagree category continued to be a crucial challenge. To detect fake news in social media, Mareeswari and Dinesh [26] developed a hybrid approach that combines deep neural networks (CNN and LSTM), while focusing on analyzing text from Twitter. This method produced impressive results for text detection, but it was only applicable to that format and lacked a broader application.
Samadi and Momtazi [27] enhanced deep semantic representation for the detection of fake news, boosting both accuracy and F1-score on the COVID-19 dataset and outperforming baselines on Persian datasets. Still, their model pointed out the requirement for content-based features as well as semantic ones. Sengan et al. [28] launched Fakefind, a CNN + RNN combination model created to identify multimodal fake news, using stance extraction. The use of this technique revealed benefits for rumor identification, but it did not achieve a full understanding of the effects of fake news and concentrated on just certain aspects within the dataset.
Zhou et al. [30], used the CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pretraining) approach, which employs vision-language transformers to identify inconsistencies between text and images, outperforming multimodal baselines by 10% in accuracy. On the other hand, the method encountered hurdles with its robustness against adversarial assaults and the generalization of the training task. Madani et al. [30] exemplify a combined detection framework for false information that fuses techniques for feature extraction, natural language processing, and machine learning into a two-phased method. Their model, which uses a curriculum strategy alongside a k-nearest neighbor algorithm, shows better performance in accuracy than the benchmark models. Nevertheless, it has its limitations, such as inadequate information about news samples and a deficiency in subject diversity in the datasets. Findings demonstrate that applying sophisticated deep learning designs along with multimodal methods has significantly enhanced the detection of fake news. Numerous issues continue to be present notably in merging multimodal features dealing with variances between visual and textual details and confirming model stability across distinct datasets. A lot of available models encounter difficulties with durability and tend to rely on models that have been pre-trained limiting their success. Noisy data problems in feature fusion often go unnoticed. Present methods usually miss a thorough analysis of the contextual and structural details in news articles that play a vital role in boosting detection performance. Also relying only on one type of data causes models to miss important elements required for complete detection. A critical restriction is the difficulty of applying models in different domains and the deficiency of adaptable frameworks that can ensure consistent high-performance confronting shifting false information techniques and unseen datasets. We propose a MM-FND framework to rectify these deficiencies by adopting a broader array of features designed to overcome the difficulties of multimodal fusion and generalization. Using cutting-edge feature extraction strategies on the temporal and spatial dimensions of news articles deepens our insight into the information. This method increases false information detection, while reducing the effects of lost features and enhances the model's flexibility across diverse datasets. In the following sections we present the full explanation of our approach.

Dataset description
We have employed three datasets in this study to evaluate the proposed model comprehensively. The datasets include ISOT Fake news dataset, LIAR Dataset, and COVID-19 Fake News Dataset.
ISOT fake news dataset
The ISOT dataset [31] is made up of both genuine and fabricated news stories that were gathered from verified sources in the real world. In order to collect actual news pieces, the writers crawled articles from Reuters.com, which is a respectable news source. On the other hand, false news stories were collected from websites that were judged dubious and had been highlighted by Politifact and Wikipedia. A significant number of these articles were published during the years 2016 and 2017. In all, there are 44,898 occurrences included inside it. LIAR dataset
A publicly accessible dataset for identifying false news is called LIAR dataset [32]. A comprehensive analysis report and links to source papers for every instance are provided by POLITIFACT.COM, where 12.8 K hand labeled brief utterances were gathered over a ten-year period in a variety of circumstances. Research that involves fact-checking may also make use of this dataset. Interestingly, compared to other, bigger public fake news databases of a similar kind, this new dataset is orders of magnitude larger. 12.8 K human labeled brief statements from POLITIFACT.COM's API are included in the LIAR dataset4, and each statement is assessed for veracity by a POLITIFACT.COM editor.
COVID-19 fake news dataset
The COVID-19 Fake News Dataset [33] contains social media postings and media reports concerning the COVID-19 crisis. Researchers have carefully organized and identified the dataset into true or false depending on the veracity of the content. Content related to COVID-19 has been assembled from various influential social media sites including Twitter and YouTube. There are 10.700 marked posts and articles in English organized into training sets by the organizers. This pre-organization allows direct evaluation of researchers' models on the original split, while maintaining alignment with the desired structure of the dataset. Understanding and tackling the rise of false information regarding COVID-19 depends on this dataset particularly in social media. Six thousand four hundred twenty news pieces comprise the dataset with 3,360 judged as authentic and 3,060 categorized as misleading. By having a fair number of true and false news pieces it is possible for models to train more effectively in spotting COVID-19 misinformation.
Dataset preprocessing
To ensure the datasets are suitable for the MM-FND model, a series of preprocessing steps were implemented:
- Data Cleaning The ISOT and LIAR datasets along with COVID-19 Fake News were all reviewed for incorrect data. To ensure the data's quality and distinctiveness of records only relevant and unique instances remained.

- Handling Missing Values To maintain the integrity of the datasets, while dealing with missing values interpolation techniques were employed to fill in associated data.

- Text Normalization To ensure accuracy the text data received normalization processes. Every line of text shifted to lowercase to avoid case sensitivity and punctuation marks got removed. The removal of common stop words such as "the", "and", and "is" occurred because they fail to add valuable meaning to the classification process.

- Train-Test Split Three segments were created from the datasets: training consisting of 70%, validation at 15%, and a testing pool of 15%. The division allowed the model to train well and test thoroughly on new data for a precise evaluation of its results.



Multimodal feature extraction techniques
Global feature extraction using word2vec and TF-IDF models
Word2Vec [34] uses neural networks to generate dense vector representations of words, while exploring their semantic connections. It collects substantial text and converts words into a high-dimensional framework in which related words are located nearby. There are two main approaches within Word2Vec: Two methods in Word2Vec are entitled Continuous Bag of Words (CBOW) and Skip-Gram. In CBOW modeling a word is anticipated from surrounding context and with Skip-Gram modeling it anticipates context based on a word. This model succeeds in highlighting fine semantic distinctions and background details that add to its strength in complex language challenges.
The importance rating of a word in a document is determined by TF-IDF [35] based on the corpus. It calculates two values: TF assesses how often a word is used in a document and IDF decreases the relevance of frequent words shared among several documents. The fusion of these elements delivers a rank for each word showing its value which positions TF-IDF as a preferred solution for identifying vital words in large amounts of writing.
The global feature extraction process is shown in Fig. 2 using the Word2Vec and TF-IDF models essential for transforming raw text into relevant representations for detecting fake news. The process is divided into three distinct steps: At the outset of this process is a labeled trace collection T L comprising known instances of real and fake news. T U denotes the unlabeled dataset. Each trace in T L is changed into a set of text elements labeled b ( t ) . The TF-IDF model along with Word2Vec models are used to identify features from these traces so that the models can understand the organized data represented in text. The system gathers semantic vectors and calculates the weights related to the text elements found in bag b ( t ) . All text elements in the collection receive examination while the TF-IDF model is used to determine their weight. These text elements are converted into heavy semantic vectors by using the Word2Vec model. Both statistical and semantic insights from the textual data are merged through this approach to boost feature representation. During Step 3 the model amalgamates the weights derived from TF-IDF alongside those from Word2Vec. Each weight is combined with its associated semantic vector and these products are totaled to create a final trace representation f ( t ) for each trace in both labeled T L and unlabeled datasets T U . This developed representation exists as a complete attribute vector that embodies the vital features of the source textual information. [See PDF for image]

Temporal feature extraction using Bi-LSTM
Temporal features in sequential data are gathered by applying a Bi-LSTM network. In contrast to conventional LSTM models, this version of Bi-LSTM recognizes dependencies in the temporal sequence, while emphasizing a forward and backward analysis for effective sequential task solutions. The time-dependent qualities of the data prompt use of Bi-LSTM to identify detailed temporal interactions. Both layers of the Bi-LSTM framework analyze the sequence moving forward and backward. Utilizing this approach permits the model to retain data insights from previous and following time intervals which strengthens its comprehension of temporal dynamics among data elements. Both forward and backward LSTM cells receive each data point to generate hidden state vectors. By joining the hidden states together, we obtain a more complete representation of time-related features at every time step. Temporal features obtained from the Bi-LSTM model play a key role in grasping the development of patterns throughout time. Using this technique lets the model grasp temporal dynamics shown by earlier information affecting later content. Subsequent tasks depend on these temporal features for classification and pattern identification.

Spatial feature extraction
Algorithm 1 presents the Spatial Information Extraction begins with the GET_COUNTRY_INFO procedure, which processes the news articles stored in the news_db dataset. The algorithm follows several key steps for efficient extraction and encoding of spatial information:
- Tokenization The algorithm starts by breaking the news articles into individual sentences. Each sentence is then further tokenized into words, a process that transforms the raw text into a structured format suitable for subsequent computational tasks.

- Part-of-Speech (POS) Tagging In POS tagging after tokenization every word is assigned a label indicating its grammatical definition. Classifying proper nouns is crucial as they generally relate to geographical places such as cities and countries.

- NER An entity identifier is employed by the algorithm to spot Geopolitical Entities (GPE), which encompasses countries and urban areas. Using specific entity types such as 'GPE', the NER method connects with a predefined vocabulary from a Natural Language Processing library to extract only location entities from the text.

- Country Recognition and Encoding The identified entities match with a database of acknowledged countries (e.g., pycountry). When an identified entity qualifies as a valid country it gets placed in the location[i] array to keep only significant geographic data for later processing.

- Embedding Locations At the end the discovered locations receive GloVe encoding. By embedding geographic locations into vectors GloVe identifies the semantic links between different areas. Such vectors operate as location features in machine learning algorithms for additional purposes including categorization or anticipating results.



This structured approach ensures precise extraction and representation of geographical data from the news articles, facilitating its use in various analytical and predictive tasks. [See PDF for image]

Random forest based classification
The false news classification is the next important task after the multimodal feature extraction process. To reach this aim, the Random Forest (RF) [37] algorithm is chosen for its durability and its capability to address complex feature input. RF serves as a collection of decision trees, where training generates many of them and after training reveals the class that occurs the most. In this case the features gathered from different modalities enter the RF classifier. The dataset identifies three kinds of features: spatial vectors derived from GloVe embeddings highlighting geographical characteristics and temporal features outlined by the Bi-LSTM model that illustrates temporal dependencies. By combining these features in a single representation of the data they boost the classification task's reliability. The Random Forest algorithm launches the algorithm by forming various decision trees using randomly picked features from the multimodal feature space. All trees evaluate the input data individually and offer predictions rooted in the decision boundaries they have established. Different instances of the training data are utilized to train each tree, where only a random feature set is analyzed for division at every node. By selecting features and data subsets at random the chance of overfitting decreases because each tree observes the data in a unique way.
Model training
Upon complete feature extraction both spatially and temporally the dataset gets divided into training and testing sets. To prepare the model، the machine learning algorithm interacts with training data and its characteristics are judged on the testing data as the hyperparameters are refined. For this task the RF algorithm has been prioritized because of its adaptability to deal with extensive feature sets. At the start of training the dataset experiences processing to normalize and scale the features to common values. The Random Forest model receives the training data to create multiple decision trees using a set of drawn-down dataset samples. At every split during training of each tree random features are selected to mitigate overfitting and boost generalization.
To achieve optimal performance for the model during training is a key step of hyperparameter tuning. Unlike many models Random Forest possesses various essential factors that call for optimization each including the number of trees (n_estimators). Changing these hyperparameters can drastically impact the model's ability to generalize and minimize overfitting. During analysis of this study Grid Search is utilized to adjust hyperparameters with cross-validation. This approach completely investigates a predetermined parameter space and assesses each variant of hyperparameters to choose the optimal configuration according to cross-validation tests. This study adopts k-fold cross-validation with k frequently defined as 5 or 10. For k iterations of the model evaluation process a separate subset serves as validation each time.
The following grid of hyperparameters is used in the Grid Search process for the Random Forest model:
- n_estimators Decision trees are usually assessed within a set of values (such as 50–200).

- max_depth There is a range of values tested for tree depth including 10 and 30.

- min_samples_split Samples at levels 2 and 5 indicate the absolute minimum necessary to create a split in a node.

- max_features "auto" identifies every feature in evaluation,'sqrt' uses the square root of the features total,'log2' associates with the total number of features options.



By iterating through all collections of these hyperparameters during the Grid Search process and evaluating the model with the designated cross-validation technique for each set. When the optimal hyperparameters are established the Random Forest model receives a full retraining on the complete training dataset.

Evaluation metrics for performance assessment
To evaluate the proposed model performance in detecting false news, we use several key performance metrics. These metrics help us understand how accurately the model identifies both true and fake news. The key metrics are:
Accuracy
Accuracy is a measurement that determines the proportion of accurate predictions (both true and fake) that are made by the model out of all of the predictions that it makes. Also, it provides an overall perspective on the performance of the model. 9
Accuracy = TP + TN TP + TN + FP + FN
TP  = True Positives, TN = True Negatives, FN = False Negatives, FP = False Positives
Precision
Precision measures the accuracy of the model in identifying true positives out of all the instances it predicted as positive (i.e., fake news). It focuses on the false positives to provide insight into how reliable the model’s predictions of fake news are. 10
Precision = TP TP + FP
Recall
Sensitivity, or recall, is the ratio of real fake news that were classified by the model as such. It is used to measure the number of actual fake news the model is missing and it emphasizes false negatives. 11
Recall = TP TP + FN
F1-Score
The F1-Score is equal and averages precision and recall, thus considering false positives and false negatives alike. That is most helpful when there is a skewed class distribution or if both precision and recall rates are critical. 12
F 1 - Score = 2 × Precision × Recall Precision + Recall

Discussion
This study presents a novel approach to detecting fake news through the MM-FND framework, integrating the ISOT, LIAR, and COVID-19 datasets. The results indicate that the proposed method significantly outperforms existing models in terms of accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score. These findings highlight the effectiveness of leveraging multimodal feature extraction techniques, including Word2Vec, TF-IDF, Bi-LSTM, and GloVe embeddings, to enhance the detection of misinformation.
The practical implications of this study are meaningful in the case of mis/disinformation warfare. As the fake news become more commonplace, especially during the important events like the COVID-19 outbreak, efficient detection techniques should be employed in order to protect the society. The proposed framework, MM-FND, can certainly be employed by media organizations, fact-checking agencies and social media platforms for better identification of misinformation. Combining different methods of feature extraction, the stakeholders can build more effective systems that can successfully counter the future changes in the fake news scenario. In addition, the proposed approach can be extended to other domains different from news, including financial fraud detection and disinformation in political campaigns.
The limitations of this study are as follows: The use of ISOT, LIAR and COVID-19 datasets may limit the extent to which the study findings can be generalized due to the exclusion of all fake news types that may be encountered in practice. Furthermore, while the methods of feature extraction based on combinations of multiple modalities improve the detection rate, they can be slow to adapt to new misinfo techniques. Further, the constant update of these methods will be required to continue the practice effectively as the information environment evolves constantly.
Conclusion
The current work presents a new solution to fake news detection using the proposed MM-FND framework that incorporates the ISOT, LIAR, and COVID-19 datasets. In the present study, Word2Vec, TF-IDF, Bi-LSTM, and GloVe embeddings, along with a Random Forest classifier, have been used as feature extraction methods and a classification algorithm, respectively; the proposed framework yields optimal performance in terms of accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. Particularly, it formed 96.3%, 95.6% and 97.1% accuracies on the respective databases proving the effectiveness and flexibility of the proposed MM-FND model. The proposed framework shows that incorporating multimodal features improves the detection effectiveness, presenting a sound approach to managing misinformation in the context of online platforms. Such outcomes prove the opportunity of the proposed framework for fake news detection and the efficiency of multimodal approach. The next steps in this research should consider combining with other datasets for better generalization and applicability in other settings. Furthermore, real time detection can be extended to include online learning which enables the model to learn new misrepresentation patterns in real time. Exploring the generalizability of the MM-FND framework in various languages and cultures could also be informative when trying to address the misinformation problem worldwide.
