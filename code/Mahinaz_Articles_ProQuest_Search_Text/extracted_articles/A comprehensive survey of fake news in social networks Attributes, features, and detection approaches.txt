1. Introduction Fake news is not a recent development; it existed before Christ (BC) (Mason et al., 2018). The spread was initiated in 1439 (Jacob et al., 2016). It is becoming widespread in newspaper articles, television, and some other traditional media. Following that, the explosion of the World Wide Web (WWW) in the mid-1990s significantly developed. Due to its convenience, quick speed, and low cost, social media has emerged as the primary medium for online human interaction and the transmission of information (Shu et al., 2017b).
Fake news is defined as “altered truths”, which shows that the media’s motivations are real (Shrivastava et al., 2020). The propagation of fake news stories was noticeable throughout the crucial days of the 2016 U.S. presidential election (Craig Silverman, 2016). During that time, 19 million bot profiles were created to spread fake news about Trump and Clinton (Sansonetti et al., 2020). Although, there were 8,711,000 Facebook shares, comments, and opinions on the 20 most popularly discussed bogus articles and comments. Moreover, more than the 7,367,000 provided by 19 major media organizations for 20 hotly disputed presidential candidates (Craig Silverman, 2016). Approximately two-thirds of American people acquire information from internet sources, and this percentage is steadily increasing (Jeffery Gottfried, 2016). These sources are used to spread fake news, fake marketing, fake rumors, fake political comments, spoofs, and many more. Fake news is becoming increasingly prevalent and frequently disseminated on social media, apart from traditional platforms (Rapoza, 2017).
As an example, consider the hundreds of youngsters in the Macedonian town of Veles. Who produced false information aimed at lots of people on shared mass media and then grew rich through pay-per-click publicity throughout the US presidential election (Smith and Banic‘, 2016). Fake news mainly draws the interest of individuals who are more engaged in political conversations and global market values, and it may have an impact on their psychological health, leading to tension and fear. And also it can have a terrible impact on both people and governments. Furthermore, it has been reported that false news gets more views on social media than actual news, with evidence for this assertion appearing on well-established social media websites. The situation of fake news is more serious than that of other types of data. Additionally, as a result of the repeated characteristics of fake news, people begin to believe it, whether it supports their existing beliefs or not (Zhou and Zafarani, 2020).
This perception and attitude can occasionally be shaped by social conditioning (Rohera et al., 2022a). Fake news is intentionally created to fool people by manipulating facts and data. Numerous patterns of genuine desire are imitated as fake news to deceive the public (Shu et al., 2017a). There’s a chance that misinformation will use actual news in the incorrect context to illustrate it (Zhu et al., 2020). Despite the efforts to identify fake news, various online fact-checking services are available on the internet, such as FactCheck.org, and PoltiFact.com, and there are also more manual identification procedures by experts. For instance, a creator fooling an online false reviewer can alter just a few phrases in an online comment. As a result, semantic similarity evaluation offers a helpful method for identifying whether news content that has been duplicated or partially copied and altered by malicious writers. It may also be used to recognize probable false news.

- Cluster analysis: Online information is studied using cluster analysis without labeling input. Information can be grouped using the maximization of intraclass similarities and minimization of interclass similarities in clustering algorithms, which can also produce classifiers for a collection of data (Lu et al., 2019). In particular, cluster analysis can be used to detect homogeneous specific groupings of news and writers in fake news identification.

- Outlier analysis: The research of identifying information for unusual activities is known as outlier analysis. Depending upon the statistical metrics, the outlier analysis can reveal misleading information and suspect writers, length measurements, and procedures are based on the density (Minh et al., 2022).

- Unsupervised news embedding: Social media is a text-based use in sentiment classification, semantic similarity analysis, and other activities related to fake news. That is crucial to identifying fake news on social media. Natural language processing (NLP) needs the process known as embedding, which involves acquiring extensive participation from analyzed text content. The quantitative representation can then be used as an initial point for more analysis aimed to detect fake news. The features of the information can be captured using various embedding technologies from different angles. To determine the exact nature of the information and identify misleading information, it is important to use an effective embedding approach. Word2vec is the popular unsupervised-based integrating technique (Naredla and Adedoyin, 2022). The author (Bojanowski et al., 2017) explained about FastText is used to development of supervised and unsupervised learning algorithms to produce word vector representation. Sent2Vec is a general-purpose sentence embedding that can be learned to use an unsupervised model (Li et al., 2021). Doc2Vec model represents each document as a vector in (De Souza et al., 2022).

Studies and development in Deep Learning (DL) may be able to assist with identifying fake news (Hu et al., 2022). The author (Meel and Vishwakarma, 2020) described that different approaches to Deep Learning exist and are increasingly dynamic. Online information (text and/or graphics) is frequently first incorporated at the level of words within a Deep Learning architecture (for text) (Yao et al., 2018), or as pixels or tensors (for image). In the fields of visual object recognition and speech recognition, deep learning algorithms have obtained impressive results (Zhou and Zafarani, 2020; Dong et al., 2020). Unlike traditional machine learning methods, which depend on manually extracting features, this technique can be used to automatically identify representations of original information (Dong et al., 2020).
Deep learning (DL) techniques, such as convolutional neural network (CNN) are used to detect features in both text and images described in (Huang et al., 2017), visual geometry group (VGG)-16/19 is used in (Simonyan and Zisserman, 2014), Text-CNN based approach for detecting fake news in (Umer et al., 2020), and healthcare issues are explained in (Upadhyay et al., 2022). 7. Datasets used for fake news detection One of the essential components for creating a successful supervised learning model is the quality dataset. We identified the various datasets for fake news identification. In Table 6, we listed fake news data sets from various research articles that have already been examined, along with the classification algorithms. We will explore different bogus news datasets below.
- Burfoot Satire News Dataset: In (Golbeck et al., 2018) datasets were produced dynamically 4000 examples of actual news and 233 samples of satirical news. The actual news items are gathered through news service document samples from the English Gigaword Lexicon, parody news items which are deeply linked in content to the genuine ones, are considered (Xu et al., 2022a).

- Credbank Dataset: This source is a collection of live tweets received between October 2014 and February 2015. The whole dataset contains over 60 million tweets wrapping, 1049 facts, and the integrity of the tweets is assessed by 30 labels (Mitra and Gilbert, 2015). Recently, The author (Garg and Sharma, 2022) used linguistic features of the Credbank dataset to get good accuracy.

- FakeNewsNet: This dataset is available in (Shu et al., 2017a) research article, it includes 211 false news stories and 211 factual news stories culled from BuzzFeed.com and PolitiFact.com. It also has significant aspects, including publisher identification, article content, and social engagement data for every news sample (Sansonetti et al., 2020).

- BuzzFeed News: The dataset includes over 2000 news items from Facebook which were posted in September 2016 (Xu et al., 2022a). These news samples have been confirmed by BuzzFeed’s reporters and editors and classified as mostly trustworthy, not verifiable facts, a combination of true and false information, or primarily untrue. Additionally, this dataset offers additional necessary information for every news example, including the URL of the news piece, available evidence, the number of shares, responses, and opinions.

- Fake News Challenge dataset: This collection has 50,000 ”stance” pairs, each of which includes a headline story in (Riedel et al., 2017). Stakeholder-based evaluation can also assist in identifying potentially incorrect information by looking at the coherence between both the headline and the content (Vishwakarma et al., 2023).

- LIAR: In (Dong et al., 2020) the information is presented and suggested for the identification of fake news online. There were 12,800 concise assertions from PolitiFact.com in a combination of settings that have been carefully labeled. Every sample selection is assigned one of six performance scores: genuine, mainly valid, partially accurate, nearly correct, or fake. Every example usually includes a thorough, detailed report and references to original materials.

- Benjamin Political News Dataset: This dataset was generated for detecting online election campaigns and satirical articles (Xu et al., 2022a). This dataset includes 75 articles from actual news classifications, phony, and parody. The bogus news sources are drawn from Zimdar’s listing of deceptive and inaccurate media websites. All authentic resources are selected from the Financial Industry insider ”Highly Reliable” category.

9. Discussion and future research direction The popularity of information sharing among online users makes online social networks (OSN) a security and privacy area for research. Optimizing solutions are implemented by security researchers consistently. However, online social media platforms still have some issues that have to be identified and solve. In the online social network (OSN), identifying fake news is the main challenge, specifically on Twitter and Facebook. We require an appropriate system that recognizes the fake news on social media platforms to detect it.
Moreover, the problem is posting images, videos, and advertisements from various third-party services that contain harmful information. Each of these will divert users to different websites when they view them. To prevent third-party activities, it is challenging to find irrelevant content in posts on OSNs. In addition, no approach explains the functionality of the OSN system after the implementation of security measures. Some of the information displayed in postings is intended to deceive, divert, or access malicious websites that contain fake data. This should be avoided by employing some strategy, such as identifying fake news. Furthermore, most of the defensive strategies we have researched focus on news content to protect user text from fraudulent users, yet these strategies fail short of automatically preventing the propagation of fake news. To avoid this issue, using a variety of hybrid approaches, reinforcement learning algorithms, and many more blockchain technologies will protect the online user’s content. Combining these approaches with existing solutions and frameworks will result in a more effective solution and security approach against OSN threats.
The exploration of technologies for detecting fake news continues to be in its beginning stages. Nevertheless, these classifications will increase the ability to identify fake news. More research is required to improve the safety and confidentiality of OSNs. Comparing the existing security solution will be implemented by various social media platforms, highlighting the advantages and disadvantages and recommending obvious strategies for improving privacy and security.
10. Conclusion People spend their precious time browsing the internet and using OSN to complete everyday tasks. Online users like using OSN to contact other people to exchange their experiences and information, including pictures, videos, text, and much more, without regard to distance or cost. Meanwhile, the OSN services expose their customers to major security concerns. We demonstrated many scenarios connected to fake news on OSN and their solutions using various methodologies and detection techniques that defend online readers from numerous challenges. We also discuss some exercise methods that will assist the individual in defending against various fake news attributes, features, and creators of that fake news. Furthermore, we have highlighted the various solutions and their comparisons to specific situations that meet the user’s requirements and educate people about detection techniques of fake news on social media networks.
The majority of these defensive strategies provide solutions by using machine learning and some deep learning algorithms in consideration of the unique privacy issues. Meanwhile, several security issues are still unresolved. In addition to a protective approach, parents must watch their children’s activity while they use the internet and OSN platforms. Therefore, researchers can do more research on OSN’s defensive mechanism against all challenges. However, certain concerns connected to persistent and advanced threats and social risks must be addressed using a hybrid approach, framework, and automatic detection approaches.
The contributions of our survey include attributes and news content-based fake news. We have reviewed knowledge-based and style-based approaches to identify fake news on online social networks. Our survey also focuses on features and detection approaches to identify fake news.
In the future, hybrid approaches of machine learning and deep learning, reinforcement learning, and blockchain-based models have fewer possibilities of producing more accurate outcomes. We strongly believe that this study will give researchers identify misleading news more accurately in different online social networks.
