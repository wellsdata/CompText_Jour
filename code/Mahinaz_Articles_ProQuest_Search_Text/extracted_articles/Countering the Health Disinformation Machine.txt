On the morning of August 31, 2022, a staff member of the Fenway Institute’s National LGBTQIA+ Health Education Center in Boston, which I direct, checked the office voicemail system. One voice message was anything but routine. “You sick [expletives], you’re all gonna burn. There’s a group of people on their way to handle [victim]. You signed your own warrant, [victim]. Castrating our children. You’ve woken up enough people. And upset enough of us. And you signed your own ticket. Sleep well, you [expletive].” The staff member reported this message to the organization’s leaders and the security department, which notified law enforcement.
Three months later, on December 2, the U.S. Attorney’s Office announced that the Federal Bureau of Investigation had arrested a 38-year-old owner of an ammunition business from Comfort, Texas, who was charged with transmitting interstate threats. 1 At the time, I hoped naively that coverage of the arrest by the New York Times , the Washington Post , CNN , and other news organizations might deter threats against transgender and gender-diverse young people, their families, the clinicians who care for them, and the educators who teach clinicians how to do so. Little did I know that the new year would bring me a crash course on the health disinformation machine.
Since 2016, Harvard Medical School (HMS) has offered an advanced course called “Caring for patients with diverse sexual orientations, gender identities, and sex development: a clinical and scholarly elective.” 2 As its director for 6 years, I had encountered no resistance or objections. On January 10, 2023, the College Fix , an online outlet that engages college students to write for right-wing media, published “Harvard med class focuses on LGBTQIA+ ‘infants’ and older: course is directed by LGBT activist.” The article showed the relevant page of the HMS course catalogue and described me as “an outspoken supporter of removing healthy organs from individuals and injecting them with puberty blockers.”
Within 24 hours, Britain’s Daily Mail had picked up the narrative and published “Harvard Medical School offers course about healthcare for LGBTQIA+ ‘infants’ with curriculum offering lessons in areas including OBGYN, pediatrics and plastic surgery.” This article claimed that HMS medical students “are being taught how to care for infant patients who identify as LGBTQIA+” and included a large headshot of me. Also that day, Fox News published “Harvard Medical School offers course about healthcare for LGBTQIA+ ‘infants.’” Two days later, Fox & Friends broadcast a nearly 5-minute segment to more than 1.5 million morning-show viewers entitled “Harvard offering class on LGBTQ+ infant health care,” with commentators twice describing the course as “insane.” The host wrapped up the segment by inquiring, “Why are the words ‘infant’ and ‘sexual orientation’ anywhere near each other in the same document? I have an infant right now. We are not focused on sexual orientation in her learning.”
When the first few stories emerged, I collaborated with HMS’s Office of Communications and External Relations, in partnership with GLAAD, an LGBTQIA+ rights–focused media-monitoring organization, to craft HMS’s response, which explained that “in this context, care for infants refers specifically to physical variations in sex development that arise in utero and are present at birth. These include chromosomal, gonadal, and anatomical variations, all of which are relevant to medical care and treatment to ensure healthy development.”
Although HMS issued this explanation online as an official institutional statement and sent it directly to the College Fix , the Daily Mail , Fox News , and other media outlets, these organizations did not remove misleading stories or even correct false headlines, articles, or recorded broadcasts, which could still be found online more than 6 months later. Moreover, dozens of additional media stories appeared in the United States and elsewhere in the world, in several languages, stating that the course teaches about “LGBT babies,” “sexual identities…of infants,” or variations thereof, with thinly veiled insinuations or explicit accusations of grooming and sexualizing infants.
This narrative went viral on social media, where one Twitter (now X) influencer’s post claiming that “Harvard is teaching medical students about transgender infants” received nearly 10,000 likes, while a Facebook user commented on a blog entry called “Harvard training medical students to treat ‘trans infants’” by warning, “In case no one told you, they’re after your kids.” Much of this media activity featured the course-catalogue page, which included my work email address so that students could request permission to enroll. An onslaught of hostile messages ensued. Guidance from HMS Security and Campus Safety, Massachusetts General Hospital Police and Security Services, the Boston Police Department, and other experts alerted me to the need for removing my personal information from the Internet to avoid being “doxxed” (having my private information publicized) or “swatted” (having armed officers arrive at my home to respond to a false report of a crime), protecting my home environment and loved ones, and obtaining security escorts when on campus.
When activity in the disinformation echo chamber reached fever pitch, a journalist from the Associated Press Fact Check team reached out to ask what the course really teaches. The reporter carefully researched the course’s content and interviewed external medical specialists and community leaders about intersex health. The Associated Press then published “Harvard med school class isn’t about ‘trans infants,’” which concluded that the claim that our course “trains students to treat transgender infants” is “false” and methodically clarified the distinction between transgender and intersex concepts, terminology, and medical needs. 3,4 Several reputable news organizations followed suit, including USA Today , which published “Fact check: Harvard course teaches infant sex development, not gender identity.”
One reporter notified us about an Instagram post in which Donald Trump Jr., who has more than 6 million followers, criticized the HMS course, linked to the song “Down with the Sickness” by the band Disturbed, and wrote, “It’s getting demonic…in my humble opinion. Fact check disclaimer it’s my opinion and someone will argue it’s not demonic but it’s my opinion. That said why on earth would an infant need this? Seriously. Only answers from people who don’t wear masks in cars by themselves in 2023 please.” The post had received more than 92,000 likes. Within 3 days after we learned of the post, Instagram flagged it as false information; within a week, they had removed it altogether.
Two months later, when another Twitter influencer reintroduced the false narrative, Reuters Fact Check investigated the claim. On March 14, Reuters published a piece called “Harvard course teaches sex development in children, not gender identity or sexual orientation.” Since then, attacks on social media seem to have largely subsided.
The initial coverage of the course illustrates the capacity of a far-reaching, systematic disinformation machine that crosslinks written press, social media, and broadcasting outlets to amplify and disseminate falsehoods around the world at dizzying speed. This media ecosystem enables the advancement of an anti-LGBTQIA+ political agenda with devastating consequences, including proliferation of legal restrictions on necessary care for transgender and gender-diverse young people 5 and threats against clinicians and educators in the field.
In this instance, the ability to withstand and counter the health disinformation campaign hinged on several key advantages: principled support for academic freedom from medical school and hospital leaders, unwavering commitment from funders, communications staff who heeded strategic guidance from an LGBTQIA+ community organization, responsive security services and law-enforcement agencies, diligent fact checking by journalists, and a social media platform’s policy of flagging and removing disinformation.
I emerged with my personal safety, job, and course intact. Working throughout the United States and in several countries around the world, I have met hundreds of clinicians and educators focused on LGBTQIA+ health who fend for themselves, without the privilege of multipronged structural backing. Millions of LGBTQIA+ people face threats to or denials of medically necessary, evidence-based care owing to escalating health disinformation.
Defending against this harm demands the same degree of coordination involved in the machine that caused it. Such efforts require subject-matter experts to vocally denounce falsehoods, institutions to stand with faculty who are under attack, and media leaders to ensure information integrity. Only with this dedicated collaboration can we preserve the truth on which our patients’ lives depend.
