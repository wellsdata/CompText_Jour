Risk Analysis https://doi.org/10.1111/risa.13980 (2022)
Fake news is a growing problem that can influence public perceptions of numerous issues. As nations look to introduce new technologies as part of decarbonization efforts, fake news may bring fresh concerns to the evolution of energy systems. One technology of particular interest in this sense is nuclear power, which is already the subject of much public debate. Now, Shirley Ho and colleagues at Nanyang Technological University examine how fake news could influence perceptions of nuclear power, and what impact fact-checking and online discussions have in mitigating it.
The researchers undertook online surveys of Singaporean citizens. Singapore does not currently generate any nuclear power, although it has discussed its introduction. Ho and colleagues randomly assigned participants to one of six different conditions. All participants were presented with a fake news item about the environmental and health risks of nuclear power. Half of them were invited to an online discussion about the news article; the other half were not. Within each of these two conditions, the participants were shown the fake news only, or the fake news plus a fact-checking article. The fact check was presented as coming from either The Straits Times (the most widely-circulated local newspaper) or Factually (a state-run fact-check website). Participants were then surveyed on their risk perceptions of nuclear power.
The survey results revealed an interaction effect between online discussion and fact checking: the two variables do not independently influence risk perceptions. For those who received fact checks, the source of the check played a role in perceptions: those receiving checks from Factually had a lower risk perception if they also participated in a discussion compared to those who did not; the opposite was true for those receiving The Straits Times checks. Although more work is required to understand the nature of these effects, the study highlights the importance of fact-checking strategy in addressing fake news around energy technology.
