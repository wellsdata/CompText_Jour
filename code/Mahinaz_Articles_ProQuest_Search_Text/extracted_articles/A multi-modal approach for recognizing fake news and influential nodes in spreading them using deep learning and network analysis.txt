Introduction Social networks, as one of the new platforms for the formation of media in different scales and provided the context for the rapid publication of information and news. This feature, along with the undeniable benefits of social networks, opened up the space for publishing news and unreliable content and spreading rumors 1,2 . The massive amount of information sent in social networks has attracted increasing attention due to the various fields of application that can be used. In fact, these virtual communities enable users to express their opinions and also influence other users. Therefore, it is very important to identify the key influencers of the social network and the associations formed around a topic, using the social network analysis method 3,4 . Social networks have changed the way people communicate. Millions of people around the world use social media to create, share and discuss information and network. Anyone connected to the Internet can share their experiences through a short film, present their personal opinion through a tweet, or show the world something interesting through a picture. This has caused social network analysis to grow exponentially in the past years due to the amount of data that is constantly being shared on the Internet 5,6 .
Rumor spreading is one of the basic mechanisms for spreading information in social networks. Rumors are one of the most important problems that are spreading in virtual networks, which have destructive effects, and investigating its causes and factors is very important 7,8 . The term “rumor” often refers to the unsubstantiated interpretation of public problems, events, and concerns that are disseminated through a variety of channels. The Internet has grown so quickly that Online Social Networks (OSN) have become ingrained in people’s daily lives. But a string of falsehoods ruin people’s lives and interfere with their regular activities in society and on the Internet. In contrast to social gossip, online rumors proliferate far more quickly. There are more unpredictable elements and a broader range of influence behind the propagation of these tales. Thus, it is crucial to verify the guidelines for disseminating rumors in the intricate networks that social media platforms offer in order to effectively manage rumors 9, 10–11 .
When dealing with false information messages, the general public thinks less and takes less trouble to check the correctness of the content 12,13 . The ease with which anybody may publish, share, or compile information regarding breaking news on social media makes it a vital source of current information. There is a tremendous quantity of fresh information created every second as a result of the users of these networks’ flexibility in sharing and exchanging information 14,15 . In today’s interconnected world, where information moves at unprecedented speeds, understanding the dynamics of gossip in complex networks has become increasingly critical.
The emergence of social media platforms and online communities has amplified the scope and impact of gossip, making it necessary to explore and understand the underlying mechanisms of rumor dissemination 7,9 . One of the major aspects of this research is the dynamics of information propagation in complex networks. Meanwhile, rumors are also exchanged in these networks as a form of information 16 . Social networking services are what structure OSNs. Their impact on people’s lifestyles is profound. These networks are sometimes referred to as small-world or scale-free networks. The dynamics of information propagation in complex networks is a key component of this study. In the meantime, rumors are also spread in these networks as a type of information. A rumor can cause panic and lead to psychological and economic damage to society. In general, with the rapid emergence of OSNs, rumor transmission is growing and has a significant impact on social stability and people’s lives. Researchers in this subject have proposed a number of models to address the phenomena of rumors spreading; these models are frequently based on epidemiological models such as susceptible-infected-recovered (SIR), susceptible-infected-susceptible (SIS), and susceptible-infected (SI). In this regard, dynamic models of infectious illnesses, such as SI, SIS, SIR, etc., are used in many classical models for the examination of information flow. These models have been constructed by researchers in many domains according to their study settings. In addition to the classic form, rumors may now spread more swiftly and readily in social networks due to technological advancements and the notable rise of online social media 7,17,18 .
A lot of research has been done on the issue of spreading rumors in social networks. In this section, some of the latest related researches are introduced. For this purpose, the studies can be analyzed in two different sections. At first, a number of studies related to the identification of rumors in social networks are discussed. In order to identify bogus news on social media, Kaliyar et al. 19 suggested a hybrid method that combines a single-layer deep convolutional neural network (CNN) with a BERT-based DL model. The results of the model evaluation showed that their proposed model has an accuracy of 98.90%. Jain et al. 20 collected news using machine learning (ML) and natural language processing to detect fake news in social media. Then they checked whether they are fake or real using the support vector machine algorithm. Comparing the results of their proposed approach with other models showed the high accuracy of the model. Ozbay and Alatas used the K-Nearest Neighbor classification method to study and identify bogus news in social media. The model evaluation’s findings demonstrated that this model’s accuracy in identifying false news is tolerable 21 . Kotteti et al. 22 used a deep learning (DL) set in the time series representation of tweets to detect rumors in social media. This model can identify rumors using time series vector representation and is based on the majority vote technique on a collection of neural network predictions. The model assessment results demonstrate that the model performs well in identifying rumors 22 . Madani et al. 23 used an approach based on natural language processing, ML and DL to detect and identify fake news in tweets. The results of the research showed that their proposed approach using random forest algorithm has appropriate accuracy.
A variety of research linked to the identification of influential nodes and the path of propagating rumors in the network are covered in the following sections. These works use a graph-based approach to the topic. Each network consists of nodes and edges. Xu et al. 24 used the content features of tweets by following the non-sequential distribution structure and creating powerful representations to identify different types of rumors 10 . In this method, two recurrent neural network models based on decision tree with top-down and bottom-up approaches are presented for learning features and classifying rumors. The results show the superiority of recurrent neural network over previous approaches with an accuracy of more than 72%. Jain and Katarya 25 identified community structure in OSN using modified Louvain method. Then, in order to find the opinion leader, they used the modified firefly algorithm. They implemented their proposed approach on synthesized data and real data. The results of the research showed that their proposed approach in analyzing social network is highly accurate. Zareie et al. 26 investigated the topic of detecting influential users in social networks in the form of an optimization problem. This problem was expressed in the form of a set of cost functions and distances between nodes in the network. Also, the gray wolf algorithm was used to optimize this problem. The results of the case study showed that the proposed approach was effective in identifying users with appropriate accuracy 26 . In order to simulate the spread of rumors in intricate social networks, Yang et al. 27 introduced a competitive diffusion model that was based on the Linear Threshold model with One Direction state transition (LT1DT). The issue of reducing the propagation of rumors in social networks is examined in this work, and LT1DT implements a novel strategy based on the dynamics of the diffusion. Ma et al. 28 have addressed the challenge of detecting rumors in complex networks. Considering that rumors are scattered among a lot of data, the combination of various data should be compatible with each other 9 . The reviewed literature reveals several gaps in the literature that our proposed model fills in as follows: Other research works address the problem of rumor identification and influential node detection independently, while our model solves both problems simultaneously, which offers a better understanding of the rumor spreading. Most research investigations fail to consider the effects of the network architecture on rumor diffusion. In our model, we apply graph analysis to capture the effects of the connection between nodes on the spread of rumors. Previous approaches do not account for the temporal aspect of rumor dissemination. In addition, our model takes into account the time dimension to know the probable paths of the rumor spread. To sum up, the proposed model gives a new approach which can overcome these shortcomings by including rumor identification, influential node identification, deep learning techniques and network topology analysis. It is believed that such an approach will yield useful information about rumor dissemination and help in designing appropriate countermeasures.
Our research offers several significant contributions to the field of rumor propagation in social networks:
- Integrated Approach: In this paper, we develop a complete framework that covers the identification of rumors, the identification of influential nodes, and the prediction of message paths, which can give a systematic understanding of the rumor dissemination.

- Deep Learning Application: Our model uses deep learning approaches for rumor detection and identification of influential nodes which may enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of the approach compared to previous methods.

- Graph-Based Analysis: We leverage graph-based analysis to model the influence of topology on the spread of rumors and make the model more realistic by modeling the social networks.

- Temporal Considerations: Our model takes into account the time factor in the spreading of rumors and hence can predict the paths through which rumors spread more accurately.

Materials and methods A social network is often represented as a graph with a high number of nodes and edges. Users are shown as graph nodes in the network graph, with interactions between them represented by edges. Multiple communities can be found inside a single social network. A subgraph that has more internal connections than the remainder of the graph is referred to as a community. It is indeed true that a node in a community shares more neighbors with other nodes in that community than with any other node in the network. Thus, a community is presumably defined as a collection of nodes with a greater number of common neighbors than the average common neighbors of all nodes. The definitions utilized in this study will be discussed first, followed by an explanation of the data structure and the phases of the suggested strategy for locating prominent nodes and rumor-spreading pathways in social networks.

Description of dataset In the present research, a data set including messages published on Twitter network related to the Covid-19 disease has been used. This database contains 99,696,204 tweets involving 156,487 users and includes 28,699,842 replays and 11,408,918 retweets along with more than 443 K temporal links. Only data pertaining to a single year has been utilized in this dataset due to its big size. By doing this, the number of users who have been active in the period of one year, decreased to 13,819 users. In this regard, the act of identifying the effective users in spreading the rumor has been done in 3 periods of 120 days. Thus; The data is separated into three 120 days sections and sorted using time index. Following the application of the suggested procedure to the collected data from each of these three domains, the time-based data was used to identify the social network’s prominent users. Due to the fact that the existing databases for identifying influential users (which also have user reaction information), lack background information regarding leader nodes (the leader nodes are not specified in advance), therefore, in order to examine the performance of the introduced approach, the SIR information dissemination strategy has been used. Also, 14,300 unique text messages related to the spread of the Covid-19 disease were extracted from the aforementioned data set, of which 8395 samples are authentic and 5905 samples are rumors.

Step 1: identify the rumor The proposed plan starts with the analysis of the content of the messages shared in the social network. In this step, the content of the messages published in the social network is processed using text mining and ML techniques to identify the content including rumors in the network. This process is performed using three stages of pre-processing, feature extraction and classification, which we will discuss further.
(A) Pre-processing of texts in the social network
We handle the preliminary processing of the textual content of exchanged messages in this stage. Each communication is regarded as a text document for this reason, and the input text is divided into the individual phrases. Text segmentation is the process of dividing a document into its constituent parts, such as words, phrases, and paragraphs. The suggested approach divides the message text into its constituent words. The content of a message can contain any type of character. This feature can reduce the accuracy of the fake news detection system. On the other hand, some words or phrases in the text can follow a certain format. For example, email addresses, web page addresses, numbers and similar items follow a specific pattern in texts. In the pre-processing stage, we identify these phrases and replace them with keywords that specify that content in the text. These patterns are:
- Numbers: in the proposed method, every number in the text of the message is replaced with the key word numberValue.

- Web page addresses: Whenever there is a web page address in the text, we replace it with the httpAddr keyword in the pre-processing stage.

- Email address: whenever we come across a template that represents an email in the text of a message, we replace it with the keyword emailAddr.



After describing these patterns by described keywords, we ignore all the remaining symbols other than letters and numbers in the text of the message. By doing this, the text of the message will contain a set of words and patterns specified by keywords.
We discover and delete unproductive terms in each document’s sentences after segmenting and summarizing the patterns of the input text with keywords. Words like be, from, as, and so on are ineffective since their appearance in the text does not represent any significant notion. As a result, deleting these terms from sentences in any text document helps enhance the process of extracting features from texts. After deleting ineffective words from the message’s content, rooting of words is performed; this is done because using rooted words limits the size of the vector of words, or in other words, the vector of features. As a result, suffixes and prefixes are omitted for each word in the title. It should be emphasized that the suggested technique employs Porter’s algorithm for rooting.
(B) Feature extraction from message content
The proposed method employs GloVe as an approach for representing each textual content as a numerical vector. The steps of numerical representation of texts using GloVe can be summarized in the following steps:
B.1. Corpus Preparation and Vocabulary Formation:

In the proposed method, the “word vector averaging” approach has been used to numerically represent a text (for example, a sentence or document). To this end, for each word in the text, its corresponding GloVe vector is retrieved. The word vectors are then averaged to obtain a vector for the entire text. More formally, if a text contains n words and each word is represented using a d-dimensional GloVe vector, then the text is represented using a d-dimensional vector obtained by summing all n word vectors and dividing the result by n. This averaged vector represents the overall semantic meaning of the text.
It should be noted that in this research, instead of training the GloVe model from scratch, pre-trained GloVe vectors have been used. These vectors, which have been trained on massive text corpora, record general semantic relationships. Thus, for each word in the text, its corresponding pre-trained GloVe vector is retrieved, and the averaging step is applied to it to obtain the numerical representation of the text. This approach will save computational resources and time. Consequently, the content of each message will be described by rows of the matrix that results from this phase. In the next step, these characteristics will be given to the classification algorithm to detect rumors.

Step 2: identifying the influential nodes and the path of spreading rumors in the network In the second stage of the proposed technique, after examining the content of the shared message in the first step, the texts containing rumors are recognized, and this group of texts is taken as the current data of the social network. The second phase of the suggested method identifies the source nodes that propagate the rumor and the data flow pathways between them using graph analysis and clustering techniques. The actions required to accomplish this aim will be outlined in the sections that follow.
(A) Determining the value of network communications
We consider a SN that is represented by graph G. Users are regarded as graph nodes in this SN, and user-to-user transactions are referred to as directed and weighted links from the graph. The subsequent user’s reaction rate will likewise be shown by the weight of each edge. The nature of SNs means that not every link made by network users may be regarded as equally significant. As a result, one interaction may depict the user’s response to thousands of messages, whereas another may simply depict the user’s response to a single message. Because of this, we will use a weight metric in the suggested technique to represent the significance of each link in the SN’s graph. We employ a criterion known as the likelihood of user reaction for this aim. The starting weight of a directed link connecting nodes A and B, for instance, is equal to the number of times user B has responded to user A’s contents. In actual SNs, the likelihood that user B will respond to a message from A is represented by the value of this link in the SN graph. (B) Network clustering and identification of influential nodes in spreading rumors
The weighted graph derived from the first stage is separated into its component communities in the second stage of the suggested approach. The purpose of this step is to reduce the difficulty of choosing successful users by decomposing the issue into a collection of more manageable issues. Equation (9) is used to weight the set of links in graph G. The probability that other users will respond to each user’s content determines the weight for every edge. Therefore, a smaller weight for every connection in the SN’s graph indicates a decreased chance that the user’s content will elicit a response from others. In the beginning the weight of graph links is inverted in the suggested solution. After reversing the weight values, this measure can be employed for determining local communities in the social network. In this case, by selecting edges with less weight, users having stronger relationships (people who are more likely to react to their content) will also be selected. For this aim and also increase the processing speed of huge networks, you can use the graph tree structure. The tree structure makes each user in the network checked only once. The result of this will be the reduction of computational complexity in the presented approach. In order to detect the communities based on the created weighted graph, an MST (minimum spanning tree) is built from the weighted graph by Prim’s algorithm. This process causes some of the connections with more weight (less likely to react to the user’s messages) in the social network graph to be removed.
After generating the MST structure, we will delete the links with the maximum weights in the obtained structure. If the desired graph contains N vertices, then by removing the connection with the highest weight in the minimum spanning tree structure, the number of local community can be obtained. The set of local communities obtained has the largest set of common internal neighborhoods; because the internal edges of each of these sub graphs have the lowest weight.
Next, the formed local communities are merged to obtained the final communities. In the continuation of this section, the process of integrating local communities and identifying communities in the proposed method will be described. We denote the set of local communities G as lC(G) . In order to identify the communities that make up the social network, a strategy based on repetition has been used in the proposed method. In this process, local communities are examined two by two. In each comparison, a local fitting function is used to check the merging capability of two local communities. In this case, communities that maximize the benefit of the local function are merged together.
The modularity criterion is used to evaluate the quality of graph clustering, and for this reason, it can be used as a function to evaluate the fit in the community detection problem. In fact, in the detection of communities based on the modularity criterion, it is necessary to analyze the graph at the level of nodes and then measure the quality of the integration of two nodes based on the modularity criterion. The previously described steps related to minimum spanning tree and edge pruning were to improve the performance in the merge step. In this way, a community can be defined as a sub graph that is able to maximize the fitting property in the graph. (C) Determining the route of spreading rumors in the network
To establish the paths of rumor propagation in the social network, we utilize the weight matrix W’ and the collection of prominent nodes in the network. The network’s important nodes are considered two by two for this purpose, and at each step, the path with the greatest value connecting these two nodes is searched. By doing this, the set of rumor propagation paths, which are actually the paths connecting the influential nodes to each other, will be identified. Limitations and ethical considerations Although the proposed multi-modal approach is highly accurate, there are a number of limitations and ethical issues that should be discussed to provide a balanced view of the results.
To start with, one of the possible issues associated with training deep learning models, including the 1D-CNN to detect rumors, is the possibility of overfitting to the training data. To counter this, we used conventional regularization methods. The performance of the model was continuously checked against a separate validation set, independent of the training data, which enabled us to follow the generalization. Moreover, an early stopping criterion was used, which ended the training process when the performance on the validation set did not improve anymore. This is to make sure that the model is the most generalizable to unknown data and not one that has memorized the training data.
Second, the strength of our results must be viewed within the framework of the dataset that was utilized. We are using a massive Twitter dataset related to the Covid-19 pandemic and restricted to one year. This particularity is a possible source of bias. The nature of rumor spreading and user actions during a special global health crisis might vary greatly with the other situations, like political events or financial news. In addition, the models were trained and tested only on the data of the Twitter platform. Our framework has not been generalized to other social media networks (e.g., Facebook, WhatsApp) that have different network structures, user demographics and content-sharing mechanisms, and thus, needs to be studied further.
Lastly, ethical issues, especially the privacy of the users were of utmost importance in this study. All the information that was extracted out of Twitter network was completely anonymized prior to any analysis. All personal identifiable information (PII) (e.g., user IDs and screen names) was obfuscated so that no individual user could be identified. The study was carried out using this anonymized data only, and our analysis was based on aggregate information flow and network patterns instead of the actions of particular individuals. Such a method is in accordance with the ethical principles of conducting research with social media data of the population and ensures the privacy of the people involved.
Conclusion In this paper, a novel and thorough technique to identifying rumors and their diffusion patterns in social networks is described. The suggested method for studying the rumor propagation model in social networks is divided into two parts. (1) Rumor identification: In this step, the content of the messages published in the social network is processed using text mining and DL techniques to identify the content containing the rumor in the network. (2) Identifying the influential nodes and the route of spreading the rumor in the network: In this step, using clustering techniques and graph analysis, the source nodes that spread the rumor are identified and the data flow paths between them are also identified. The results of a case study on a dataset including messages published on the Twitter network related to the Covid-19 disease showed that the proposed model is more accurate than others. The results of the first phase (rumor identification) showed that the proposed model has the highest values of Accuracy , Precision , Recall and F-Measure indices compared to others. After the proposed model, the models FakeBERT and Jian et al. are respectively in the next ranks of the model with the best classification quality. Also, the KNN model has the lowest index values and, as a result, the lowest level of accuracy in data classification. The results related to the second phase, i.e. the identification of influential nodes and the route of rumor propagation in the network, showed that the proposed method has more values of Accuracy , Precision , Recall and F-Measure indices than the other two methods including Jian and Katarya 25 and Zareie et al. 26 . The results showed that the proposed method has the most correlation with the SIR method. The results also showed that by increasing the time period of data from 120 to 360 days and increasing the volume of data, the values of the evaluation indices are also increased. Therefore, it can be concluded that with the increase in the amount of information available in social networks, it is possible to identify the effective nodes and the route of spreading the rumor more accurately. The results also revealed that as the number of effector nodes increased, so did the Accuracy and Recall indices. Because as the number of picked leaders increases, so does the possibility that a leader will be selected correctly and the algorithm’s capacity to identify the leader. F-Measure and Precision index values drop with an increase of effector nodes. Because there is a greater chance that unsuccessful nodes will be included in the list of effective nodes as the number of leader nodes rises.
