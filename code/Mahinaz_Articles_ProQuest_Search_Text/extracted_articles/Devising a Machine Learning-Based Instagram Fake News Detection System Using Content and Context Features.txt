Introduction
Nowadays, social network platform is the most prevalent news-sharing medium. People are spending a significant portion of their time on these social, digital, virtual, and some sense free networks like Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, Weibo, and so on. One can easily create and share fake news on these networks with just a few clicks and harass society. Fake news refers to any piece of information that is intentionally or unintentionally false or misleading. Different studies have shown that the negative effects of fake news will not be rectified even after announcing its falseness. Research works like (Shu et al. 2020), (Shu et al. 2017), (Kumar et al. 2016) show that the high volume and velocity of fake news generation and dissemination along with some cognitive shortcomings, make it impossible for normal people to detect fake news timely and effective. The influential power of fake news was best highlighted during the 2016 U.S. presidential election where the top twenty the most discussed false stories got more than 8 million reactions (likes, shares, comments,…) on Facebook (Silverman 2016). On the other hand, manual fact-checking by journalists or experts is time-consuming and infeasible; therefore, AI seems the only promising solution to this problem, at the moment.
Studies in the field of automatic fake news detection can be grouped into three main categories according to (Zhou and Zafarani 2020): Style-based, Propagation-based, and User-based methods. The idea behind Style-based techniques is that writing style is a discriminative factor for fake news detection; for example, it is shown that emotional words are more common in fake news than in real news. Propagation-based methods use dissemination patterns of news for assessing its credibility. They believe that fake news spreads like infectious diseases and we can hunt them using some epidemic models. In user-based techniques, the focus is on authors. They try to investigate the originality of news using the credibility of its creator and spreaders.
Instagram with one billion monthly active users (Peng and Gatschke 2022) is one of the most common social networks in the world and has a great effect on our society. To the best of our knowledge, there is no specially-designed automatic fake news detection system for Instagram. So, in this work we have devised a system for automatic fake news detection on Instagram using a combination of Style-based and User-based techniques. Our contributions can be cleared as:1- Combining content-based features with context-based features in one system to achieve better accuracy. 2- Creating a labeled Instagram Fake News Dataset for training our Machine Learning (ML) models that can be also used for other studies. 3- Conducting a large number of experiments and a holistic analysis of our system.
The rest of the paper is as follows: In the next section, fake news detection literature will be reviewed. In Sect. 3, the proposed system will be described. In Sect. 4, experimental results will be explained and the paper will be concluded in Sect. 5.
Related Work
The fake news detection literature is very rich and we will review only the most relevant studies to our work. Amer and Siddiqui (2020) worked on COVID-19 fake news detection on Instagram. Their method was based on caption analysis of news using textual feature extraction. They reached 92 and 94 % accuracy using the decision tree model and the random forest model, respectively. A study by Thejas et al. (2019) has focused on the differences between likes produced by robots and by humans on Instagram. They collected likes of real users and bots for a month and generated a 38-feature dataset for training an ML model for detecting the types of likes. Finally, they created an automated ML model to show the correlation between followers and followings with the validity and invalidity of Instagram likes. Their experimental results showed that the Random Forest and the SVM models can reach over 90 % of precision, recall, and accuracy in detecting fake and real likes.
Sheng et al. (2022) suggest that we should zoom out and consider the news environment for detecting fake news. Based on this idea, they have devised the News Environment Perception Framework (NEP) in which for each post the micro and macro environments are built based on mainstream media. They have shown that NEP outperforms basic fake news detection techniques. Raza and Ding (2022) proposed a transformer architecture based on BART (Lewis et al. 2019) and exploited both new content and social context for early detection of fake news.
Zarei et al. (2020) worked on the forgery of identity on Instagram. They worked on three datasets: political news, general news, and sports news consisting of real and fake Instagram accounts including celebrities like Messi, Ronaldo, Trump, and Obama, and news agencies like CNN and BBC. They applied NLP techniques and different ML algorithms like k-means, Gaussian mixture model, and spectral clustering in their research. It is shown that their method can distinguish between real and fake accounts using only 10 attributes and can find fake news generated and disseminated by fake accounts.
Apuke et al. (2021) studied different factors for the dissemination of fake news about the COVID-19 pandemic in Nigeria. Finally, they concluded that “altruism” is the most significant factor for fake news sharing on social media while “entertainment” motivation has no effect. Authors in (Ghayoomi and Mousavian 2022) have leveraged transfer learning and multilingual pre-trained language models for detecting COVID-19 fake news in the Persian language as a low-resource language.
Sahoo et al. (2021) worked on the automatic detection of fake news on Facebook. They used a robot to collect data, and deep learning and SVM models to detect fake news. Features used in their research contained the number of followers, number of likes, and number of friends and followings. Their experimental results showed 99.4 % accuracy using the SVM model.
Reis et al. (2019) considered some new features for fake news detection. Features such as bias, credibility and popularity, news producer IP location, and interaction with likes and comments have been investigated in their work. For the learning module, they tried different ML algorithms like KNN, SVM, Random Forest and XGBoost. They also tagged and archived resources, and showed that this can be effective in detecting fake news.
Bazm et al. (2020) presented a method for detecting Instagram bots. They believe that bots are the main source of fake news dissemination in social networks and finding them can help remove misleading data from the virtual world. They gathered a dataset containing 1000 records of Instagram bots and 1000 records of real human accounts on Instagram. They applied different ML techniques for learning and concluded by experimental results that boosting methods like AdaBoost are the most accurate methods.
Mena et al. (2020) worked on the credibility of Instagram posts and showed that the likes of celebrities can have a great effect on the approval of fake news by other people.
Granik et al. (2017) proposed a method for detecting fake news using classical ML algorithms. They believe that fake news usually is created for political and financial aid, so the title is often deceptive. For feature engineering they have used content features such as words and sentences; for the learning module Naïve Bayes has been used and data has been collected from Facebook posts, and political news agencies such as Politico, CNN, and ABC News.
In 2020, Oliveira et al. (2020) proposed a style-based fake news detection system. They selected Twitter, and collected 33,000 tweets for learning. Different ML techniques have been used in their work and 86 % of accuracy and 94 % of precision has been reported.
Vosoughi et al. (2018) have worked on fake news dissemination patterns on Twitter. They have reported interesting mined knowledge: 1- Falsehood spreads faster and deeper than truth in social networks which means false news is more interesting than real news. 2- Political false news gains more attention and more diffusion than false news about terrorism or science. 3- False stories have more novelty than real stories and this is the reason why people are more likely to share false news than real ones. 4- False stories inspire fear, disgust, and surprise in comments while true stories inspire sadness, joy, and trust.
Ajao et al. (2018) use d a combination of CNN and LSTM neural networks for fake news detection. Their dataset consisted of 5800 tweets, and their study showed that LSTM was more accurate and had a lower error detection rate than other models.
Khanam et al. (2021) investigated different ML algorithms for fake news detection. In their research, they used labeled political data and labeling was based on content and context features. Their dataset was 2012 Indian election data and they applied Naïve Bayes, Neural Networks, and SVM for the learning phase. They reported that the Naïve Bayes model achieved 96 % of accuracy and Neural Network and SVM achieved an accuracy of 99.9 %.
Despite a vast amount of research works that have been done in this area, there is a long way to go. In this paper, we tried to create new context and content features from Instagram news posts and devised an ML-based system for fake news detection. Our proposed system will be scrutinized in the following.
The Proposed System
We can divide our contributions in this paper into three parts: (1) Devising an automatic fake news detection system based on a combination of content and context features and a variety of ML techniques. (2) Creating a real Instagram fake news dataset that allows other researchers to investigate the effectiveness of their methods on Instagram. (3) Implementing and investigating the performance of our method on our real dataset using a large body of experiments.
The proposed system has been depicted in Fig. 1. It has three modules: (1) the data collection module (2) the feature engineering module (3) the learning module. The data collection module has the responsibility of gathering data from Instagram. Although data labeling has been done manually. The feature engineering module generates and selects effective features of news content and news publishers. The classifying module uses ML algorithms for learning and predicting. These three modules will be described in the following subsection. [See PDF for image]

Data Collection Module
Because of the lack of a Persian Instagram dataset labeled for fake news detection, we created it in this work. The data collection module contains activities that have been done for gathering and labeling data. In the first step, 40 public Instagram Persian news pages were selected for data collection (8 of them publish true news and others publish fake news). In the second step, a computer robot programmed by PHP has been used for collecting the required data from selected pages. Extracted data includes: - Page information: username, title, biography text, length of biography text, number of followers, number of followings, number of posts, whether the page has profile picture or not, and whether the page has saved stories or not.

- Post information: type, caption, length of the caption, hashtags, number of hashtags, number of likes, number of comments, and number of views.

- Information about comments of each post: sender username, comment text, comment text length, comment posting time, and number of comment likes.

- Information about followers of each page: username, title, biography text, private or public, whether have a profile picture or not, number of her/his posts, number of her/his followers, and number of her/his followings.

In the third step, the labeling has been done by human experts. For labeling, popular and reputable news sources have been checked. If a news post was reported by any reputable news source, it was labeled as true and otherwise labeled as fake news. In this version of our dataset, we labeled 5028 posts where 3772 of them are true and 1256 of them are fake.
Feature Engineering Module
The success of ML algorithms is highly dependent on features used for representing data objects. In this work, we tried to find the most discriminative features for fake news detection on Instagram data. Our new feature set is a combination of successful features reported in the literature and new features which are proposed by considering the unique characteristics of Instagram. Both content and context features have been considered in our feature vector. Content features are attributes of a post, like the number of comments written on it or the number of hashtags used in its text part. It is worth noting that in this work the text part of the post has not been used for fake news detection unlike what is done in the NLP-based methods. Among our proposed features three of them need more explanations: engagement_rate, number_of_fake_followers, and relevant_hashtag_rate. Engagement_rate computes the volume of user activities (likes and comments) on a page relative to the number of page followers. We observed that pages that spread fake news have more fake followers than others, and fake followers usually show less engagement -using likes and comments- in various posts on a page. engagement_rate can be computed using Formula 1. 1
engagement _ ratio = C + L F where C is the total number of page comments, L is the total number of likes attained by the page and F is the number of page followers.number_of_fake_followers is an indicator of page authenticity. In this work, three simple heuristics have been used for labeling followers as fake:1- No profile photo, 2- Zero number of posts, and 3- Big differences (at least 100 persons in this work) between the number of followings and followers.relevant_hashtag_rate has been proposed based on this hypothesis: Fake news has more irrelevant hashtags than real news. It is computed by Formula 2. 2
relevant _ hashtag _ rate = H caption ∩ H body n × 100 where H caption for a post is the set of all hashtags used in its caption; H Body is the set of all hashtags used in its body part and n is the total number of hashtags in the post.
After representing the dataset using the proposed feature vector, it is fed into the learning module. In the next subsection, the ML detection module will be explained.
ML Detection Module
In the first and second modules of the proposed system, data will be gathered and prepared for the learning module. In this module, some of the most prominent ML shallow algorithms have been used and their effectiveness has been investigated on our Instagram dataset. Deep techniques have not been utilized in our ML detection module, because detecting fake news based on this type of feature vector is a kind of shallow task. Logistic Regression, KNN, SVM, Naive Bayes, and Random Forest which are some of the most successful techniques in the ML era have been applied for learning. We have done a large body of experiments to analyze their discriminative power on our Instagram dataset.
Experimental Results
Implementation has been done using Python, within the Google Colab environment. Data has been normalized using the Min–max normalization technique. For evaluation, the cross-validation technique, with k  = 10 has been used and Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1-Score have been reported as performance measures. The significance level for confidence interval computations has been set at 0.05 for all models (it means 95 % of confidence about the numbers). The detailed experimental results for each ML algorithm have been presented in the following subsections.
One of the major contributions of our work is proposing new features fitted to Instagram characteristics. Here, feature importance has been used to evaluate the discriminative power of proposed features against the state-of-the-art features. For feature importance computation, the permutation importance intuitive proposed by (Parr et al. 2018) has been used. It is noteworthy that, this heuristic has been implemented in the “mlxtend” python library.
