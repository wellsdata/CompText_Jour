INTRODUCTION In the digital age, the rapid dissemination of information has led to the rapid proliferation of fake news, which has had a significant impact on public opinion, social stability, and public trust. Due to the increasingly sophisticated and covert production and dissemination of fake news, its identification has become increasingly difficult [1, 2]. Fake news not only causes chaos in the political field, but also has negative impacts on society, economy, and public health. Therefore, developing effective False News Detection (FND) technology is particularly important. At present, FND mainly relies on manual review and traditional machine learning methods. Although these methods are effective in certain scenarios, they often appear inadequate when faced with large-scale and diverse data. Especially on social media platforms, the multimodal nature of news makes the detection of fake news more complex [3]. In addition, existing methods often struggle to achieve ideal accuracy and efficiency when dealing with social media content with complex semantics and multiformatted information [4]. Faced with multimodal data and constantly changing fake news dissemination, existing detection technologies have obvious shortcomings in accuracy, real-time performance, and adaptability. How to effectively integrate and analyze different types of data and how to improve the universality and robustness of detection techniques are the main challenges currently faced in this field. Given these challenges, the main objective of the research is to develop an efficient FND model. It can accurately identify and classify fake news content under multimodal conditions, while ensuring high processing efficiency and adaptability. To achieve this goal, a Knowledge-Driven Adaptive Multimodal Graph Convolutional Network (KAMGCN) using Adaptive Graph Convolutional Network (AGCN) is proposed. This model utilizes AGCN, combined with text, visual information, and external knowledge bases, to conduct deep learning and analysis of fake news. This research’s innovation lies in the combination of AGCN and multimodal data analysis, which can more effectively process and analyze social media content with complex structures. In addition, KAMGCN improves its ability to identify deep features of fake news by integrating external knowledge bases and utilizing advanced word vector technology. The research is mainly divided into four parts. Firstly, the research content of domestic and foreign scholars on Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) and FND is summarized. Secondly, an FND model based on AGCN is constructed, and the basic model and its detection methods for fake news are introduced. Then, performance experiments are conducted on the constructed model to verify the advanced and practical nature of the proposed model through comparative model validation. Finally, the research results in the experiment are summarized, and the current shortcomings and future research directions are pointed out.
RELATED WORKS With the continuous development of the internet society, various types of news are constantly emerging, and the authenticity of news has become an important hidden danger in the current internet society. Scholars at home and abroad have conducted extensive research on methods for identifying and detecting fake news. Al B. M. E. believed that false text was anonymous and sometimes associated with potential URLs. Due to data limitations and communication categories, the current system was not worth accurately classifying a news article. Machine learning methods were used in this experiment to predict and detect malicious data on online social networks. The enhanced random forest classification and NaiveBayes collected tweets were used to search for spam tweets. To evaluate the work, different validation metrics were calculated, such as F1-score, accuracy, and precision values [5]. Mbona et al. proposed using Benford’s law to predict the effective leading digits’ frequency distribution. Features strictly adhered to Benford’s law on human datasets, while on malicious robot datasets the same features violated it. This study helped to intelligently detect malicious robots, thereby minimizing the spam spread [6]. Almenar E. et al. analyzed representative surveys of the Spanish population. The participants were asked about their relationship with fake news and their ability to determine the authenticity of information, and their ability to identify false content was evaluated. These surveys confirmed that men and women had difficulties to identify fake news. Female cared about how misinformation affected society than men [7]. Sivasankari et al. used similarity measures to verify the credibility of users on social media. If users were incredible, the content they posted was considered incredible. However, this method had the disadvantage of being time-consuming and facing many difficulties in verifying false content on social media. Therefore, they attempted to isolate incredible users who indirectly helped people identify the fake content they posted [8].
Zhang et al. proposed a knowledge graph modeling method using GCN to capture learner preferences. In addition, the learning resources’ weights were calculated by utilizing the learning resources and learners’ features. Finally, by balancing learner preferences and the importance of learning resources, the optimal learning path was recommended for learners [9]. Yangzhi Li et al. put forward an action recognition algorithm using a spatiotemporal attention GCN model. Both spatial and temporal attention mechanisms were integrated into a unified GCN framework to achieve end-to-end training. Experiments on the Kinetics and NTU RGB+D datasets confirmed that the proposed STA-GCN-based algorithm had strong robustness and stability [10]. Rezaee proposed a mixed means to make heart sounds classified through two-step learning. Sampling layers and prior layers were used. The PCG signal was separated into overlapping equal sections. Using the Michigan heart and noise database, the accuracy of the model reached 99.44% [11]. Chen et al. proposed a new means using GCN and semantic feature guidance. A deep network was utilized as the feature generator, and GCN was used for clustering allocation. Experiments on 8 public datasets confirmed that this algorithm was significantly superior to most competitive clustering methods, with a clustering accuracy 20% higher than the best model [12].
In summary, many existing FND methods are difficult to effectively handle multimodal data, which limits their effectiveness in practical applications. The production and dissemination strategies of fake news are constantly changing, and existing methods often lack sufficient adaptability and flexibility to cope with these changes. GCN can effectively integrate and analyze data from different modalities, utilizing its deep feature extraction capabilities to improve understanding of complex and unstructured data. Therefore, the study proposes a multimodal FND method that integrates AGCN.
A FAKE NEWS DETECTION MODEL USING ADAPTIVE GRAPH CONVOLUTIONAL NETWORK The FND task can be regarded as a binary classification problem, with a goal of identifying whether each news post is fake news. There are two types of task modes in FND. Firstly, it is necessary to distinguish the authenticity of a social news post in the dimension of news posts. Secondly, FND is conducted on the dimension of news events, which identifies a group of events composed of consecutive posts. Therefore, the study proposes a multimodal FND method based on GCN.
Multimodal Fake News Detection based on Graph Convolutional Networks The FND task has a goal of identifying whether each news post is fake news. There are two types of task modes in FND. Firstly, it is necessary to distinguish the authenticity of a social news post in the dimension of news posts. Secondly, FND is conducted on the dimension of news events, which identifies a group of events composed of consecutive posts [13]. The proposed model’s goal is to perform FND on the post dimension, consisting of several textual and visual information. Research requires a model to classify each post into predefined categories. To determine whether news posts are fake news, a knowledge-driven multimodal GCN is proposed to model semantic level feature representations of posts in a unified structure in Fig. 1.

In Fig. 1, the input of KMGCN contains textual, visual, and related knowledge information from news posts. Firstly, KMGCN obtains the concept of knowledge from the textual content of the post. Then, it transforms the multimodal content and knowledge concepts of the post into a topology diagram. Finally, each topology map requires two layers of GCN to aggregate the information of each node in the graph and one layer of pooling operation to get each post’s features. They are input into the fully connecting and softmax layers to distinguish the fake news [14]. The input news post’s multimodal content is modeled as a topology map using the Point Mutual Information (PMI) values between word pairs. Word2Vec has two ways to generate word vectors, including the Continuous Bag of Words (CBOW) structure and skip-gram [16]. CBOW predicts the central word based on the context within the window. At this point, the order of vocabulary in the context does not affect the prediction results. In the skip-gram structure, the model predicts its contextual vocabulary based on the current vocabulary, and the weight of contextual vocabulary closer to it is greater than that of contextual vocabulary farther away. ANALYSIS OF MULTIMODAL FAKE NEWS DETECTION BASED ON ADAPTIVE GRAPH CONVOLUTIONAL NETWORKS To verify the proposed model’s performance, comparative experiments were conducted to verify the predictive ability of this model. Comparative models include common benchmark methods, mutation models, and current methods. In this study, accuracy, precision, recall rate, and F1-score were utilized as evaluating indicators. The effectiveness of the model was validated using the PHEME and WEIBO datasets, both of which were publicly available social media datasets. PHEME contains a large number of genuine and fake news posts about a certain event, including text content, images, and contextual information related to them. The WEIBO dataset is collected from large social networks, and each post contains links to text, images, and responses to this post.
Model Training and Performance Analysis In knowledge distillation, the entity connection method was used to obtain the entity set in the each news post’s text content. PMI was used to convert the post’s text content into a graph structured dataset. The window size for calculating word co-occurrence frequency in PMI was set to 20. The pretrained bidirectional encoder representations from transformers (BERT) model generated an initial word vector for each word, with a dimension size of 768. The output dimensions of the two-layer AGCN were 64 and 32, respectively. The model’s learning rate was 0.001, and the training iteration was 300 times. The experiment was implemented using the PyTorch deep learning framework. The study divided the dataset into training and testing sets, with the training set accounting for 70% and the training set accounting for 30%. Comparative models include convolutional neural network (CNN), gated recurrent unit (GRU), KMGCN, and SpotFake [19, 20]. Figure 5 shows the accuracy and precision results of each model in WEIBO data.

To verify the computational efficiency and performance of the model under articles of different complexities, a comparative experiment was conducted for verification and analysis. The verification algorithms include KAMGCN, KMGCN, GRU, CNN, and SpotFake. The specific evaluation metrics include training time, inference time, memory usage, accuracy rate, and F1-score. The experimental data come from the real article data of the PHEME and WEIBO datasets and are classified into three levels according to the text complexity: low, medium and high. Low complexity means that the text length is ≤100 characters, there is no multimodal information (no images or links), and the number of entity links in the knowledge graph is ≤2. Medium complexity indicates a text length of 101 to 300 words, containing 1 image or 1 link, and the number of entity links is 3 to 5. High complexity indicates a text length of more than 300 words, containing at least 2 images or links, and having at least 6 entity links. The analysis of Table 1 reveals significant variations in the performance and computational efficiency of different models—CNN, GRU, KMGCN, and KAMGCN—across low, medium, and high complexity news articles. For low complexity texts, KAMGCN exhibited the highest accuracy (0.902) and F1‑score (0.891), followed by KMGCN, GRU, and CNN, confirming its superior capability in handling simpler data. As the complexity of the text increased, the training and reasoning times also escalated, with KAMGCN taking the longest time for both training (62.4 s) and reasoning (19.8 ms/sample). Despite the increased computational overhead, KAMGCN maintained strong predictive performance, achieving an F1-score of 0.865 for high complexity articles. In contrast, the performance of CNN declined sharply in terms of accuracy and F1-score as text complexity increased, reaching only 0.621 and 0.603 respectively for high complexity articles, indicating its limitations in processing complex semantic features. This analysis underscores the trade-off faced when adopting advanced models like KAMGCN, which, while offering enhanced accuracy and robustness, requires greater computational resources, making it a preferable choice for scenarios where performance is prioritized over speed. In summary, the results indicate that KAMGCN is well-suited for diverse complexity levels of news articles, effectively balancing accuracy and efficiency, positioning it as a robust solution for fake news detection.
CONCLUSIONS It is crucial to develop effective FND tools in the face of the negative impact of fake news on public opinion and social order. The study utilized AGCN combined with entity recognition and word vector technology to construct a multimodal information integration FND model KAMGCN. By utilizing PMI to obtain graph structures and using BERT to extract semantically rich word embeddings, a deep analysis of fake news content was completed. Tests on two standard datasets showed that KAMGCN significantly outperformed existing detection methods. Specifically, on the WEIBO dataset, the model improved accuracy by approximately 28.4% and precision by approximately 28.5% compared to the baseline CNN. On the PHEME dataset, KAMGCN also performed significantly, with the highest accuracy among all models, an improvement of approximately 1.9% compared to SpotFake. In the model robustness test, KAMGCN showed an average error reduction of about 56.07% compared to the benchmark test, demonstrating its excellent noise resistance ability. It has been confirmed that KAMGCN outperforms current popular models on two mainstream fake news datasets, confirming the potential of AGCN in detecting fake news. Although this model has shown good performance, the slower detection speed may affect the demand for real-time queries. Its complexity may also bring a burden on computing resources. Future work will optimize the real-time performance of models and reduce computational resource consumption. It is possible to explore incorporating more knowledge graphs and social dynamics into the model to further enhance its detection ability. In addition, the ability of the extended model to handle FND in different language and cultural backgrounds will also become the next research focus.
