Introduction
Fake News Video Detection (FNVD) is a growing and complex field that demands machines to accurately discern and evaluate deceptive content within videos, drawing significant interest from researchers [1]. This task’s intricacy lies in the need to comprehend diverse and nuanced video elements, including object relationships and frame transitions, making relational inference a critical component of FNVD. Recent research [2, 3–4] has explored the use of advanced methods like memory networks [5], attention mechanisms [6], and Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) [7] to enhance relational reasoning in FNVD. While these approaches have shown some success, they continue to encounter two prevalent challenges.

Fig. 1 Multi-level relation reasoning


Firstly, current FNVD approaches primarily focus on relational reasoning at either the object [8] or frame [9, 10] level, lacking an effective integration of the two in a multi-level framework. As illustrated in Fig. 1, humans typically identify key objects and their interactions within individual frames (e.g., a zookeeper playing with a panda ) and then connect these frames to comprehend the sequence of actions and their temporal dynamics (e.g., the panda falling and the zookeeper catching it ). This reflects a multi-level reasoning process that transitions from object-level to frame-level analysis. For machines to replicate such reasoning, a method that explicitly enables multi-level relational reasoning is essential. However, existing techniques have yet to achieve this, potentially overlooking critical relationships necessary for accurately identifying fake news. Consequently, developing an FNVD approach that combines object-level and frame-level information for effective multi-level reasoning is vital to enhancing detection accuracy and reliability.
Secondly, current FNVD methods mainly focus on using visual information for judgment, while ignoring the deep reasoning analysis in semantic space. In related studies [11, 12], relation analysis is performed by using Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) to extract features from video frames. Similarly, some researchers such as Zhang et al. [13] and Jin et al. [14] used region Convolutional neural Network (RCNN) to capture object-level visual information. However, these approaches generally ignore the importance of integrating semantic knowledge for comprehensive reasoning, possibly because of the limitations in understanding caused by the inherent gap existing between visual content and semantics. Compared with visual information alone, semantic knowledge, which includes the attributes, categories and logical relationships between objects in a video, provides more explicit and rich clues, which is crucial to accurately judge the authenticity of news videos.
In this study, to address the shortcomings of current FNVD approaches, we propose a multi-level visual-semantic relation reasoning framework (VS-FND) that integrates both visual and semantic reasoning within a hierarchical structure. Specifically, we introduce two graph memory mechanisms. The first is the visual graph memory, which leverages the visual content of the video to progressively extract task-relevant visual features through relational reasoning. The second is the semantic graph memory, where object categories and attributes in the video are represented as nodes, and edges between nodes are established based on commonsense knowledge, explicitly incorporating semantic information to strengthen relational reasoning. Building on this, we further design a multi-level structure that transitions from object-level to frame-level analysis, enabling the framework to reason about the interplay between visual and semantic elements across multiple levels. Our VS-FND framework effectively uncovers and utilizes both visual and semantic information in videos, offering a more precise and comprehensive foundation for detecting fake news videos.
In summary, the contributions of this work are threefold:

- We propose an efficient graph memory mechanism for relational reasoning, and specifically design visual graph memory and semantic graph memory modules for processing visual information and semantic knowledge in videos.

- We construct a multi-level vision-semantic relation reasoning framework (VS-FND). The framework can integrate object-level and frame-level relational reasoning to realize multi-level analysis of fake news videos.

- The experimental results show that the VS-FND framework performs well on the fake news video detection dataset. It not only exceeds the existing methods in accuracy, but also has significant advantages in the number of model parameters and inference speed.



Related work
Fake news video detection
Fake news video detection is more complex than image forgery detection, because it involves not only spatial tampering trace identification [15], but also the analysis of temporal dimension [16]. This requires the detection algorithm to identify multiple inconsistencies in space, time, vision and semantics [17]. Most of the existing researches convert video frames into global feature vectors and focus on frame-level relationship recognition [18]. For example, Jiang et al. [19] proposed a heterogeneous graph alignment network. However, these methods have limitations in capturing spatial details, and it is difficult to accurately detect the tampered videos of objects and their relationships. To improve this situation, Wu et al. [20] tried to use location-aware graph convolutional networks for detailed object-level analysis, but did not fully incorporate frame-level relationships. Different from them, our proposed multi-level relational reasoning framework integrates frame-level and object-level analysis, simulates human cognitive processes, and realizes multi-level comprehensive reasoning.
GNN and memory network
Graph Neural Network (GNN) has shown great potential in relation reasoning due to its excellent message passing between nodes and iterative update node representation ability, which is widely used in image fields such as image recognition [21], pose estimation [22], and video fields such as action recognition and video object segmentation [23]. Despite studies [24, 25] that attempt to represent nodes as a fusion of visual and query features, GNNs still struggle to effectively cope with such dynamic requirements. Memory networks [26] provide an effective solution for multimodal tasks [27] by explicitly retrieving and storing information through read and write operations, which are able to learn query-relevant information step by step and dynamically. Given its successful application in multi-modal QA, we are inspired to combine GNN with memory network, aiming at dynamic relation inference in fake news video detection task. We call it graph memory, which not only inherits the advantages of GNN in relation reasoning, but also incorporates the dynamic information retrieval capability of memory networks.

Experiments
Datasets
We use two publicly available COVID-VTS [31] and FVC [18] datasets. The COVID-VTS dataset contains news videos related to COVID-19, all of which are derived from video posts on Twitter. The dataset is divided into training set, validation set and test set, containing 8500, 500 and 1000 news items respectively, for a total of 10,000 items. The average length of news titles is about 13.25 words, and the average duration of videos is about 75.08 s. FVC dataset covers the official English news videos from the foreign Xinhua News Agency, which has a wide range of content, including news reports on multiple topics such as politics, sports, and health. FVC dataset contains 2505 news videos, divided into training set (2130), validation set (125) and test set (250). The average title length of news is about 11 words, and the average video duration is nearly 70 s.
Implementation details
In our research, we take a systematic approach to processing and parsing video data, aiming to extract key information from each video precisely. First, we uniformly pick out 16 frames from each video as key frames. We then use Faster R-CNN to identify and select objects in each frame. In each frame, we pick out the eight highest scoring objects, which represent the most salient objects in that frame. We set the embedding space to 512 dimensions. In this space, we represent each target as a 512-dimensional vector. During training, we used the Adam optimizer to optimize the parameters of the model. We set the initial learning rate to 1e-5 to ensure that the model can gradually converge and achieve the best performance during training. Meanwhile, we set the batch size to 32 to make full use of computing resources during training. To evaluate the performance of the model, we used precision (Acc.), precision (Prec.), recall (Rec.), and F1-measure (F1) as evaluation metrics on the two datasets.
Baseline system
In order to better illustrate the advantages of the proposed methods, we selected current representative baselines for comparison, including video-based methods and fake news video detection methods.
Methods based on fake news video detection: (1) TikTec [32]: can use captions to accurately capture key information from distracting video content; (2) FANVM [18]: A topic agnostic fake news detection model based on adversarial learning and topic modeling is proposed; (3) TwtrDet [31]: An effective model combined with cross-media consistency checking is proposed to detect malicious tampering of different modes; (4) HGRN [20]: A local view multi-hop knowledge inference network is proposed to capture richer evidence information in context.
Video-based approach: (1) VEIN [34]: A visual-based emotion interpretation network is designed to effectively capture the emotion conveyed in the video; (2) AMFM [33]: can learn potential visual relationships between frame-level and video-level embedment to dynamically enhance pre-trained visual features; (3) VCMR [35]: A query-specific multimodal context is built to capture complementary and consistent semantics between captions and video frames.

The VS-FND shows significant performance advantages in the detection of fake news videos compared to TikTec, FANVM, TwtrDet and HGRN, which rely on a single error message. Reasoning steps
In exploring the depth of reasoning about visual and semantic relations in the FNVD task, we tested different reasoning steps against the model, and the results are shown in Fig. 5. We have the following observations: (1) Best synergy point: When both the visual reasoning () and semantic reasoning () steps are set to 2, the model performs best in the four FNVD tasks. This indicates that visual and semantic information are equally important in identifying fake news. (2) The complementary role of visual reasoning: when the semantic reasoning step is fixed at 1, the model performance is gradually improved as the visual reasoning step increases from 1 to 3. This shows that deepening visual reasoning can effectively supplement semantic understanding when semantic information is limited, because deep visual features can capture more high-level semantic clues. (3) Semantic reasoning overprocessing: Increasing the number of semantic reasoning steps (from 2 to 3) leads to performance degradation compared to increasing the number of visual reasoning steps. This may be because too much semantic reasoning will smooth or blur the originally clear and specific semantic information.

Qualitative studies
To get a deeper understanding of our VS-FND, we present a visualization of the relational reasoning process in Fig. 6. Initially, the model failed to focus on relevant objects and frames (e.g., objects “road”, “traffic light”) and could not model accurate object-object and frame-frame relationships (e.g., the relationship between “vehicle” and “police line” and between frames 1 and 2). As the iterative steps continue, the model gradually learns to focus on the most relevant objects and frames (e.g., object “cordon” and frame 4), and on the exact object-object relationship and frame-frame relationship (e.g., the relationship between “police car” and “cordon” between frame 3 and 4). In particular, in the absence of explicit semantic knowledge, the model misidentifies objects and actions, even though more visual relational reasoning steps have been performed. After the semantic knowledge is used for relational reasoning, the ability to judge the inconsistency between video and news title is enhanced, and the model finally gives the correct answer.
Conclusion
In this paper, we introduce a fake news video detection method named VS-FND, which achieves effective identification of fake news videos by fusing object-level and frame-level relational reasoning at multiple levels. In order to integrate and utilize this information more effectively, VS-FND also introduces a novel graph memory module. The module adopts advanced graph data structure, which can dynamically filter and integrate video information according to query requirements, so as to generate more accurate video representation. Experimental verification shows that VS-FND performs well in fake news video detection. It has high accuracy and strong adaptability, and can easily deal with complex video content and scenes.
Future work will focus on enhancing model generalization, fusing multi-modal information, and enhancing detection capabilities. It pays attention to real-time application and social responsibility, ensures fairness and transparency of technology, and is committed to building a more real and credible information dissemination environment.
