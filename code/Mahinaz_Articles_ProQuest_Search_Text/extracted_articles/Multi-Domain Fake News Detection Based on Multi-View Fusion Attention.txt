1. Introduction With the rapid development of the Internet, online social platforms have grown vigorously. The public increasingly relies on these platforms for news consumption, which concurrently accelerates the proliferation of fake news across diverse domains. This rampant dissemination of fake news not only severely compromises individuals’ physical and psychological well-being but also poses a significant threat to societal safety and stability. Posts on online social networking platforms are predominantly presented in the form of short text, characterized by an informal linguistic style and the pervasive use of internet slang and emojis. Taking the Weibo platform as an example, fake news often propagates through such short-text posts, which typically exhibit sensational, emotional, and exaggerated features aimed at rapidly capturing users’ attention and facilitating the spread of misinformation. In addition to issues such as unverified sources and misleading headlines, this type of content further expands its reach through non-textual carriers, including images and videos. Currently, multi-domain fake news detection confronts two major challenges: one is the domain shift problem, and the other is the cross-domain correlation problem.
News from different domains often exhibits significant discrepancies in vocabulary, sentiment, and writing style, a phenomenon known as domain shift. Meanwhile, a single news topic usually sparks discussions across multiple domains, referred to as cross-domain topic correlation. Traditional domain-agnostic methods that directly apply generic models fail to adequately capture inter-domain differences, resulting in inconsistent predictive performance across domains and limiting the overall improvement of prediction accuracy and efficacy.
To address the aforementioned challenges of domain shift and cross-domain correlation, this paper proposes a fake news detection method based on Multi-View Fusion Attention. This method extracts news features from three perspectives. Firstly, features are extracted from three views: textual semantic expression, emotional tendency, and writing style. An external view attention mechanism is employed to compute attention weights among features from different views, thereby more deeply capturing the differences in the application and expression of various features and enabling the filtering of original features. The method constructs four news feature spaces, where domain features within the news feature spaces serve as indirect features to perform domain awareness on the target news to be detected. Cross-domain connections in the target news are revealed through the similarity between view features and domain features. Furthermore, this method optimizes the fusion process of multi-view features across three dimensions: news content, domain affiliation, and cross-domain relationships. This enables the model to derive feature extraction strategies based on news content and the relationships among different domains, thereby effectively addressing the issues of domain shift and cross-domain correlation.
To enhance the model’s ability to identify the domain to which news belongs, this paper proposes a fake news detection framework based on multi-view fusion attention. This framework treats fake news detection as the primary task and domain classification as the auxiliary supervised task. By simultaneously training these two tasks, the model parameters are optimized. This forces the model to learn the associations between fused multi-view features and domain features.
In summary, this paper proposes a multi-domain fake news detection model based on Multi-View Fusion Attention to address domain shift and cross-domain association issues. It effectively captures differences among news across domains, explores correlations between news items and between news and domains, and optimizes feature fusion strategies for the model across diverse domains from multiple dimensions. Specifically, an external view memory matrix is proposed to achieve multi-view fused feature screening and employ a domain-based matrix to establish news domain memory within the sample space. This enables the association between sample news domains and multi-view features, significantly enhancing fake news detection performance across multiple domains. The proposed model is trained within a joint training framework on both Chinese and English datasets, achieving notable improvements in predictive performance. Compared to state-of-the-art models, the F1 scores on Chinese and English datasets reach 93.26% and 85.31%, respectively, with gains of 0.53% and 2.00%, fully validating the effectiveness of the proposed method and framework.
2. Related Work Social media cyberspace, with its vast user base, high penetration, and rapid dissemination capabilities, has become a crucial platform for the public to access information and express opinions. Influenced by the mechanisms of information dissemination—empowerment, amplification, targeting, and transformation—the confrontation between infiltration and counter-infiltration, attack and counter-attack, as well as control and counter-control within social media cyberspace has intensified. According to statistical data, Weibo effectively handled and removed over 310,000 pieces of rumor information and more than 3600 rumor accounts in 2025. In 2024, Weibo effectively addressed over 130,000 cases of false information, while the Douyin platform cumulatively dealt with 4.52 million instances of misinformation. With the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence technologies such as automated text generation, AI-generated false information has experienced explosive growth, spanning multiple domains, including politics and society, thereby posing a threat to global public information ecosystem security.
As an interdisciplinary research field combining natural language processing and social computing, fake news detection has garnered significant attention from researchers in recent years. Deep neural networks, owing to their powerful data modeling capabilities and strong performance in complex tasks, have consistently played a pivotal role in fake news detection.
Ma et al. [1] pioneered the application of recurrent neural networks to fake news detection, utilizing hidden layer vectors as input features for classifiers and achieving superior predictive performance. Chen et al. [2] categorized existing fake news detection methods based on content semantics, social context awareness, and knowledge-driven approaches. Garg et al. [3] focused on various linguistic features for effectively identifying fake news, including complexity features, readability metrics, psycholinguistic features, and stylistic features. Luvembe et al. [4] proposed a random forest with adaptive genetic weight updating, which utilizes deep normalized attention to enrich and extract dual sentiment features for classification.
Subsequently, multi-task learning, prompt learning, generative deep learning, and graph neural networks have gradually been introduced into fake news detection tasks. Ma et al. [5] first applied multi-task learning to this task, using a recurrent neural network as the backbone to simultaneously train both fake news detection and stance detection tasks. The collaborative training between tasks enhanced the performance of the fake news detection model. Jiang et al. [6] proposed knowledge prompt learning. First, prompt learning was applied to fake news detection by designing complex prompt templates and relevant verbalizer words. Second, external knowledge was integrated into the prompt representation to enhance its expressive power, thereby improving the accuracy of predicting verbalizer words. Cheng et al. [7] utilized a variational autoencoder to encode news text, generating text embeddings that were then applied to multi-task learning. Vaibhav et al. [8] treated sentences in news articles as nodes and constructed edges based on node similarity, transforming the fake news detection task into a graph classification problem. Atanasova et al. [9] employed the DistilBERT network architecture for joint learning of news content and factual information, not only predicting the veracity of news but also using the Transformer’s decoder structure to generate explanatory statements about the verdict of news. Ma et al. [10] demonstrated that detection methods based on generative adversarial networks, which use generators to produce rumors and augment training data by transforming rumors into non-rumors and vice versa, along with discriminators to detect fake news, continuously optimize both the generator and discriminator to improve the robustness and classification accuracy of model predictions. Huang et al. [11] proposed a self-critical sequence training framework guided by natural language inference to generate training data with a writing style similar to that of humans.
Compared to traditional media, although single-domain fake news detection holds certain value, contemporary news and posts often encompass information across multiple domains. Single-domain detection methods struggle to effectively address the increasing volume of fake news. Consequently, multi-domain and cross-domain fake news detection has increasingly become a research hotspot. Nan et al. [12] pioneered the multi-domain fake news detection task, established a baseline model for this task, and released a multi-domain dataset. Zhu et al. [13] integrated emotional and stylistic features with textual features of news to predict its veracity and uncovered potential domain labels of news through a domain memory mechanism. Nan et al. [14] employed meta-learning methods to train on multi-domain datasets, constructing a general prediction model, and evaluated its transfer performance to new domains using perplexity as a metric. Yue et al. [15] utilized meta-learning techniques to enable models to learn the capability of detecting news across different domains. To achieve few-shot fake information detection with domain adaptation, this method trains an initial model on multiple source tasks and calculates similarity scores between the model and meta-tasks, adjusting meta-gradients based on similarity to enable adaptive feature extraction from source tasks. Ran et al. [16] addressed the domain shift issue in news across different domains by adopting instance-level and prototype-level contrastive learning methods, combined with a cross-attention mechanism, to enhance the model’s robustness in cross-domain prediction. Choudhry et al. [17] used sentiment classification as a research metric for cross-domain fake news detection and experimentally demonstrated the effectiveness of sentiment-guided methods in cross-domain fake news detection. Zeng et al. [18] explored the application of zero-shot learning on test sets to enhance model adaptability. Liu et al. [19] employed adversarial learning techniques to address the challenges of out-of-distribution data samples. Liang et al. [20] introduced a model utilizing a soft-sharing mechanism. This model obtains domain representations of news articles through an expert module network and then integrates news content features using these domain representations. Wei et al. [21] proposed a cross-domain fake news detection method (FADED) based on dual-granularity adversarial training. The authors observed fluctuations in the relationship between entities and labels across different domains, a difference overlooked by existing methods, leading to entity bias in models. To address this, FADED conducts adversarial training at two granularities—document-level and entity-level—to build encoders capable of generating domain-invariant representations. Fu et al. [22] proposed a knowledge graph-based model to alleviate domain differences at the lexical level by integrating external knowledge and improving BERT. It specifically addresses challenges such as heterogeneous embedding spaces and knowledge noise, introducing soft position and visible matrices to tackle these issues while employing label smoothing to mitigate the impact of label noise. Li et al. [23] proposed a precise domain localization method, utilizing multiple BERT models for word embeddings and multiple extraction networks for feature extraction. They designed a domain localization module to accurately identify the domain of news to address domain shift issues. Gu et al. [24] proposed a star-topology multi-domain fake news detection model that explicitly models the domains of fake news, leveraging a star topology to integrate domain-shared and domain-specific features. Wang et al. [25] introduced a topic embedding module to further mitigate the phenomenon of knowledge shift.
3. Proposed Method 3.1. Overall Architecture of MVFA To integrate multiple views, such as news text, sentiment, and style for fake news detection, this paper proposes a fake news detection method based on MVFA. The overall structure of the MVFA model is illustrated in Figure 1. In order to address the issue of weak correlations between textual domain features, sentiment features, and stylistic features with token features, which hinders the effective utilization of features beyond tokens for detection, the MVFA model incorporates a fusion feature attention module (Fusion Attention) and a news domain-aware selection module (Feature Select). To enhance the extraction efficiency of token features, the proposed model employs RCNN [26] for feature extraction of token features. The multi-view attention mechanism is utilized to recalculate and redistribute feature weights, while a gated network optimizes and controls the feature selection process.

The Token Feature, Emotion Feature, and Style Feature are processed through the RCNN-extractor, BiLSTM, and MLP, respectively, to obtain their respective abstract features, which are then concatenated to form the original shared features of the news. These original features suffer from issues such as redundant expression and incomplete representation of individual features, necessitating optimization and enhancement to provide higher-quality features for the classifier.
The MVFA model assigns a domain feature vector to each domain, which is used to record the domain-level features of news articles in each domain. Specifically, when the dataset contains N different domains, the model randomly generates N embeddings during initialization as domain feature vectors, with each vector uniquely corresponding to one domain. During the training process, the feature vector of the domain to which the news to be detected belongs serves as one of the inputs to the gated network, used to compute gating parameters. Each domain feature vector is trained solely on news from its corresponding domain and optimized based on the loss function. Therefore, this vector can be regarded as storing only the feature information of all news within that domain. As shown in Figure 1, the proposed MVFA can be broadly divided into two parts: the Multi-View Feature Extractor (MVFE) on the left and the Domain Feature Selector (DFS) on the right. The MVFE primarily extracts and interacts with the input multi-view features. The news content (Content) first passes through a Mask Attention layer to filter out some invalid expressions, then is concatenated with Emotional Feature and Style Feature to achieve multi-view feature fusion. Finally, the fused features are screened by an external view memory matrix to select important view features, obtaining an efficient multi-view feature representation. The DFS mainly relies on domain information to filter the fused multi-view features. Its inputs include the multi-view features and a learnable domain memory matrix. First, for the input multi-view features, a feature compression-expansion method is used to screen the key features from the input. Then, through matrix multiplication with the domain memory matrix, domain-relevant key features are obtained, achieving the encoding of multi-view features and domain information. Ultimately, the output includes feature weights that integrate multiple perspectives and have been processed by the domain-relevant selection module. These feature weights are element-wise multiplied with the original features to yield the final classification features. Finally, the classification features are passed through a classifier to output the probability of news authenticity. This process can be expressed by Equations (4)–(8). 3.2. Multi-View Fusion Attention The Multi-View Feature Extractor (MVFE) extracts and fuses features from three views: Token, Emotion, and Style, and then uses an external view memory matrix to compute the importance of different views. However, news in different domains should exhibit distinct distributions in terms of token usage, emotion, and style. Therefore, the multi-view fusion attention should not only judge the importance of features across multiple views of news but also describe the potential domain-related label distribution by modeling the relationships between domains and their corresponding token usage, emotion, and style.
Therefore, the input accepted by the Domain Feature Selector (DFS) is Inp = { M ( E txt ), E , S , Domain } (9)
where M ( · ) denotes the masked text feature obtained via the Mask Attention operation. Its main role is to selectively suppress domain-irrelevant or noisy features in cross-domain fake news detection, thereby enhancing the model’s generalization ability across different heterogeneous domains (e.g., politics, finance, entertainment). E and S are the emotion and style features of the news, respectively, and Domain is the domain label of the news. The text feature, emotion feature, and style feature are fed into the Fusion Attention module to capture their intrinsic correlations, yielding a latent representation of news features. This representation interacts with the domain label via an attention mechanism to produce a domain-aware feature representation. Finally, this feature representation is transformed into feature selection weights through multiple fully connected layers.
3.2.1. Fusion Feature Fusion Attention Unit The Fusion Feature Fusion Attention (F3A) Unit aims to refine the domain labels of news and construct rich domain representations. It consists of a hybrid feature perception unit and a domain memory unit.
The input of Fusion Attention consists of the concatenated features of Token, Emotion, and Style, as well as a trainable domain memory matrix. Initially, the three input features are information-isolated without any interaction among them. To achieve the interaction between Token, Emotion, and Style features and extract the associated information, the hybrid feature F f is first computed through an MLP with an “ESE” structure. “ESE” is a three-layer MLP: The first layer is the “Extractor layer”, a linear layer whose output dimension equals the input dimension, aiming to fuse multiple features. The second layer is the “Squeeze layer”, a linear layer whose output dimension is smaller than the input dimension, used to filter non-critical features. The third layer is the “Expand layer”, whose output dimension is equal to the input dimension of the first layer and activated by the sigmoid function, intended to obtain the importance weights of the original features.
To establish the relationship between domains and multi-view features, we design a domain memory unit to mine the latent associations between news and domain labels. The core idea is to maintain a domain memory matrix for each domain, which records the features of all news within that domain. For an input news item, to implicitly learn the feature correlations between the three feature types and the domain, and to strengthen the latent feature relationships between multi-view features and domains across different samples, the features are fed into the domain memory unit for processing. This enables the network to utilize sample-specific information for additional domain memory. The unit’s output, A d o m a i n , is obtained by multiplying the features with the domain memory matrix Md. Domain information is stored in this matrix. During training, domain-related information from the training sample features can be stored in the memory matrix; then, during inference, domain information about the sample features can be retrieved from this matrix. This enhances the network’s ability to model the domain aspects of sample features. The specific computational process of Fusion Attention is shown in Equations (10)–(13). 3.2.2. Domain-Based Feature Selection After Fusion Attention, the news sample features are processed by the domain memory mechanism to obtain a rich feature correlation representation related to the domain. This feature correlation representation is applied to the original features of the sample to enhance the feature representation ability. A d o m a i n , output of the Fusion Attention unit, incorporates multi-view feature information as well as memory encodings relevant to all domains. By incorporating the domain label of the news, it effectively assigns a weight—derived from domain label information—to each component of the original features. This weight originates from the analysis of correlations among various features (across multiple views) within Fusion Attention and the domain information of the sample. Finally, the Fusion Feature Fusion Attention module computes the feature weight representation for each view using multi-view feature information and domain information, and ultimately redistributes the weights through matrix multiplication.
3.3. RCNN-Extractor Extracting concatenated multi-view features directly via MLP may lead to insufficient information flow among multiple views and incomplete modeling of intra-view features. To more efficiently achieve inter-view correlation extraction and effective intra-view feature representation under multi-view feature scenarios, we propose to use RCNN to process complex multi-view feature data. Specifically, inspired by multi-scale convolution in computer vision, we apply 1D convolutions with kernel sizes of 1, 2, 3, 5, and 10 to further extract features from the multi-view feature data. The resulting feature maps are then concatenated and finally passed through an RNN to obtain the ultimate feature representation. The RCNN-extractor significantly reduces the number of model parameters and computational complexity through parameter sharing and local connections in convolutional kernels, thereby achieving efficient intra-view feature extraction. Leveraging the strengths of RNNs in modeling long-range dependencies in sequential data, it subsequently performs inter-view feature extraction via the RNN.
4. Experiments 4.1. Experimental Setup To verify the effectiveness of the proposed model, we conduct performance evaluation using the Weibo21 dataset [12]. This dataset covers 9 domains: technology, military, education, disaster, politics, health, finance, sports and entertainment, and society. The statistical distribution of news in each domain is shown in Table 1, each piece of data undergoes strict multi-domain labeling, independently judged by 10 annotators, and only adopted when at least 8 people agree, ensuring high reliability and consistency of domain labels. The English dataset consists of the English portions from the Fake-NewsNet [29] and MM-COVID datasets [30], including three domains: Gossipcop, Politifact, and COVID. The statistics of each domain are presented in Table 2. As can be seen from Table 1 and Table 2, there exists a significant discrepancy in data volume among different domains in both datasets. Since fake news is intentionally fabricated for economic or political benefits, it often contains opinionated and inflammatory language. Therefore, detecting fake news using linguistic features of news content is reasonable. On the other hand, the inflammatory language and writing styles vary across domains. Thus, employing multi-view analysis and domain-aware attention mechanisms for feature selection can effectively improve detection performance. It is worth noting that in fields with small sample sizes, their textual features (such as vocabulary, emotional tendencies, writing styles) may not fully represent the overall characteristics of the field. This makes it difficult for the model to capture sufficiently diverse and robust domain-specific patterns during training in these domains, thereby affecting the model’s classification accuracy and generalization ability for news in these domains.
The experimental model was implemented using the PyTorch 1.11.0 framework and ran on an RTX 3090 GPU with 24 GB of memory. The batch size was uniformly set to 256, and the early stopping strategy was configured to halt training if the F1 score did not improve for 10 consecutive epochs. The performance results for all models were taken from the epoch with the highest F1 score during the entire training process.
4.2. Performance Comparison We selected eight multi-domain fake news detection models—MMoE [31], MoSE [32], EDDFN [33], MDFEND [12], M3FEND [13], KG-MFND [22], PLFND [23], and StarMFND [24]—as baselines for comparison. MMoE and MoSE are multi-task learning models. MMoE employs multiple sets of expert networks and multiple gating networks to control the feature combination process for different tasks. MoSE is an improvement upon MMoE, incorporating an LSTM layer as a shared bottom layer before the expert networks. In the comparative experiments, we partitioned the dataset by domain and treated prediction tasks on different domains as distinct learning tasks, enabling MMoE and MoSE to simultaneously predict news across multiple domains. EDDFN is a model designed for cross-domain fake news detection. It implicitly models different domains while preserving both domain-specific and cross-domain knowledge. MDFEND, M3FEND, KG-MFND, PLFND, and StarMFND are end-to-end multi-domain fake news detection models that leverage various techniques to encode domain information into the model for multi-domain detection. We compared the proposed model against the aforementioned baselines. The prediction results on the Chinese dataset are presented in Table 3, with F1 score visualizations shown in Figure 2. It can be observed that the proposed MVFA outperforms all comparison models across the F1 and AUC metrics. The prediction results on the English dataset are shown in Table 4. MVFA achieves higher F1 scores than all other models across all domains, and also attains optimal values for the remaining evaluation metrics.
The experimental results in Table 3 demonstrate that our model outperforms all baseline models on the key performance metric, F1 score. The proposed model achieves the highest F1 scores in five domains: education, politics, finance, entertainment, and society. Experimental results on the English dataset, presented in Table 4, indicate that the proposed model attains absolute advantages across all domains, with F1 score improvements of 0.65%, 1.79%, and 1.27% over the suboptimal models, respectively. Furthermore, it achieves optimal results across F1 score, ACC, and AUC metrics on the English dataset. These findings demonstrate that the proposed MVFA is an effective approach for multi-domain fake news detection.
MVFA significantly outperforms MMoE, MoSE, and EDDFN among multi-domain approaches. This is because EANN, MMoE, and MoSE all rely on a shared bottom layer and multiple independent task-specific heads to learn features across different domains, employing a sharing mechanism to acquire cross-domain shared knowledge. However, due to the differing distributions of textual style, sentiment, and other features across domains, the shared bottom layer struggles to balance training biases arising from varying sample sizes across different domains. Consequently, it is challenging to directly learn common knowledge from multiple domains with heterogeneous structures. In contrast, our model integrates useful entity knowledge through domain knowledge embedding—a soft, domain-based feature screening mechanism. MDFEND and M3FEND also employ soft feature masking for feature selection. However, MDFEND overlooks the importance of raw textual features—particularly long-range dependencies among words in news text—during the domain-guided feature selection process. M3FEND introduces a domain memory repository to enrich domain information but neglects the varying importance of different feature memories within the repository. In contrast, the fusion attention mechanism in MVFA enables selective memorization of features across different domains. The performance of MVFA on the Chinese dataset is only 0.53% higher than StarMFND, while there is indeed a 2.00% improvement on the English dataset. Combined with MVFA’s 6.44% improvement on Polotiact classification on the English dataset compared to StarMFND, this is due to an imbalanced sample size. The difference in sample size between categories in the Chinese dataset is relatively small, so it can perform relatively well without domain memory. However, the number of Polotiacts in the English dataset is significantly less than other categories, making domain memory modeling particularly important.
A comparison between Table 1 and Table 3 reveals that the proposed MVFA achieves near-optimal results in domains with a larger number of samples. However, it does not attain optimal performance in domains with relatively fewer samples (e.g., science and military). This may be attributed to sample imbalance or performance degradation caused by the unique characteristics of domain-specific texts. Addressing these issues will be a focus of future work.
4.3. Ablation Study To verify the effectiveness of each proposed module, ablation experiments are conducted in this section by discarding or replacing specific components. Three variants of MVFA are implemented.
(1) . “w/o DFS” refers to the configuration where the Domain Feature Selector branch is removed, and the multi-view features are directly fed into the classifier for prediction.
(2) . “w/o Fusion Attention” refers to the configuration where the Fusion Attention module is removed from the Domain Feature Selector branch and replaced with a fully connected layer.
(3) . “w/o RCNN-extractor” refers to the configuration where the proposed RCNN-extractor is replaced with an MLP.
The experimental results are shown in Table 5 and Figure 3.
The experimental results presented in Table 5 and Figure 3 demonstrate that our model achieves optimal performance among all variants. When the Domain Feature Selector branch is removed, model performance significantly declines, with F1 scores decreasing by 10.96% and 4.65% on the Chinese and English datasets, respectively. This is because domain-based feature selection effectively filters raw news features—a filtering process that is essential for accurate fake news detection. The feature fusion within the Domain Feature Selector branch ultimately produces domain-specific feature weights, which effectively facilitate the classifier in generating more efficient feature representations. Removing the Fusion Attention module from the Domain Feature Selector also leads to degraded model performance. This indicates that the raw concatenated news features input to Fusion Attention contain substantial noise, causing the domain memory matrix to memorize redundant and noisy features, thereby preventing the achievement of optimal results. The RCNN-extractor is designed to better extract multiple news features and their intercorrelations. Compared to MLP, RCNN possesses the capability to preserve high-level abstract features across multiple views—including semantics, sentiment, and style—and progressively compose them into more abstract representations. This results in more robust extracted features and enhanced model generalization. Therefore, employing the RCNN-extractor yields better performance than using an MLP.
Furthermore, examining the experimental results with and without the Domain Feature Selector branch on both the Chinese and English datasets reveals that the performance degradation on the Chinese dataset is substantially greater than that on the English dataset when the Domain Feature Selector branch is absent. One contributing factor is the relatively smaller overall data volume of the Chinese dataset compared to the English dataset. However, we consider the more significant reason to be the greater number of domains present in the Chinese dataset. Consequently, the selective memorization and filtering of fusion features guided by domain information play a crucial role in fake news detection.
5. Conclusions To address the limitation of insufficient utilization of multi-view features and domain information in multi-domain fake news detection, a method employing Multi-view Fusion Attention for effective fusion of multi-view feature information and encoding of domain information is proposed. Firstly, multi-view features of news are filtered using an external view memory-based approach. Subsequently, domain information is encoded into the multi-view features by combining multiple view information with a domain memory matrix, thereby making the features fed into the classifier more domain-specific. Finally, to enhance semantic feature extraction, an RCNN-extractor is adopted for extracting news semantic features. Experiments on both Chinese and English datasets demonstrate that the proposed MVFA successfully captures domain information and multi-view features along with their correlations, achieving superior performance compared to recent advanced methods.
Although the MVFA model proposed in this study has been shown to achieve superior performance in multi-domain fake news detection, certain limitations remain. The current model is primarily built upon semantic, emotional, and stylistic features extracted from news text, while external contextual information and fact-checking knowledge have not been fully integrated. This may limit its capability in handling complex misinformation that heavily relies on background knowledge. For example, in domains such as political or scientific news, where rich background knowledge is essential, authenticity may not be adequately determined based solely on intrinsic textual features. Posts on Weibo are typically presented in short-text form, characterized by informal language and the frequent use of internet slang and emojis. Although multimodal content such as images and videos is supported by the platform, only the textual modality is considered in this study, leaving multimodal misinformation unaddressed. Moreover, as indicated by the experimental results, the model performs slightly worse in domains with limited sample sizes, such as science and military domains in Chinese datasets, which may be attributed to domain-specific data distributions or class imbalance. Beyond fake news detection on social media, more challenging benchmark datasets, such as FEVER [34] and SciFact [35], have been introduced in academia, with a focus on fact verification and evidence-based reasoning, requiring models to not only identify misinformation but also retrieve evidence to support or refute claims. In contrast to these benchmarks, this study is focused on text-based multi-domain fake news detection in social media environments, with the aim of addressing issues such as domain shift and cross-domain correlations. Through the use of a multi-view fusion attention mechanism, the generalization capability and detection accuracy of the proposed method have been effectively improved across different domains, without reliance on external fact-checking knowledge. Future work will be directed toward the integration of knowledge graphs, event timelines, or multimodal feature fusion to further enhance the robustness and generalization of the model. Additionally, the combination of the feature fusion and domain adaptation strategies proposed in this study with fact-checking approaches is expected to be explored in future research to address more complex challenges in fake news detection.
In terms of societal and technical utility, the proposed framework is designed to be deployable in real-world moderation systems where domain shifts are common and external knowledge is often unavailable. By relying only on textual, sentiment, and stylistic features, it balances effectiveness with practicality, offering a cost-efficient solution for platforms operating in low-resource or knowledge-constrained environments.
