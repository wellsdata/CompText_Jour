Introduction
Online social networks are a well-liked method of communication. People interact with one another and share their daily activities, images, and status updates. People love using social networking sites like Facebook [1]. With the development of communication technologies and social media, fake news is expanding quickly. Fake news detection is a new field of research that is receiving a lot of attention [2]. In today’s social media and political world, fake news plays a significant role. The detection of fake news requires significant investigation, but there are also certain difficulties [3].
Misinformation is a matter of grave concern and a complicated ecosystem with many actors that produce and disseminate content that may be true or may have harmful implications, such as incorrect information or malicious intent. This condition is made possible by people’s increased ability to share concepts and get data from many different content suppliers [4]. Social media has taken over everyone's life in recent years. Most fake news is disseminated via social media [5]. Fake news poses a threat to politics, business, finance, education, and democracy. Even while fake news is not a new issue, people today tend to place greater trust in social media, which encourages them to believe it and share it. Nowadays, it’s difficult to tell the difference between accurate and misleading news, which leads to issues and misunderstandings [5]. Ever since the internet took off, there has been a problem with fake news.
The identical network that gives us access to worldwide news is the ideal environment for the growth of malevolent and false information [6]. Because information shapes how people see the world, it is crucial to combat this fake news. People establish their own opinions in addition to basing critical judgments on knowledge. If this information is inaccurate, terrible things may happen. It is virtually impossible for a human to independently verify every single piece of news [6]. In the past, information was more strictly regulated by journalists, librarians, and members of the media, but with the open and pervasive nature of the digital world, it has become more challenging for these conventional gatekeepers to regulate and verify the information. However, the consequences of this propagation of false information and disinformation could be devastating and extremely hazardous [7].
While the propagation of false information and fake news has drawn attention from many sectors of society, individuals working in the information science disciplines are becoming increasingly concerned. Fake news has become a serious issue in recent years, particularly with the advent of social media and other internet platforms. Detecting false news is a difficult problem that necessitates the use of advanced technologies such as natural language processing (NLP), machine learning, and deep learning [8, 9]. Several ways to detect fake news have been developed, including:
Content-based approaches: These approaches examine the substance of the news piece for indicators of bias, sensationalism, or inconsistency. NLP tools are used to find patterns in the text that indicate the article is a forgery. Social network-based techniques: These [9] Approaches examine the social network activity surrounding the news piece for evidence of manipulation, such as bots or fake accounts [10]. They also examine sharing patterns and engagement levels to see if the material is being shared unusually.
Hybrid approaches These methods mix content-based and social network-based methodologies to provide a more in-depth study of the news story. Some of the cutting-edge approaches utilized in fake news identification include Deep learning models These methods analyze enormous volumes of data using neural networks to find patterns that indicate a news article is fraudulent.
Attention mechanisms These mechanisms allow the model to focus on specific elements of the news story that are most important in deciding whether or not it is false. Transfer learning is training a model on a large dataset of comparable tasks, such as sentiment analysis or topic modeling, and then fine-tuning it to detect fake news. Despite these advances, detecting false news remains a difficult task [11]. There are still [12] several ways for fake news to be transmitted and manipulated, and new techniques to avoid detection are continually being created. However, it is believed that with further study and development, viable solutions to this expanding problem can be discovered. Research gap
While the aforementioned review titled “A review of deep learning-based models for Afan Oromo fake news on social media” offers valuable insights into the utilization of deep learning models for identifying counterfeit news within the specific context of Afan Oromo language and social media platforms, it is imperative to acknowledge certain constraints. Firstly, the review’s scope is limited, as it exclusively concentrates on Afan Oromo language and social media platforms, impeding the findings’ general applicability to other languages and online platforms. The efficacy of deep learning models in detecting fake news may differ across various languages and social media ecosystems. Secondly, the review may be founded on a particular dataset of Afan Oromo fake news articles, potentially introducing bias and influencing the generalizability of the results. Consequently, it is crucial to consider the diversity and representativeness of the data employed in training and evaluating deep learning models for fake news detection. Thirdly, the review might not encompass a comprehensive comparative analysis between deep learning-based models and other conventional machine learning approaches or heuristic-based methods for fake news detection. A comparative analysis would furnish a more comprehensive understanding of the strengths and weaknesses inherent in different detection techniques. Furthermore, the review may emphasize a limited set of evaluation metrics, such as accuracy or precision, while neglecting other significant metrics such as recall, F1 score, or interpretability. A more comprehensive evaluation framework would yield a more nuanced assessment of the performance of deep learning models. Lastly, the review may overlook ethical considerations associated with fake news detection, such as privacy concerns, potential biases in algorithmic decision-making, or unintended consequences of automated content moderation. It is essential to meticulously contemplate ethical implications in developing and implementing deep learning models for fake news detection.

Result and finding
Evaluating the output of a machine-learning model is a critical stage in a predictive modeling pipeline. An evaluation matrix is a critical tool for planning and organizing an evaluation, and the confusion matrix displays a summary of model performance on the testing dataset [36]. The number of labels in most classification problems is fixed, so determining the score for each class and the loss from the ground truth is straightforward. In the case of NLP, even though the output format is predetermined, dimensions cannot be chosen, as a single remark can be presented in a variety of ways without changing its intent or meaning. If we are attempting to solve two problems with one model, evaluation measures are critical for assessing its performance. Various evaluation measures will be utilized to assess the effectiveness of algorithms for the false news identification problem.
The majority of current methods view the fake news problem as a classification issue that seeks to determine if a news story is false or not [37]. The Afaan Oromo false news dataset was used in this study to test the performance of the machine learning models, and the confusion matrix was used to represent the classification’s four possible outcomes. Conclusion
This study examines fake news detection techniques in Afan Oromo using deep learning methods. It compares machine learning and deep learning algorithms, focusing on three categories of deceptive information: liars, fake news publications, and corpus datasets. The findings suggest the need for specialized datasets and advanced models to mitigate fake news spread and enhance information credibility. The paper analyzes various deep-learning models for detecting fake news in the Afaan Oromo language, discussing model performance, data challenges, feature extraction techniques, implications for social media, and future directions. The analysis primarily focuses on textual data, but future research could benefit from analyzing image data alongside textual information. The review critically examines various methods for identifying fake news, including natural language processing (NLP) and advanced deep learning (DL) strategies. It explores diverse NLP techniques and deep learning architectures, examining various assessment metrics and outlining potential future research trajectories. The ongoing evolution and development of innovative deep learning network architectures significantly reduce the risk of obtaining erroneous results when implementing deep learning-based models. The findings of this review are promising and reliable due to the ongoing discourse surrounding this pressing societal challenge. The study provides an exhaustive examination of the mechanisms underlying the identification of false news, emphasizing its defining characteristics, critical features, comprehensive taxonomy, various types of data, categorization of false news, and detection methodologies employed to unearth fake news.
