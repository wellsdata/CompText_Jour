Introduction
In the modernized world, social media [1, 2] plays the major role in the life of people. It becomes the part of daily activity as the easy accessing and cost-fee medium to share information all over the world in a no time. Every day, People are spending several hours [3] in a social media. There are various type of social media platforms which comprises of both the authentic and fake news. In recent times, enormous false news [4, 5] take over the social media which affects the people life of all ages, both mentally [6] and physically. For Instance, the fake stories in the digital platform impacts the children lives by influencing to take on misleading views of the world which make them or others harms in the real world. Similarly, in the society [7], the misrepresentation or fake news [8] have the ability to divide the public opinion on a specific issue which leads to discouragement of various processes that are important to the society [9]. Besides, the fake news are made intentionally or unintentionally on the particular person, leads to foster anxiety and depression [10] which even spoil the quality of life. It is necessary [11, 12] to detect the fake news in order to avoid the several consequences such as decrease of offence on individuals, general public, political party or organization. Traditional methods are fake news detection can be error-prone as the humans is unable to identify all the fake news. Further, the investigation by the department of police is the time intensive and complicated. To resolve the issue of the traditional detection, technology based effective detection mechanism for fake news is required. Artificial intelligence (AI) is the one such technology which are widely used for the detection of false information in the social media platform. It uses Deep Learning (DL) and Machine Learning (ML) algorithm for the detection and classification process.
Correspondingly, several conventional methods utilized ML and DL for the false news detection. For instance, the conventional technique has analyzed certain classifiers for the identification of fake news. For that, SVM, NB, RF, LR has been utilized for the classification. Here, LIAR dataset has been used for the classification. The outcome of the exiting method signifies the LR and SVM system achieved better performance in the fake news detection [13]. Similarly, Passive Aggregation (PA), Linear Support Vector (LSVC) and Logistic Regression (LR) has been used for the detection of fake news. Here, three diverse sets of dataset has been used in the existing system such as return websites, kaggle and trends. The efficacy of the traditional model has been signified through the results with the higher accuracy of 94.3% [14]. Likewise, eleven Ml based classifiers has been used for the classification of fake news. Here, FakeNewsNet dataset has been used in the existing system. The effective performance of the conventional model has been signified through the results with the higher accuracy of 94.5% [15]. Though several conventional methods accomplished in the classification of fake news, are limited by inadequate accuracy, speed and better handling of larger datasets.
To resolve the issue, the proposed system employs Modified Feature Attention for CNN-BiLSTM system for the classification of fake news. For that purpose, four diverse dataset is used such as GossipCop, LAIR, Politifact and WELFake datasets. Primarily, loaded dataset is preprocessed with three techniques such as removing special caharcters, tokenisation, stemming and vectorisation. Further, the data is divided for training and testing where eighty percent of data utilizes for training and twenty percent of data utilizes for testing. Formely, the classification is processed through Modified Feature Attention for CNN-BiLSTM system. Then, the model is evaluated in the prediction phase. Finally, the efficiency of the respective model is calculated using performance metrics.
Objectives and motivations tied to gaps in the literature
The suggested system presents a significant improvement over current fake news detection methods by integrating a Modified Feature Attention with CNN-BiLSTM architecture. This integration effectively addresses the challenges faced by traditional methods, such as accuracy, speed, and scalability. The system leverages the power of deep learning for a more accurate and efficient classification of fake news. The use of CNN boosts the model's capability to extract hierarchical features from text data, while the BiLSTM component enhances the understanding of context by capturing long-range dependencies. Furthermore, the system introduces a modified feature attention mechanism that allows the model to concentrate on essential information within news articles, thereby improving its ability to distinguish between real and fake content. The proposed approach help to tackle the limitations of existing research and provides a strong solution to curb the widespread dissemination of misinformation on social media platforms, ultimately protecting the integrity of online information ecosystems.
Research questions
The research questions at the core of this study revolve around the efficacy and applicability of the proposed Modified Feature Attention-based CNN-BiLSTM approach for fake news classification. Firstly, the study seeks to ascertain the extent to which this novel architecture improves upon the limitations of traditional fake news detection methods, particularly in terms of accuracy, speed, and scalability. Secondly, it aims to investigate the effectiveness of the integrated feature attention mechanism in enhancing the model's ability to discern between genuine and fabricated news articles by focusing on relevant textual features. Additionally, the research questions delve into the generalizability of the proposed system across diverse datasets sourced from various social media platforms, such as GossipCop, LAIR, Politifact, and WELFake, with a focus on understanding its performance across different contexts and types of misinformation.

Pre-processing
Handling datasets in text classification presents various challenges. Data quality issues, such as inconsistencies and noise, can impede the model’s ability to learn effectively. Class imbalance, where certain categories are underrepresented, can result in biased models that struggle with minority classes. Text pre-processing is vital, as variations in formatting and tokenization can significantly impact performance. Additionally, feature extraction with high-dimensional embeddings poses computational challenges. Concerns about overfitting and generalization arise, especially with limited or unrepresentative training data. Ensuring high annotation quality and addressing these issues through careful evaluation are essential for improving model accuracy and robustness. To overome this issues, an effecient pre-processing strategy is necessary to ingest the data in a NN for fake news identification, as the data present in the input dataset are mostly noisy, unstructured and fragmented. The loaded datasets are applied for pre-processing that involves different approaches namely, removing special characters, tokenisation and vectorisation.
- Lowercasing: All text data is transformed to lowercase to maintain consistency and avoid discrepancies due to case sensitivity.

- Removing Punctuations and Special Characters: Non-alphanumeric characters, including punctuation marks and special symbols, are eliminated from the text data using regular expressions.

- Tokenization 1: The nltk.word_tokenize() function is used to break down the text into individual words or tokens.

- Stop Words Removal: Words like “the”, “is”, “and”, etc., which don’t add significant meaning to the text, are removed from the tokenized text data to reduce noise and enhance the efficiency of subsequent analysis.

- Joining Tokens: The tokens left after removing stop words are re-joined to form pre-processed text.

- Tokenization 2: The pre-processed text is tokenized once more to create a list of individual words.

- Stop Words Removal: Stop words are removed again from the tokenized text data.

- Lemmatization: The WordNetLemmatizer() is used to apply lemmatization to the tokenized text data. Lemmatization brings words to their base or root form, which aids in standardizing the text data and enhancing its quality for analysis.

- Stemming: The PorterStemmer() is used for stemming on the tokenized text data. Stemming also reduces words to their base or root form by removing suffixes, further standardizing the text data.



These pre-processing steps are essential for cleaning and standardizing text data, making it ready for tasks like classification or clustering. The same pre-processing technique is used consistently for all the datasets.
Train-test split phase
The pre-processed feature vectors are further fed into train-test split phase, where the train data is used for training the proposed classifier and test data identifies the predicted label of fake news based on the trained model. The train and test data are split with the ration of 80:20, in which 80% of data belongs to train data and 20% of test data. Further, the train data is tranferred into classifier model.
Classification
The train dataset is employed for classification of real and fake news present in the dataset using Modified Feature Attention based CNN-BiLSTM classifier.
Convolutional neural network (CNN)
Word embedding is integrated into the CNN :
- Word Tokenization: The input sentences are broken down into individual words or tokens. This allows for further analysis at the word level.

- Word Embedding Matrix: Each tokenized word is mapped to a dense vector representation, known as word embeddings. These embeddings capture the semantic relationships between words, enabling the model to understand the contextual meaning of each word.

- Training with Word Vectors: In the context of natural language processing (NLP), word vectors are initialized with pre-trained embeddings, such as Word2Vec, GloVe, or FastText. Alternatively, the model can learn word embeddings from scratch during training, adjusting the vectors based on the context of the input text and the objective of the classification task.

- Convolutional Layer: The word embeddings serve as input features during the convolutional layer's operation. The model applies a series of convolutional filters to the input, extracting local features or patterns from the text.

- Pooling Layer: After convolution, the pooled features are aggregated using pooling operations such as max pooling or average pooling. This step reduces the dimensionality of the feature maps while retaining the most salient information.



By incorporating word embedding techniques into the CNN architecture, the model can understand the underlying semantic structure of the input text, facilitating more effective feature extraction and classification.
Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM)
Word embedding is a crucial part of the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM) processes:
- Input Embedding: The input text, represented as sequences of word tokens, is first mapped to their corresponding word embeddings. These embeddings provide a continuous representation of the input words, capturing their semantic meanings and relationships.

- Bi-LSTM Processing: The word embeddings are then fed into the Bi-LSTM layer, which processes the input text bidirectionally. The Bi-LSTM architecture allows the model to capture both forward and backward contextual information within the input sequences, enabling it to learn complex patterns and dependencies.

- Hidden States: As the input embeddings propagate through the Bi-LSTM layers, hidden states are generated at each time step, encapsulating the model's internal representation of the input sequence. These hidden states encode valuable information about the sequential structure of the text, which is crucial for accurate classification.

Modified feature attention mechanism
The attention mechanism is an important component in the proposed classification model, as it allows the model to concentrate on significant elements from the modified CNN-BiLSTM layers. Before applying attention, all input information needs to be condensed into a fixed-length vector. Words carry different levels of importance, and the attention mechanism, in conjunction with feature and correlation matrices, is used in the proposed model to highlight the significance of each sentence and focus on pertinent parts of the input sequence. The feature matrix is composed of the input sequence, while the correlation matrix holds information about the relationship between different parts of the sequence. The modified feature attention mechanism works by taking a weighted sum of the feature matrix, with the weights being determined by the correlation matrix. This procedure includes the normalization of the hidden representation of the context vector, which is learned during the training process. Structure of the proposed model:
- Input Layer: The model starts with an input layer that accepts sequences of integers, which represent word indices.

- Embedding Layer: The input sequences are passed to an embedding layer, which transforms each word index into a dense vector representation.

- Convolutional Layer (Conv1D): The embedded sequences are then processed by a 1D convolutional layer. This layer applies 32 filters of size 3 to the input sequences, performing convolution operations along the temporal dimension.

- Max Pooling Layer (MaxPooling1D): The output from the convolutional layer is passed through a max pooling layer with a pool size of 4.

- Bidirectional LSTM Layer: The pooled sequences are fed into a bidirectional LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) layer with 128 units in each direction.

- Attention Mechanism: An attention mechanism is applied to the output sequences from the bidirectional LSTM layer.

- Global Max Pooling Layer (GlobalMaxPooling1D): The output sequences from the bidirectional LSTM layer are also passed through a global max pooling layer.

- Feature and Correlation Matrices (Dense Layers): The global max pooled sequences are separately passed through dense layers to obtain feature and correlation matrices.

- Multiplication (Multiply Layer): The feature and correlation matrices are element-wise multiplied to emphasize relevant features based on correlations.

- Final Output (Dense Layer): The multiplied output is then passed through a dense layer with Softmax activation, which predicts the probabilities of the input belonging to each class.



This model architecture combines convolutional, recurrent, and attention-based mechanisms to effectively process sequential data and make accurate predictions.

Accuracy = Tp + Tn Tp + Fp + Fn + Tn where Fp, Fn Tp, and Tn, describes false positive, false negative, true positive and true negative.
2. Precision: The precision of the proposed system is represented as the tendency of the classifier to correctly detect the positive class and is given by the Eq. (20), 20
Precision = Tp Tp + Fp
3. Recall: Recall is defined as the ratio of number of times the classifier detected the negative class to all the time the negative class is obtained, 21
Recall = Tp Tp + Fn
4. F1 Score: F1-score is the metric that depends on both the values of precision and recall and is given by the formula (22), 22

Conclusion
Communicating using social networks is fast and easy to share information one place to another. The collaboration of several social network sites associatively generates Terabytes of information every second. However, the fast scattering of data at high rate with minimal effort creates a widespread of fake news that are hazardous to people and society. So, an efficient approach is required to solve the extensive spread of false information and to verify the authenticity of the content. Various researchers developed techniques based on fake news detection, with different sets of features. But, these techniques provides a limitation of automatically identifying suitable features and classification models, to generate better accuracy for fake news detection. To overcome these issues, the proposed approach implemented modified feature attention based CNN-BiLSTM, to effectively improve the precision of classification of real and fake news from the given dataset. The method applied four different datasets such as GossipCop dataset, LAIR dataset, Politifact dataset and WELFake dataset. The proposed model employed the hybrid CNN-BiLSTM, to increase the learning ability of the system and to optimise the ability of the proposed approach using different datasets. Attention mechanism with feature matrix and correlation matrix was incorporated into the approach in order to predict the most relevant information from input sequence. Thus, the efficiency of proposed method was analysed based on probabilistic metrics namely, recall, precision, F1-score and accuracy. From analysis, the accuracy produced by proposed system with GossipCop dataset was 82%, LAIR dataset was 50%, Politifact dataset was 84% and WELFake dataset was 97%. Moreover, the comparison of proposed methodology with other conventional techniques was performed, to predict the efficacy of the model. Therefore, the proposed model produced better accuracy, which possesses the potential of tackling fake news classification. However, the proposed approach can further be enhanced by employing different datasets with better accuracy and explore advanced data augmentation techniques to increase the diversity of the training dataset, especially for underrepresented classes or scenarios. The present model encounters scalability issues in processing large-scale data in real-time and faces substantial computational demands, which may not be feasible in all applications. The modified feature attention-based CNN-BiLSTM model faces several challenges, including design complexity, high computational demands, and the need to manage diverse fake news content. Additionally, scalability, real-time processing, seamless integration into social media platforms, and ethical concerns regarding false positives and negatives complicate its effective deployment and use. Future prospect of the project is to develop methods for incorporating user feedback into the model’s training and evaluation process to improve performance and user satisfaction.
