Introduction Online social networks (OSNs) which have provided a strong platform for communications changed the world significantly by allowing efficient exchange of information. Billions of users have integrated social networks as their primary sources of daily information (Pham et al. 2018). However, OSNs also allow spreading misinformation and rumors, which can lead to economic losses (Domm 2013), as well as negative impacts on the public (Allcott and Gentzkow 2016). Therefore, it is necessary to develop effective strategies to block misinformation and rumors on OSNs.
Information diffusion models are the foundations for the study on preventing misinformation. Kempe et al. (2003) first proposed two diffusion models Independent Cascade ( IC ) and Linear Threshold ( LT ) for investigating Influence Maximization ( IM ) problem. Based on such models and their variants, recently, there is an effective strategy to block the influence spread of given source by removing the nodes or edges that play an important role in propagation process from networks (Tong et al. 2010; Prakash et al. 2010; Khalil et al. 2014; Pham et al. 2018, 2017). Some works have proposed a strategy of placing monitor machines (Zhang et al. 2016a) or immunization vaccines (Zhang and Prakash 2015; Zhang et al. 2016b; Song et al. 2015) on some nodes to against the spread of known misinformation or epidemic sources. Basically, placing monitors or vaccination on a node is equivalent to removing this node from the network during propagation process under diffusion models. The authors in Zhang et al. (2016a) formulated the problem of placing monitor at a set of nodes so that information spreading from known sources of misinformation to protected central nodes is not greater than the protection threshold.
Zhang and Prakash (2015) have developed vaccination strategies for nodes to limit the spread of disease on social networks on the IC model. The similar methods have also been applied for edges and nodes to control propagation at groups under LT model (Zhang et al. 2016b). Later on, authors in Song et al. (2015), Pham et al. (2018), Pham et al. (2017) studied the problem of limiting misinformation by combining time constraint on a various of IC and LT models, and they also designed algorithms that outperform the previous ones.
In this paper, we investigate a dual problem of existing studies in the literature of this topic. In particular, we aim to find the smallest set of nodes whose (minimize the budget) removal from a social network reduces the influence of misinformation spread to at least a threshold γ , called Targeted Misinformation Blocking ( TMB ) problem.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows.
- We formulate the Targeted Misinformation Blocking problem based on both IC and LT models.

- We prove that TMB problem is #P-hard under LT model and is NP-hard even when the network is a directed acyclic graph under IC model.

- Under LT model, we prove that the objective function is monotone and submodular function. Base on that, we propose a nature greedy algorithm which provides a ratio of 1 + ln ( γ / ϵ ) . We further propose a scalable algorithm for TMB on large-scale networks, named STMB - LT . However, we show that influence reduction is no longer submodular under IC model and we design an efficient algorithm, called STMB - IC .

- Extensive experiments are conducted on various real-world social networks. The results show that the proposed algorithms STMB - LT and STMB - IC outperform other heuristic algorithms in term of both effectiveness and scalability. In particular, STMB - IC provides significantly higher quality solution, as well as faster running time than the state-of-the-art methods.

Related work Due to the enormous harms of misinformation to the public, misinformation blocking has been paid significant attention from academia and engineering. Some authors proposed decontamination information approaches to encounter misinformation spreading by injecting good or official information into some nodes which have been investigated (Budak et al. 2011; Nguyen et al. 2013; He et al. 2012; Tong et al. 2017; Song et al. 2017). For instance, Budak et al. (2011) investigated a problem of selecting a seed set to initialize positive cascade so that the number of users influenced by negative information is minimal. By utilizing the reverse sampling method, (Tong et al. 2017) designed an efficient randomized algorithm for rumor blocking which provide a ( 1 - 1 / e - ϵ ) -approximation with high probability. He et al. (2012) studied Influence Blocking Maximization problem which aims to choose k nodes to inject positive information in order to maximize influence reduction of negative information. They devised a ( 1 - 1 / e ) -approximation algorithm and a scalable algorithm which construct a directed acyclic graph (DAG) to estimate the local competitive influence of nodes. With the same methodology, the authors in Nguyen et al. (2013) studied β T I -Node Protectors problem which finds the smallest set seed of good information to decontaminate misinformation with the ratio of decontamination 1 - β , β ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) . In general, Tong et al. (2018b) studied misinformation containment with multiple cascades. This approach was also considered under the game theory (Tong et al. 2018a).
Recently, there is a common campaign to block misinformation by removing the set of important links and nodes in the process of misinformation diffusion. For links removal problem, Khalil et al. (2014) first proposed ( 1 - 1 / e - ϵ ) -approximation under the LT model. By removing the set of nodes, Pham et al. (2018) studied a problem that focused on maximizing misinformation restriction within the budget and time constraints under a variant of LT model. They showed that calculating the objective function is #P-hard and designed a ( 1 - 1 / e ) -approximation and efficient heuristic algorithms. This problem was also studied in a deterministic linear threshold model in the context that all nodes have the same cost (Pham et al. 2017). Recently, the author in Nguyen et al. (2019) proposed a scalable algorithm which guarantees approximation ratio of 1 - 1 / e - ϵ for epidemic blocking problem by edges and nodes removal.
Other approaches used vaccinating, placing monitors on a set of edges or nodes (Zhang et al. 2016a; Zhang and Prakash 2015; Zhang et al. 2016b) to reduce the influence of misinformation. These methods are equivalent to solutions of removing edges or nodes in the network. Zhang et al. (2016a) proposed τ -MP problem which aims to place monitor on minimum-size set of nodes so that the influence of misinformation converges to the center node set at most threshold τ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] . Given a set of infected epidemic nodes in a network, Zhang and Prakash (2015) aimed to find the optimal allocation on nodes to control the out breaking of epidemics under IC model. They devised some heuristic methods without approximation guarantee. The vaccination method has also been applied for edges and nodes groups to control propagation of epidemics under LT model (Zhang et al. 2016b).
Approaching from a dual angle of the above studies, in this work, we aim to find minimum-size nodes set whose removal reduces the influence to less than a threshold γ . The threshold can control the number of nodes influenced by misinformation in a social network. Thus, it is possible to maintain the credibility of the information that is distributed on the OSNs.
In other contexts, Zhu et al. (2019) studied influence blocking which consider the location of competitors. Zheng and Pan (2018) investigated rumor blocking within a given community. Farajtabar et al. (2017) studied the fake news mitigation problem in social networks by combining reinforcement learning with a point process network activity model.

Diffusion models Let G = ( V , E ) be a directed graph representing a social network with a node set V and a directed edge set E , | V | = n and | E | = m . Let N in ( v ) and N out ( v ) be the set of in-neighbors and out-neighbor of node v , respectively.
Linear threshold model In this model, each directed edge ( u , v ) ∈ E is associated with an influence weight w ( u , v ) ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] such that ∑ u ∈ N in ( v ) w ( u , v ) ≤ 1 Given a subset S ⊆ V representing the misinformation sources (as the seed set in IM problem Kempe et al. 2003). In LT model, each node v ∈ V has two possible states, active and inactive and the influence cascades in G as follow. Firstly, every node v ∈ V uniformly chooses a threshold θ v ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] , which represents the weighted fraction of v ’s neighbors that must be active to activate v . Next the influence propagation happens in round t = 0 , 1 , 2 , … as follow:
- At round 0, we activate nodes in S , and set all other nodes inactive.

- At round t ≥ 1 , an inactive node v is activated if weighted number of its activated neighbors are greater than or equal to its threshold, i.e., ∑ in-activated neighbors v w ( u , v ) ≥ θ v

- Once a node becomes activated, it status remains in the process of spreading. The influence propagation ends when no more nodes can be activated.



Independent cascade model In this model, each edge e = ( u , v ) ∈ E has a probability p ( u , v ) ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) that represents the misinformation transmission from u to v . The diffusion process from a misinformation source set S is in the following steps. At step 0, we activate nodes in set S , and set all other nodes inactive. At step t ≥ 1 , the node u active at step t - 1 has a single chance to activate each currently inactive out neighbour node v with successful probability p ( u ,  v ). Once a node becomes activated, it remains in that state in all subsequent steps. The influence propagation stops when no more nodes are activated.

Definition 1 ( TMB problem) Let G = ( V , E ) be a directed graph representing a social network under a information diffusion model M . STMB - IC algorithm In LT model, the function h ( · ) is monotone and submodular function, therefore TMB admits greedy algorithm with approximation ratio of ( 1 - 1 / e ) . Unfortunately, the function h ( · ) is neither submodular nor supermodular under IC model.
Theorem 5 The function h ( · ) is neither submodular nor supermodular under IC model
Proof We prove the theorem by counter example. We consider a graph G = ( V , E ) , with five nodes V = { a , b , c , d , e } and five directed edges E = { ( a , b ) , ( a , c ) , ( b , d ) , ( c , d ) , ( d , e ) } under IC model. For each edge e , we assign probability p ( e ) = 1 , and set misinformation source S = { a } . We have h ( ∅ ) = 0 , h ( { b } ) = 1 , h ( { b , c } ) = 4 , h ( { b , c , d } ) = 4 , and h ( { b , c } ) - h ( { b } ) > h ( { b } ) - h ( ∅ ) . Therefore, h ( · ) is not a submodular function. On the other hand, h ( { b , c , d } ) - h ( { b , c } ) < h ( { b , c } ) - h ( { b } ) . Therefore h ( · ) is not a supermodular function. □
In this model, we propose STMB - IC algorithm for TMB on IC model with with some changes. First, we use merge algorithm for IC model in Zhang and Prakash (2015) to merge set source S = { s 1 , s 2 , … , s k } into a single node I . In this case, the new probabilities p ( I , v ) , ∀ v ∈ N out ( S ) : 42
p ( I , v ) = 1 - ∏ s ∈ N in ( v ) ∩ S ( 1 - p ( s , v ) ) We then remove S from G to obtain new instance G ′ (called merged graph). Base on Remark 6.1 in Zhang and Prakash (2015), the instances of TMB before and after using merge algorithm are also equivalence. Next, we generate η sample graphs from G ′ by live-edge model. For each sample graph g , we build a tree root at I by DFS algorithm. The process of constructing a tree root at I from G under IC model is illustrated in Fig. 2 and summarized in Algorithm 3. After getting set L contains η trees by Algorithm 3, we apply Algorithm 2 to find result for TMB problem under IC model. The procedure is summarized in Algorithm 4.

Experiments To see the efficiency and effectiveness of STMB - LT and STMB - IC algorithms for TMB prblem, in this section we present the results of our algorithms on real-networks.
Experiment setup Algorithms comparison We compare our proposed algorithms with the other algorithms. The following is a list of algorithms we evaluate in our experiments.
- STMB - LT , STMB - IC : Our proposed algorithms. In LT model, we call our algorithm is STMB - LT 500 , STMB - LT 1000 with the number of live-edge graphs are η = 500 and η = 1000 , respectively. If STMB - LT 500 and STMB - LT 1000 have same solution quality result, we commonly call is STMB - LT . In large graphs we don’t run STMB - LT 1000 algorithm. Similar for STMB - IC .

- DAVA (Zhang and Prakash 2015): The current state-of-the-art algorithm for Data-Aware Vaccination problems in Zhang and Prakash (2015), that finds the set k nodes in V - S to distribute vaccine to minimize σ G ( S ) under IC model. Because DAVA is only usable on IC model, we compare it with STMB - IC .

- Greedy : The Greedy algorithm in Sect. 5.1 with the number of Monte-Carlo simulations is 1,000.

- Degree : The heuristic based on the notion of degree centrality. We setup degree of a node equal sum of in-degree and out-degree that node. We sort all nodes base on degree and we then use binary search algorithm to find A until mets condition h ( A ) ≥ γ .

- PageRank —compute a ranking of the nodes in the graph G based on the structure of the incoming links (Page et al. 1998). We implement the power method with a damping factor of 0.85. We setup this algorithm similar to Degree algorithm.

Conclusions In this paper, we studied the TMB problem, which aims at finding smallest set nodes to remove from a social network so that the influence reduction greater than threshold γ . We have some proofs on complexity side of the problem. Specifically, the TMB is NP-hard under IC model and is #P-Hard under LT model. Moreover, we have proposed efficient algorithms, namely STMB - IC and STMB - LT to solve TMB problem under two models IC and LT . Experiments were conducted on real-life network datasets showing the effectiveness and scalability of our algorithms that outperform the state-of-the-art methods.
